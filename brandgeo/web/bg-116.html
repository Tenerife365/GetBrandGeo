London and Dublin, Same Questions: Which Names AI Engines Carry Across the Border

We asked four AI engines the same eight business questions in London and Dublin. Software names travelled: 14 of 17 showed up in both cities. Law firms and advisors did not: 4 shared names across five questions.

A company that sells in both the UK and Ireland usually asks one question about AI search: if the engines recommend us in London, do they recommend us in Dublin too? We had the data to check. On July 10, 2026 we asked four AI engines the same eight business questions for London and for Dublin, and compared the names in the two sets of answers.

The answer splits by what you sell. Software names crossed the border: 14 of the 17 CRM and project management tools named at least twice in one city also appeared in the other. Business banking split along the border, with Revolut the only name in all 8 answers. Law firms, financial advisors and property solicitors almost never crossed: across five questions, 4 firms appeared in both cities, and each of them in only one answer per city.

How this was measured, and what it cannot tell you

Claude, Gemini, Meta AI and Perplexity each answered eight questions for London and the same eight for Dublin, 64 answers in all. Three questions were worded the same apart from the city (commercial contract disputes, wealth management for expats, property purchase solicitors), one apart from the country (business banking apps), and four were close but not identical: the London CRM question asked for UK-based software, the Dublin one for tools used by startups. ChatGPT answered in London but returned errors in Dublin that day, so it is left out of both sides. Meta AI has since been retired from our collection; its July answers stay in.

For software and banking we searched the answer text for a list of brand names, with word boundaries, and read every hit in context. For the five law and advice questions we listed every firm named, by hand, and searched for each one in the other city's answers. With 4 answers per city per question, we report counts, not percentages. We count what the engines wrote. We cannot say why an engine named a firm, and we do not name law or wealth firms here, since most carry their founders' surnames.

The question was the same apart from the country. UK answers named UK challenger banks, and Irish answers named Irish banks and N26. Of the 8 names that came up at least twice, 3 appeared in both countries, and only Revolut was in every answer on both sides. Banking is a regulated product sold country by country, and the answers follow that line.

The commercial dispute question was worded the same for both cities, and the four engines agreed strongly inside each city: one London firm was in all 4 London answers, and five Dublin firms were in all 4 Dublin answers. Not one name crossed. The property question, also worded the same, gave two separate lists with nothing in common.

The four shared names have something in common. The one employment firm is a large UK firm, and the Dublin answer named its Dublin office. The three wealth managers are UK firms, and each Dublin answer introduced them through an Irish office or presence. None was named by a second engine in either city. So the overlap that exists sits with firms that have a presence in both countries, and even there it is one engine on each side.

Two answers named no firm of their own. Gemini, asked about London contract disputes, said it could not recommend a firm, then named four as examples of what a legal directory ranks. Perplexity, asked about London financial advisors, pointed to two adviser directories and a regulator's search tool instead of any firm.

What this means for a business in two countries

For a software or app company, the result is encouraging: if the engines name you in one of these cities, they probably name you in the other, because the answer is about the product, not the place. For a bank, the country decides the list. For a law firm or an advisor, visibility in one city carried almost nothing into the other in this sample, even for firms with offices in both.

What to do with this

If you sell a product across borders, check one market first and spot check the second; the names in these answers mostly travelled. If you sell a service, or anything regulated, treat each country as its own audit. Ask each engine the question a buyer in that city would type, and note whether your local office is named under its local name.

If you have an office in a second country, give it its own page, its own business profile and its own local facts, so an engine answering a Dublin question has a Dublin page to read. This study cannot show that doing so changes what an engine names. It shows how little crossed the border on its own.
