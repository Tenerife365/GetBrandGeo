AI Overviews on 68% or 40% of Local Searches?

Two spring 2025 studies are still quoted on how often Google shows an AI Overview for local searches: 68% in one, 40.2% in the other. Both can be right. They counted different queries, in different places, in different ways. A methods piece on what each measured.

If you read about local SEO and AI, you have probably seen one of two numbers. Google shows an AI Overview on 68% of local business searches, says one. On 40%, says the other. A business owner deciding whether AI Overviews matter for them could reasonably ask which is true.

Both are, for what they measured. The gap comes almost entirely from which searches each study chose to count. It is a good example of why a percentage without its method is not much use.

Sources, and what they cannot tell you

The 68% figure is from Whitespark's study, published 12 May 2025. The 40.2% figure is from Local Falcon's whitepaper, posted 21 May 2025. We read both on 6 November 2026. Neither is our data, and we have not reproduced either. Both predate the changes Google made to AI Overviews and AI Mode in 2026, so neither should be read as today's rate.

Intent decides the answer

The most useful number in either study is not the headline. It is Whitespark's split by intent.

Local intent, such as a business near me15%93%

When someone wanted a business, Google mostly showed the local pack. When someone wanted information, Google mostly showed an AI Overview. The 68% average is therefore mostly a statement about how many informational and hybrid queries were in the sample. Real estate agents were the exception in Whitespark's data, with AI Overviews on up to half of local intent queries.

Local Falcon's breakdown points the same way. Its AI Overview rate was 58.3% for informational queries and 59.9% for what it calls reason queries, but 17.2% for commercial and 10.5% for navigational ones.

Two more things that moved the number

Putting the place in the query. Local Falcon ran half its queries with a location name in them. Without one, 46.1% showed an AI Overview. With one, 35.0%. Naming a place lowered the rate by 11 points. The page reports the difference; it does not test why.

Query length. Local Falcon found that longer queries drew far more AI Overviews: 19.7% for queries under 20 characters, rising to 60.5% for those over 100, though only 76 queries were that long. In that sample, questions written out in full, the way people ask an assistant, drew AI Overviews far more often than short keyword searches.

One possible reading, which the whitepaper does not test: these are services where buyers ask questions before they pick anyone, such as what it costs or what to look for, which would fit the intent pattern above. The page does not split these categories by intent, so it cannot show that directly.

What each figure can support

Whitespark's 68% supports a narrow claim: in three US cities and six industries, with its mix of query types, most searches showed an AI Overview in spring 2025. Its intent split supports a more useful one: local intent searches mostly got a local pack. Local Falcon's 40.2% supports a claim about a broad, machine-written, international sample. Neither supports "AI Overviews now answer most local searches" as a general statement, and neither tells you the rate for your own buyers' questions today.

Small print worth knowing

Neither study is perfect. Whitespark does not state the device, the login state or the collection dates. Local Falcon's page lists seven query types but describes six intent categories, and the sample sizes it gives for queries with and without a location add up to 70,000, not the 60,000 stated elsewhere. Neither problem changes the main pattern, but both are reasons to quote these figures with their sources, not as plain facts.

Where AI Mode fits

Both studies measured AI Overviews, the summary at the top of a normal Google search. AI Mode is a separate conversation surface. In our own data, Google AI Mode answered local buyer questions with a lot of local detail: it gave a street address for a named firm in 77 of 111 answers (BG-109). A study of AI Overview prevalence says nothing about what AI Mode does with the same question.

What to do with this

When someone quotes you an AI Overview percentage for local search, ask four things: which queries, what intent mix, which country and device, and when. A rate across mostly informational queries tells you little about whether buyers looking for a firm like yours see an AI Overview.

Then look at your own queries. Write down the questions your buyers ask, search them, and note what appears: local pack, AI Overview, or both. That is a sample of one business, but it is your business.
