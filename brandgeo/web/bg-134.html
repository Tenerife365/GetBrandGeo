AI Visibility in Sacramento: Engines Agree on Property Managers, Not on Agents

We asked ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini six local buyer questions about Sacramento. Between them they named 116 firms, and 14 were on all three lists: 5 property managers, and not one real estate agent or immigration firm.

Ask three AI engines the same question about Sacramento and you get three lists. We wanted to know how much those lists overlap, because a business that appears on only one of them is missing for buyers who use the other two.

On November 6, 2026 we put six local buyer questions about Sacramento to ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini: a personal injury law firm, real estate agents, a property management company, an immigration lawyer, and two questions written for the state capital, lobbying and government relations firms, and agricultural and water rights law firms. Then we counted the firms each engine named and the ones all three had in common.

How this was measured, and what it cannot tell you

18 answers: six questions, three engines (ChatGPT on gpt-5.5, Claude on claude-sonnet-4-6, Gemini on gemini-2.5-flash), all collected on November 6, 2026. We took every name an engine put in bold, then read each of the three answers to the same question to see whether it named that firm anywhere, in bold or in running text. Ratings, directory names and advice were removed, and a lawyer or agent named without a firm was not counted as a firm. Firms named after a person are reported as a count, not by name, and that covers most of the law firms here.

Claude's stored answers stop at about 3,000 characters and two of its six (personal injury and immigration) were cut mid-list, so its counts there are a lower bound. This is one day of answers. It does not rank anyone and it does not say why an engine chose a firm.

Of the 116 firms, 14 were on all three lists (12%), 25 on two (22%) and 77 on one engine only (66%). Two of the six questions produced no name on all three lists.

Property management: 5 of 17 companies on every list

This is where the three engines agreed most. ChatGPT named 8 property managers, Claude 10 and Gemini 15, and between them only 17 different companies. Five were in all three answers: Utopia Management, Keyrenter Sacramento, Real Property Management Select, Sacramento Delta Property Management and River Valley Property Management. Real Property Management is one of the national brands we counted in BG-113.

Six more were named by two engines: Tower Bridge Property Management, Cornerstone Property Management, Titan Property Management, Peak Residential, M&M Property Services & Management and Ziprent. Every company on ChatGPT's list was on at least one other list. Gemini's list was the longest, and 5 of its 15 companies were its own.

Lobbying: 27 firms, three in common

The question written for the capital produced the longest combined list. Gemini named 19 firms, ChatGPT 10 and Claude 6. All three named Capitol Advocacy, Governmental Advocates and one boutique firm carrying its founders' names. Two more were named by two engines, and 22 by one only, 14 of them by Gemini.

One pair shows how close a near miss can be. ChatGPT named a lobbying firm called Axiom Advisors; Gemini named "Axiom Strategies". We counted them as two names, because that is what a buyer reading the two answers would see.

Water law and personal injury: three each

Agricultural and water rights law: 14 firms, 3 on all three lists and 3 on two. All six carry founders' names, so we count them and do not print them. ChatGPT named 10 firms here, Claude 5, Gemini 8.

Personal injury: 20 firms, 3 on all three lists and 4 on two, all named after people. One of the three was written under two different names: ChatGPT and Gemini used one, Claude used a form in which one of the three names is different. We counted it as one firm, because the two forms share two names; a business owner checking only one form of the name would have found it on two lists, not three. Gemini named 13 firms here and 7 of them were its own.

Real estate agents and immigration: 38 names, none in common

Real estate: ChatGPT named 5 agents, Claude 10 agents or teams, Gemini 12. No agent or team was in all three answers. Five were named by two engines, and four of those five were shared by Claude and Gemini. ChatGPT's list overlapped with the others on one agent only.

Immigration: ChatGPT named 5 firms, Claude 9, Gemini 7, and none was in all three answers. Five were named by two engines. One came close: Claude and Gemini named a firm, and ChatGPT named the lawyer who leads it without naming the firm. Under our rule a person is not a firm, so it counts as two engines, not three.

An exact match on bold text finds 3 firms on all three lists; reading the answers finds 14. The property managers show why. Gemini put a colon inside the bold after every company name, so a simple text match treats each one as a heading. Elsewhere the same firm appeared with and without "LLP", "P.C." or a city name. For a business owner checking their own visibility, search the full answer for your name and its variants, not only the highlighted list.

Gemini named the most, 74, and gave the longest lists on lobbying and property management. ChatGPT wrote the longest answers and named the fewest, 45: much of its space went to questions to ask, ranking sites and what to check before signing. A bold span is not always a firm.

What a Sacramento business should do

Ask each engine your own buyer question, with your trade and "Sacramento", and note which list you are on. Treat the three as separate channels: 102 of the 116 firms named here were missing from at least one engine, and 77 were on one list only.

If you are an agent or an immigration lawyer, search for your own name as well as the firm's. In both trades the engines agreed on nobody, and in immigration one engine named the person where the others named the firm.

Pick one form of your firm's name and use it everywhere: your site, your Google Business Profile, your directory listings. Several of the 14 shared firms were written in more than one form across the answers. Read the answers for what they say about you too, not only whether you are in them.
