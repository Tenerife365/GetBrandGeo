Which Case Types AI Engines Attach to Personal Injury Firms

We read 97 AI answers about personal injury lawyers in 20 US cities. Four engines tied case types like wrongful death and truck crashes to the firms they named. Perplexity listed the same case types, but as a filter for the buyer, almost never next to a firm.

Ask an AI engine for the best personal injury lawyer in your city and most answers do more than list names. They say what each firm handles: car crashes, truck wrecks, wrongful death, medical malpractice. We counted which case types each engine names, and, reading the answers by hand, whether a case type was attached to a firm or offered to the buyer as a way to choose.

The engines named 6.2 case types per answer on average, and 93 of 97 answers named at least three. The split is in how they used them. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Google AI Mode described the firms they named by case type. Perplexity named nearly as many case types in its lists, but as filters for the buyer: compare firms by case type, or ask it to narrow the list to car accidents or wrongful death.

How this was measured, and what it cannot tell you

We asked the same personal injury lawyer question in each of 20 US cities. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Mode answered on July 24 and 25, 2026, and 97 usable answers came back (ChatGPT 19, after one city's July rows were removed in a later refresh; Google AI Mode 18). We searched the answer text for 13 case types, each with a word pattern, and counted the answers that named each one. For wrongful death, medical malpractice and truck crashes we then read every matching passage and sorted each answer: a case type attached to a named firm or lawyer, or only a general mention addressed to the buyer.

Stored Claude answers are cut at about 3,800 characters and ChatGPT and Google AI Mode answers at 10,000, so the counts are lower bounds for those engines. We count what the engines wrote. We did not check whether the firms really handle the cases attached to them, and we cannot say why an engine described a firm the way it did.

Attached to a firm, or handed to the buyer

A case type in an answer can do two jobs. It can describe a firm ("handles car accidents, truck wrecks and wrongful death"), or it can be advice to the buyer ("the best choice depends on whether your case is a car crash or medical malpractice"). We read every passage for three case types and counted the answers where at least one mention was attached to a named firm or lawyer.

Read the table by row. Claude, Gemini and Google AI Mode almost never mentioned these case types except to describe a firm. ChatGPT did both: it described each firm by its best fit, and many of its answers also opened by telling the buyer that the right firm depends on the case type. Perplexity is the other way round. Of its 19 answers that mentioned wrongful death, 18 did so only in a list for the buyer, typically "compare firms on case type: car accident, truck crash, wrongful death" or an offer to narrow the list to the best firms for one of them. Across the three case types, 4 of its 20 answers tied any of them to a firm, each time in a single short line.

Why Perplexity's lists look different

Perplexity's personal injury answers were short: a median of about 1,450 characters, against 3,000 to 4,300 for Claude, Gemini and ChatGPT, and close to the 10,000 character cap for Google AI Mode. A short answer has room for a few firm names and little description of each. Its case types sat in the closing advice instead. That fits what BG-112 found across all trades: Perplexity attached almost no facts of any kind, years in business, certifications or awards, to the firms it named.

So the same question gets two kinds of answer. On four engines a buyer with a truck crash can see, next to a firm's name, that it handles truck crashes. On Perplexity the buyer is told that case type matters and is left to find out which firm handles what.

What this means for a personal injury firm

When an engine described a firm, it described it in the language of case types, and it used the same handful most of the time: car accidents, wrongful death, premises liability, medical malpractice, truck crashes. The narrower types, dog bites, nursing home neglect, rideshare crashes, appeared in a minority of answers. A firm that focuses on one of those cannot assume an engine will mention it.

What to do with this

Ask the five engines who the best personal injury lawyers in your city are, and write down the case types each one attaches to your firm and to the firms named next to you. If an engine lists your firm with case types you do not handle, or leaves out the one you are known for, that is the gap to look at.

On your own site, give each case type you take its own page in plain words, with the results you can state publicly, and use the same names buyers use: truck accident, wrongful death, medical malpractice. This study cannot show that doing so changes which firms an engine names or what it says about them. It shows that four of five engines describe firms by case type, and which case types they reach for.
