What Each AI Engine Tells a Buyer About a Local Firm

Eight studies, one set of 591 answers to local buyer questions in 20 US cities, five engines. Put side by side, each engine has a clear habit: Google AI Mode gives addresses, ChatGPT gives vetting advice, Claude and Gemini give facts about the firm, Perplexity gives the least.

Between 28 October and 6 November we published eight studies on how AI engines answer local buyer questions. Each one looked at a single thing: directories, empty answers, star ratings, addresses, fees, vetting advice, firm facts, national brands. Read one at a time, they each show a gap between engines. Read together, they show something more useful: each engine has a habit, and it is the same habit from study to study.

This page puts the eight side by side. There is no new data in it. Every number below is copied from the study named in the table, so you can follow any cell back to its method.

How this was measured, and what it cannot tell you

All eight studies read the same answers: six local buyer questions in each of 20 US cities (a personal injury lawyer, real estate agents, a property manager, an immigration lawyer and two questions about each city's economy), put to ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Mode on July 24 and 25, 2026. 591 answers came back usable: 120 per engine, 111 for Google AI Mode.

One correction since then: on 6 November an admin refresh deleted six Los Angeles ChatGPT answers from that set, so rerunning the studies' queries today returns 585 answers. The published figures below stand as published. These are counts of what engines wrote in one collection run in July. They do not say why an engine answered as it did, and they do not rank anyone.

"Stated a fact about a firm" means at least one of the five kinds of fact counted in BG-112: years in business, a track record in dollars or counts, board certification, ownership, or a former judge, prosecutor or officer on the team. "Vetting advice" means telling the buyer to check a license or bar record, read reviews, interview several firms or ask set questions, as defined in BG-111.

Each engine's habit

Google AI Mode hands over the details. It gave a street address in 77 of 111 answers, quoted a rating or review count in 57, and linked out in every answer. It almost never told the buyer to check anything: 8 answers. It reads like a listings page with a summary on top.

ChatGPT tells the buyer how to choose. Vetting advice in 83 of 120 answers, and 30 of the 39 answers in the whole set that said to check a license or bar record. It linked out in 119 answers but gave a street address in only 2. In real estate it was the only engine to tell buyers to ask how the buyer's agent is paid, in 15 of 20 answers (BG-110).

Claude and Gemini describe the firms. Claude stated a firm fact in 96 of 120 answers and Gemini in 89, mostly years in business. Neither gave many addresses; Gemini gave none, and it contained no links in any answer. Gemini told the buyer to interview several firms in 21 answers (BG-111).

Perplexity gives the least of each. It was the only engine that sometimes named nobody: 15 of 120 answers had no firm in them, 10 of those about real estate agents (BG-107). It stated a firm fact in 9 answers and gave vetting advice in 9. Its one strong column is ratings: 50 answers quoted a rating or review count, including 14 of its 20 immigration answers, most of those a 5.0 from a lawyer directory (BG-108).

What the scorecard does not say

It does not say which engine is right. A street address can be out of date, a 5.0 rating can come from three reviews, and vetting advice can be generic. It also does not say which engine buyers use most; that is a survey question, not something these answers can show. And it is one run in July: engines change, and we have not yet measured these 20 cities a second time on the same footing.

What to do with this

Check each engine for the thing it tends to repeat. On Google AI Mode, your address, phone and rating. On ChatGPT, the directories and license records it sends buyers to. On Claude and Gemini, your years in business and track record. On Perplexity, whether you are named at all.

Keep the engines apart when you track them. An average across five engines would blur exactly the differences this table shows.
