Which National Brokerage Names AI Engines Put Beside Real Estate Agents

We read 97 AI answers to a home buyer question in 20 US cities. 77 of them named a national brokerage brand next to the agents. One engine almost never did.

Ask an AI engine for the best real estate agents for a home buyer in your city and you get a list of people. Next to most of those people sits the name of the company they work under. We wanted to count how often that company is a national brokerage brand, and which brands. A local agent reading the list learns whether the engines frame the competition as individual agents or as agents carrying a large brand.

77 of 97 answers (79%) named at least one national brokerage brand. 20 named none from our list. Two names dominate: Compass appeared in 38 answers and Keller Williams in 37. In 28 answers, three or more different brands appeared together.

How this was measured, and what it cannot tell you

We asked one real estate agent question for home buyers in each of 20 US cities. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Mode answered it on July 24 and 25, 2026, and 97 usable answers came back. We searched the answer text, with links removed, for ten national brand names: Compass, Keller Williams, RE/MAX, eXp Realty, Coldwell Banker, Sotheby's International Realty, Redfin, Berkshire Hathaway HomeServices, Douglas Elliman and Century 21. We also searched for Howard Hanna and Weichert and found neither.

We read 22 matched passages by hand, spread over all five engines. All 22 were real: the brand was the company the named agent works under, or, for Redfin, a brokerage the engine recommended directly. Our list is short, so other national or regional brands exist and are not counted, which means the real share is higher. Stored Claude answers are cut at about 3,800 characters and Google AI Mode answers at 10,000, so brands late in a long answer are missed. We count what the engines wrote. We did not check whether any agent or brand is good, and we cannot say why an engine named it. No agent is named in this article.

Four engines name a brokerage in nearly every answer, from 16 of 18 to 20 of 20. Perplexity is the outlier at 4 of 20, and the gap is too large to blame on a small sample. BG-107 covers answers where an engine named no firm at all, and that is part of the reason Perplexity's count is low here. We did not recount it for this article.

The engines also differ in who they put beside the agents. ChatGPT named Redfin in 10 of 19 answers, as a brokerage to consider directly. Claude named it in none. Gemini and Google AI Mode leaned on the franchise networks, with Keller Williams in 11 of 20 and 10 of 18 answers. Gemini also gave the longest brand lists: 11 of its 20 answers named three or more different brokerages.

Unlike property management, where 3 cities had no national brand at all, no city here escaped. In every one of the 20 cities, at least 3 of the answers carried a national brokerage name. For a local agent, the question is not whether a national brand will appear in the answer, but which ones.

What else shares the answer

36 of the 97 answers also named a property portal such as Zillow or Realtor.com. That overlaps with BG-106, which counted the directories and review sites engines name, so we only note it here. A buyer reading these answers often meets an agent, a brokerage badge and a portal in the same list.

What to do with this

If you are an independent agent or a small brokerage, expect to be listed beside agents who carry a national badge in about four answers out of five. Run your own city on the five engines and write down which brands sit next to you, and whether your own firm name appears in the same answer.

Then check what an engine can read about you. Your firm name, years in the market, the neighborhoods you work and how many homes you sold are the facts these answers quote beside a name. This study cannot say that publishing them changes the list. It shows how often the national names are there.
