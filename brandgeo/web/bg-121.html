AI Visibility in Portland: Engines Agree on IP Lawyers and Breweries

We asked ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini six local buyer questions about Portland, Oregon. 14 firms were named by all three engines, half of them intellectual property law firms. No real estate agent and no immigration firm made all three lists.

Ask three AI engines the same question about Portland and you get three lists. We wanted to know how much those lists overlap, because a business that appears on only one of them is invisible to buyers who use the other two.

On November 6, 2026 we put six local buyer questions about Portland, Oregon to ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini: a personal injury law firm, real estate agents, a property management company, an immigration lawyer, an intellectual property and trademark law firm, and a craft brewery. Then we counted the firms that all three engines named.

How this was measured, and what it cannot tell you

18 answers: six questions, three engines (ChatGPT on gpt-5.5, Claude on claude-sonnet-4-6, Gemini on gemini-2.5-flash), all collected on November 6, 2026. We took every name an engine put in bold, then read each of the three answers to the same question to see whether it named that firm anywhere, in bold or in running text. Headings, beer names and award labels were removed. Firms named after a person are reported as a count, not by name.

An exact match on bold text alone finds only 5 firms on all three lists, because engines shorten names, add suffixes such as "IP Group", or mention a firm in a sentence without bold. The hand read finds 14. This is one day of answers. It does not rank anyone and it does not say why an engine chose a firm.

Intellectual property law: the strongest overlap

Seven IP and trademark firms appeared in all three answers. All seven carry the names of founding partners, so we report them as a count. What stands out is how they were named: Gemini put most of them in bold, but mentioned one only in a closing sentence listing "other reputable firms". A buyer reading quickly would see it; a tool that counts bold names would not.

Craft breweries: four on every list, ten on only one

All three engines named Breakside Brewery, Great Notion Brewing, Ruse Brewing and Wayfinder Beer. Seven more were named by two engines and ten by one only, seven of those by Gemini alone. A brewery outside the shared four was on, at most, two of the three lists.

Personal injury and property management

Two personal injury firms made all three lists: Bridge City Law and one firm named after its founder. In property management, one company did: Sleep Sound Property Management. Six more property managers were named by two engines each, so the lists were close in this category without meeting. The same pattern held one level down in personal injury, where two more firms were named by two engines each, and in IP law, where one more firm was named by ChatGPT and Claude but not by Gemini.

Why counting bold names is not enough

Most AI visibility tools, ours included, start by reading the names an engine puts in bold. On these 18 answers that alone finds 5 firms on all three lists; reading the answers finds 14. Seven of the nine missing firms are in intellectual property law. Gemini wrote three of those firms with an "IP Group" suffix after the name, and it named one only in its closing sentence, without bold. ChatGPT wrote "Breakside" in one place and "Breakside Brewery" in another, and "Wayfinder" next to "Wayfinder Beer". None of these firms is missing from the answers; they are missing from a count that trusts the formatting.

For a business owner checking their own visibility, the lesson is simple: search the full answer for your name and its variants, not only the highlighted list.

ChatGPT bolded the most, 56 spans in the IP law answer alone, but many of its bold spans were descriptive labels such as "IP litigation" or "IP boutique work", not firms. Claude's stored answers stop at about 3,800 characters, so its length is a lower bound. Gemini bolded the least overall, yet it named the most breweries, 17.

What does not show up

No real estate agent and no immigration firm was named by all three engines. The words "Portland immigration" appeared in all three immigration answers, but no single firm did: the names did not repeat from one engine to the next. BG-016 looks at when engines agree in general.

What a Portland business should do

Ask each engine your own buyer question, by name of your trade and "Portland, Oregon", and note which list you are on. Treat the three as separate channels: even among breweries, 17 of the 21 named were missing from at least one engine.

Check how each engine writes your name. If one engine adds a suffix, shortens your name or mentions you only in passing, make your full name appear the same way on your site, your directory profiles and your Google Business Profile.
