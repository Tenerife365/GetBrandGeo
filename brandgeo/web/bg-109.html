How Often AI Engines Give an Address or a Phone Number

We read 591 AI answers to local buyer questions across 20 US cities and counted every street address and phone number. Google AI Mode printed an address in 77 of its 111 answers. Gemini printed none. The other three engines rarely did.

A buyer who asks an AI engine for an immigration lawyer or a property manager gets names. Sometimes they also get what they need to act on a name: a street address, a phone number, a link. We wanted to know how often each engine hands over those details, and for which kinds of firms.

Across 591 answers in 20 US cities, 103 gave a street address for at least one firm they named, and 77 of those 103 came from one engine, Google AI Mode. Gemini gave no address at all. ChatGPT gave two. Phone numbers were rarer still in the answer text, and the ones ChatGPT gave were public help lines, not firms.

How this was measured, and what it cannot tell you

We asked six local buyer questions in each of 20 US cities: a personal injury lawyer, real estate agents for a home buyer, a property management company, an immigration lawyer, and two questions specific to that city's economy. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Mode answered each one on July 24 and 25, 2026, and 591 answers came back usable.

We searched the answer text for a US street address (a house number followed by a street name and a word such as Street, Avenue, Boulevard, Road or Suite) and for a US phone number, and read about a hundred matches by hand. One Gemini match was a newspaper name, not an address, and was removed. Google AI Mode answers often include the page text of the sources they cite, so some of its addresses and many of its phone numbers sit inside that quoted text rather than in the engine's own sentences. Our stored copy of a Claude answer stops at about 2,500 characters and of a Google AI Mode answer at 10,000. We did not check whether any address or number was correct, and we print none of them here. We report what the engines wrote, not why they wrote it, and one collection run in July is not a statement about other days.

Google AI Mode often set out each firm with its own line for the address, sometimes as a map link, and it rarely stopped at one. Its 77 answers with an address held 313 distinct address strings between them, about four per answer. The other four engines together held 35.

The gap is not about whether an engine searched the web. ChatGPT searched and linked a source in 119 of its 120 answers, yet gave an address in only two. Claude, which answered from its own knowledge with no live search, gave an address in 14 answers and a phone number in 9, usually for a named firm and often on a line of its own under the firm's name. Nobody checked those numbers against the firms, and a phone number recalled from training data can be out of date.

The phone numbers ChatGPT gave were not firms

ChatGPT gave a phone number in four answers. All four were public lines: a county lawyer referral service listed by a state court guide, a court resource's referral number, a bar association pro bono intake line, and a nonprofit immigration help line listed by a state legal aid office. In those answers ChatGPT pointed the buyer to a service that hands out names, not to a firm.

Google AI Mode showed a number in 37 answers, but much of that came with the source text it quoted: a firm's office page, an agent's profile, a listing title, and in a few cases a fax line. Gemini and Perplexity gave no phone number in any of their 240 answers.

Immigration answers carried an address or phone number most often, on every engine that gave any. Claude gave contact details in 8 of its 20 immigration answers and Perplexity an address in 5. On Google AI Mode, property management came closest to every answer: 18 of 19 gave an address.

Real estate agents came last. An agent is often presented by brokerage and by profile page rather than by office, and 12 of 98 answers gave an address or a phone number.

Websites: the link is usually a directory

Links split the engines in two. ChatGPT and Google AI Mode linked in nearly every answer; Gemini and Perplexity linked in none of our stored text. Where Claude, Gemini or Perplexity did write out a web address, it was usually a directory or a listing site rather than the firm's own site. So a buyer reading those three engines often gets a firm's name and the name of a directory, and has to search again to reach the firm.

What to do with this

Check the address and phone number Google shows for your firm, because on Google AI Mode those details are part of how a firm is presented, often in the answer itself. If you have moved office, the old address may be the one a buyer reads first.

Do not count on the other engines to carry your contact details. On ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity the buyer mostly gets your name, and has to find you from there. That makes your name and your directory listings the parts that have to be easy to find. This study cannot promise that a correct address gets you recommended; it shows where the address appears when you are.
