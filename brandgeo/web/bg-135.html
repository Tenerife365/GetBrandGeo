Who Owns Your AI Visibility Tool Now?

Since late 2025 a content platform bought Scrunch, HubSpot agreed to buy XFunnel, and Profound raised $180 million from two large venture firms. What each deal changed, from the companies' own announcements, and what a customer should ask a tool whose owner has changed.

AI visibility tools are a young market, and it has started to consolidate. Since late 2025 two of the tools that track how brands appear in AI answers have been taken, or agreed to be taken, into larger platforms, and a third raised enough money to change its investor base. If you pay for one of these tools, or are about to, it is worth knowing who now owns it and what they have said they will do with it.

This is a summary of what the companies themselves announced. Where a figure comes from someone else, we say so.

Sources, and a disclosure

We read Sitecore's release on Scrunch, HubSpot's page on XFunnel and Profound's release on its Series D on 6 November 2026. Customer counts and results in those releases are the companies' own claims. BrandGEO competes with all three tools, so read this with that in mind.

Scrunch, now inside Sitecore

Sitecore sells a digital experience platform, the software companies use to run their websites and content. Its release says Scrunch will work with SitecoreAI and that Scrunch's recommendations will be automated within its content management, content marketing and digital asset management products. Scrunch, which Sitecore says is trusted by more than 500 brands and agencies, now describes itself as a Sitecore company.

The release does not say whether Scrunch stays available to companies that do not use Sitecore, or whether its pricing changes. Sitecore did not disclose the price. CMSWire reported that Bloomberg valued the deal at $225 million.

XFunnel, going into HubSpot

HubSpot's page says it has entered into an agreement to acquire XFunnel, an answer engine optimization platform, and that XFunnel will be integrated natively into HubSpot's marketing products, so marketers can see how their brand appears in AI tools and how to improve it. The page gives no terms and no closing date. The direction is clear, though: the stated plan is a feature inside HubSpot, not a separate product.

Profound, still independent with new money

Profound was not bought. It raised a $180 million Series D at a $1.8 billion valuation, co-led by Sequoia Capital and Kleiner Perkins, with its existing investors also taking part. The release says the money goes into an applied AI lab in New York and San Francisco that studies how frontier models perform on marketing work. Profound claims more than 1,000 enterprise brands as customers. A funding round does not change who owns the product the way an acquisition does.

If you already use HubSpot or Sitecore

For a company already on one of the parent platforms, these deals may be good news: AI visibility data next to the CRM or the content system it already runs, without another login. The questions for those customers are different: whether the AI visibility features come with the plan they already pay for or as an add-on, and whether the depth of the original tool survives the move into a bigger product.

Why this matters to a customer

Two of the three tools are being folded into a larger platform's own products. That can be good for customers of the parent: the data shows up where they already work. For everyone else, it raises practical questions the announcements do not answer.

Question to askWhy it matters
Will the tool stay available on its own?A product built into a platform can stop being sold separately
Will my plan or price change at renewal?A tool folded into a platform can be repriced as part of it
Can I export my history?Months of tracked answers are the part you cannot recreate
Is my data shared with the parent company?Your prompts and competitors are commercially sensitive
Does the roadmap now assume the parent's other products?New features may only work inside that platform

The export question deserves the most weight. What an AI visibility tool builds up over time is a record: which questions were asked, what each engine said, on which date. AI answers change from run to run, so that history is the only way to see a trend. If you cannot take it with you, you are tied to the tool whatever its new owner decides.

What we are not saying

None of these announcements says a product will be shut down, repriced or restricted, and we are not predicting that any will be. Being acquired can be how a small tool survives and grows. The point is narrower: when the owner changes, the answers to the questions above can change too, and the time to ask is before you renew, not after.

What to do with this

If you use Scrunch or XFunnel, ask your account contact the five questions above and get the answers in writing. If you use any AI visibility tool, export your tracked history now and on a schedule, whoever owns it.

And keep the record that matters in your own hands: the questions your buyers ask, and what each engine answered, by date.
