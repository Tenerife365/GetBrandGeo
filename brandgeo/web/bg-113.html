How Often AI Engines Put a National Brand Next to Local Property Managers

We read 99 AI answers to property management questions in 20 US cities. 63 of them named at least one national brand next to the local firms. One franchise name accounted for most of it.

Ask an AI engine for the best property management company in your city and you get a short list. We wanted to know how many of those lists put a national brand next to the local firms. A landlord comparing the list sees one set of names. A local manager reading it learns whether the competition includes a franchise with a marketing budget.

63 of 99 answers (64%) named at least one national brand. 36 named none from our list. The count is dominated by one name: Real Property Management, a franchise whose offices carry a city after the brand, appeared in 42 answers. The next two brands appeared in 17 answers each.

How this was measured, and what it cannot tell you

We asked one property management question in each of 20 US cities, and ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Mode answered it on July 24 and 25, 2026. 99 usable answers came back (one Google AI Mode answer was not usable). We searched the answer text, with links removed, for seven national names: Real Property Management, Mynd, Evernest, FirstService, Greystar, Associa and Renters Warehouse. We also looked for Invitation Homes and Progress Residential and found neither.

We read 45 matched passages by hand. Two hits were false and are removed: a truncated name that only looked like Associa, and an answer that mentioned Belong only as a site that publishes roundups. Belong is a rental management company that also publishes lists, so it is left out of every count here. The list is ours and short. Other national or regional operators exist and are not counted, so the real share is higher. Stored Claude answers are cut at under 3,800 characters and Google AI Mode answers at 10,000, so brands late in a long answer are missed. We count what the engines wrote. We did not check whether a brand has an office in the city, and we cannot say why an engine named it.

Most answers that named a national brand named only one: 42 named one brand, 17 named two and 4 named three. In the 20 passages we read for the three biggest names, each one recommended the brand as a manager, not as a passing word. The wording was often a city office, such as the franchise name followed by a city or a metro area.

Two of the smaller names came with a different job. Where ChatGPT named FirstService or Greystar, it described them as large operators for condo towers, associations and apartment buildings. A landlord with a single rental house was not necessarily being pointed to them.

The spread between engines is five answers, from 10 of 20 to 15 of 20. With about 20 answers per engine that is a small gap, and we would not build a plan on it. The brand mix is clearer. Perplexity leaned on the franchise name (12 answers) and rarely named Mynd (1). Gemini spread its picks, with Mynd in 7 answers, more than any other engine. Claude named the franchise in 4 answers, fewer than the others.

The 36 answers with none of the seven

36 answers named none of our seven brands: ChatGPT 10, Claude 8, Gemini 7, Google AI Mode 6 and Perplexity 5. These are not necessarily all local lists. Our brand list is short, so an answer in this group may still name a regional or national operator we did not search for. What we can say is that in these 36 answers, none of the seven names we counted came up, and that a local firm competing for the same question did not face them.

One detail on the franchise name matters for local managers. Real Property Management is a national network, but the answers usually wrote it with a city or a metro area, and often listed it as one entry among several in a numbered or bulleted list. To a reader it looks like another local company. Counting the brand, as we did, is the only way to see how common that is.

The brands are not spread evenly. In 11 of the 20 cities, at least four of the five answers named a national brand from our list. In 3 cities none did, so no engine put one of our seven names in the answer. In one more city only one answer did. For a local manager, the practical question is which of these two kinds of city you work in.

What to do with this

If you manage property locally, expect a national or franchise name to appear next to yours in about two answers out of three. Search your own city on the five engines and see which names sit beside you. The brand does not need to be large in your city to be listed.

Then give the engines something specific that a franchise page does not carry: who owns the company, how long you have managed rentals in the city, how many homes you manage, and what you charge. BG-112 found that tenure, ownership and units under management were the facts engines most often wrote next to property managers. This study cannot say that publishing them keeps a national name off the list. It shows how often the name is there.
