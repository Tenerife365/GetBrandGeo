BrandGEO Launches Radar, a €29 Entry Tier, and Moves Its Free Plan Off ChatGPT

BrandGEO Editorial Team August 1, 2026 3 min read

SANTA CRUZ DE TENERIFE, SPAIN. BrandGEO has added a new entry tier to its AI visibility platform. BrandGEO Radar is available from today at €29 a month for the first 100 customers, and €39 a month at list price after that. It carries Gemini and Claude, seven commercial buyer prompts, a weekly refresh, one website, and a one-page AI readiness audit of the landing page.

Radar exists because the company's own ladder had a gap in it. Until today the step from the free plan to the first paid plan was €0 to €99, which asked a small business to make a hundred-euro decision before it had ever seen its brand tracked over time. Radar is the rung in between.

What the weekly refresh is actually for

The free plan runs monthly. One collection a month produces one data point, and one data point is not a trend, so a free account can tell you whether an engine mentions you and cannot tell you whether that is getting better or worse. Radar runs weekly across two engines and seven prompts, which is roughly 61 checks a month against the free plan's five, and it is the cheapest plan that can draw a line rather than a dot.

Radar also unlocks the AI readiness audit that was previously sold from €299 upward, scoped to a single page. The queue seeds the customer's homepage rather than reading whatever URL happens to sit first in their sitemap, because a one-page allowance is only useful if the page is the one that matters.

The free plan moves to Gemini, and why

On the same day, BrandGEO's free plan moves from ChatGPT to Gemini. The company is publishing the reason rather than describing it as repackaging, because it was a live defect.

Free accounts run five prompts a month against a €0.30 monthly collection budget. Measured against production rather than modelled, 152 real ChatGPT collections over 30 days averaged €0.0615 each, in a range of €0.0600 to €0.1234. Five of those average €0.307. The free tier was therefore budgeted about half a cent short of its own promise: a free signup was stopped on its last prompt at typical rates, and around its third when rows ran expensive, and it met a budget error where it had been promised a result.

The fix was to move the engine rather than raise the budget. Across 146 real Gemini collections the average was €0.0339, in a tight band of €0.0320 to €0.0340, so five prompts cost €0.170 and leave headroom for variance. The budget stays at €0.30. Raising it as well would have doubled free-tier spend to buy nothing.

The change has a second effect the company considers the more important one. Radar runs Gemini and Claude. With the free plan on ChatGPT, a customer paying €29 would have lost an engine on the way up. Free is now a strict subset of Radar, so no upgrade takes anything away.

The honest cost of that decision is stated here rather than left to be discovered: ChatGPT is now first sold at Essentials, at €99 a month. A free account that wants ChatGPT specifically is looking at the €99 plan, not the €29 one.

What Radar does not include

Radar carries no Google surface, and that is a supply constraint rather than a packaging choice. Google AI Mode and AI Overviews are collected through a search API with a 500-credit monthly pool shared across the entire platform. One hundred Radar customers on that engine would draw about 700 credits a month, roughly 140 percent of the whole pool, consumed by the cheapest tier on the ladder. Google surfaces stay on Growth and above, where the pool can support them.

No allowance was reduced anywhere on the ladder to make room for Radar. Essentials rises from 15 prompts to 18, Growth PRO from 35 to 56, and Managed from 120 to 200. The company verified against production before shipping that no existing customer is put over a cap by any row of the new table.

"The cheapest tier is where a company is most tempted to promise something it cannot pay for, and we had done exactly that on the plan that costs nothing," said a BrandGEO spokesperson. "The free plan was short by less than a cent a month, which is precisely why nobody caught it by reading the code. We would rather publish the measurement than quietly move an engine and call it an improvement."

Radar is available now at getbrandgeo.com/#pricing. The launch price applies to the first 100 subscribers and is not time-limited.
