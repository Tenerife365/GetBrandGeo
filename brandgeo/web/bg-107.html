When an AI Engine Names No One

We asked five AI engines for the best local professionals in 20 US cities and read every answer that looked empty. Four engines named at least one firm every time. Perplexity named none in 15 of 120 answers, and in half the cities it named no real estate agent at all.

Most of what we publish counts which firms AI engines recommend. This one counts the answers where an engine recommended nobody. You ask for the best real estate agent in Seattle and the reply is a checklist: interview three agents, read reviews, ask about local experience. Useful advice, and not a single name in it.

Across 591 answers in 20 US cities, four engines named at least one firm every time. Perplexity named none in 15 of its 120 answers. Ten of those fifteen were about real estate agents.

How this was measured, and what it cannot tell you

We asked six local buyer questions in each of 20 US cities: a personal injury lawyer, real estate agents for a home buyer, a property management company, an immigration lawyer, and two questions specific to that city's economy. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Mode answered each one on July 24 and 25, 2026, and 591 answers came back usable.

Our automatic firm extractor came back empty on 162 of those answers, but it misses bullet lists, so we did not trust it. We read all 162 by hand. An answer counts as "named no firm" only if it gives no firm, brokerage or individual professional at all. Pointing to a ranking list or a directory without naming anyone in it still counts as no firm. We report what the engines wrote. We cannot tell you why they wrote it, and one collection run in July is not a statement about how these engines behave on other days.

The other four engines sometimes hedged. One Gemini answer opened by calling a single best list impossible and subjective, then named five firms anyway. That counts as naming a firm. Only Perplexity left answers with nobody in them.

Perplexity's answers were also short. They averaged 1,341 characters, against 3,111 for Claude and 8,823 for Google AI Mode. Its fifteen no-name answers averaged 1,149.

For a home buyer looking for an agent, Perplexity named no agent and no brokerage in Chicago, Philadelphia, Atlanta, Washington DC, Boston, Seattle, San Diego, Denver, Minneapolis and Baltimore. In the other ten cities it named agents or teams. For property managers it always named companies. The fifteen empty answers fall in 12 of the 20 cities, so this is not one city's quirk.

What an empty answer looks like

Twelve Perplexity answers said in plain words that its search results were missing or too thin to recommend anyone, along the lines of not having search results to verify which agents are currently best. Nine of those twelve then named no one. The other three named one or two firms while warning that the results were thin.

The remaining six empty answers declined without saying so. One pointed to a published ranking of law firms without naming a firm from it. One pointed to a city magazine's list of top agents. One listed agent directories. The others gave criteria and questions to ask in an interview.

The near misses

Three more Perplexity answers came close. Asked for law firms that handle medical device work in Minneapolis, automotive and manufacturing work in Detroit, and home services in Phoenix, it said the results were too limited and then named one or two firms. In the Detroit answer it added that the one firm it found was a poor match for the question. We counted all three as answers that named a firm, so the 15 is the strict number. A looser reading, counting any answer that warned it could not really recommend anyone, would put Perplexity at 18 of 120.

The contrast with property management is worth noting. Perplexity named companies in all 20 of those answers, and its property management answers were its longest, averaging 1,947 characters. In the same run, the same engine had no trouble naming companies for that question.

None of these answers is wrong, exactly. The advice is sensible. But for a local firm the result is the same as not being mentioned, and for a buyer it means a second search somewhere else.

Why this matters for how you measure

If you average visibility across engines, an empty answer looks like a firm that lost. Here it is an engine that did not pick anyone. In all ten cities where Perplexity named no agent, every other engine that answered named agents or brokerages for the same question. That is 38 answers, because Google AI Mode returned no usable answer in two of those cities. A firm checking only Perplexity would conclude it is invisible in its market. A firm checking only ChatGPT would never see the gap.

What to do with this

Read each engine's answer to your buyers' question separately, and record "named no one" as its own result, not as a miss. If you are a real estate agent or brokerage, check Perplexity in particular, because that is where we saw empty answers most often.

An empty answer is not a verdict on your firm. It tells you that, on that day, the engine did not find enough it trusted to name anyone. This study cannot say what would change that. It can say that four other engines answered the same question with names, so the question itself is not unanswerable.
