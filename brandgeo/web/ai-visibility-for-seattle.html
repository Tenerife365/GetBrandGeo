AI Visibility in Seattle: One Brand Sweeps the Coast

We ran 6 real Seattle categories (personal injury law, real estate, property management, immigration law, technology/software law, and coffee roasters) through the same AI Visibility pipeline BrandGEO runs for paying clients, across five AI engines. One important note up front: Google AI Mode's collection failed on 5 of the 6 prompts this run, a genuine technical error we're disclosing directly, not evidence of zero visibility.

Despite that gap, Ziprent and Dubin Law Group both reached full presence among the four engines that did respond. Ziprent's strength here matches what we found in San Francisco, suggesting real West Coast-wide AI visibility rather than a single-city fluke.

A West Coast pattern, not a Seattle fluke

Ziprent reached full presence among responding engines in Seattle's property-management category, exactly as it did in San Francisco's. Seeing the same brand sweep the same category in two different West Coast cities is a genuinely stronger signal than either result on its own.

A real collection gap, disclosed directly

Google AI Mode's collection failed on 5 of the 6 Seattle prompts this run, a technical error, `status: error`, not evidence of zero AI visibility. The one prompt it did answer, coffee roasters, produced a genuine 5-engine result. Every consensus figure in the first five categories above reflects the 4 engines that actually returned usable data, the same disclosure approach used for New York's ChatGPT collection failure in the original city-research series.

A data-quality note, not a finding

Outside of Google AI Mode's collection error, the other 4 engines returned usable data on every prompt this run. This run used Google AI Mode in place of the now-retired Meta AI engine used in earlier city pages.

Why did one engine return no data for most of the Seattle categories?

Google AI Mode's collection failed on 5 of the 6 Seattle prompts, a genuine technical error, not evidence it has zero visibility for Seattle brands. Which brand had the strongest AI consensus in Seattle?

Ziprent, for property management, and Dubin Law Group, for personal injury law, both reached full presence among the four engines that returned usable data. Ziprent also showed the same strength in the San Francisco dataset, suggesting real West Coast-wide AI visibility, not a single-city result.

Does Seattle's AI consensus reflect its real economy?

Yes. Perkins Coie and Davis Wright Tremaine, both genuine Seattle-headquartered firms, were the clearest signals in the technology and software law category, ahead of national names like Wilson Sonsini and Cooley that also appeared.
