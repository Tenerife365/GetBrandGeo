What Fees AI Engines Quote When They Recommend a Firm

We read 591 AI answers to local buyer questions across 20 US cities for fees. Engines explain how firms charge far more often than they put a number on it. Property management is the one profession where a percentage shows up regularly.

Price is one of the first things a buyer wants to know, and one of the last things a firm puts on its website. So when a buyer asks an AI engine for a personal injury lawyer or a property manager, what does the answer say about money? We read the same 591 answers from our 20 city study for fees.

The engines explain how firms charge far more often than they say how much. 64 of 98 personal injury answers mentioned contingency fees, and 3 gave a percentage. Across all 591 answers, 25 quoted a fee percentage, and 20 of those were about property management, the one profession where a number shows up regularly. Perplexity quoted none.

How this was measured, and what it cannot tell you

We asked six local buyer questions in each of 20 US cities: a personal injury lawyer, real estate agents for a home buyer, a property management company, an immigration lawyer, and two questions specific to that city's economy. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Mode answered each one on July 24 and 25, 2026, and 591 answers came back usable.

We searched the answer text for fee words (contingency, commission, management fee, leasing fee, flat fee, free consultation) and for percentages near them, then read every percentage match by hand and dropped the ones that were not fees, such as a rent collection rate or a share of happy clients. We did not check whether any fee was correct, and we name no firm. Google AI Mode answers include page text from the sources they cite, and our stored copy of a Claude answer stops at about 2,500 characters. We report what the engines wrote, not why, and one collection run in July is not a statement about other days.

Two thirds of the answers told the buyer that an injury lawyer is paid out of the settlement, and Gemini mentioned a free consultation in all 20. The rate itself came up three times. ChatGPT gave a general range of about a third to 40%. Claude, twice, set one firm's discounted rate against a standard of about a third. What the answers did quote, often and in dollars, was what firms had won for clients: settlement and verdict totals in the millions and billions.

The general ranges were close to each other: 8 to 10% of monthly rent in several ChatGPT answers, 8 to 12% in ChatGPT and Claude, 7 to 12% and 4 to 10% in Gemini, 6 to 12% or a flat monthly amount in Google AI Mode. Single company rates ran from 5% to 12.9%. ChatGPT also built a fee checklist into almost every answer: in 19 of 20 it warned about a leasing or placement fee, often put at half to a full month's rent, and in several it asked about a markup on repairs.

So a landlord asking for a property manager often gets a price frame before any company name, and sometimes a named company's rate beside a typical range. If your rate is in that answer, the buyer compares it there.

Real estate: a question to ask, not a rate

Real estate answers mentioned commission in 24 of 98, and only 2 gave a percentage. What stood out was ChatGPT: in 15 of its 20 answers it told the buyer to ask how the buyer's agent is paid, or what compensation agreement the agent uses, and several of those answers said the rules on buyer agent pay had changed nationally. No other engine raised the question. Claude used the word commission to say that agents work on commission, so time in the business tells you something.

Immigration: flat fee or hourly

No immigration answer quoted a percentage, which fits how that work is billed. 23 of 98 answers raised flat fee or hourly billing, 15 of them from ChatGPT, nearly always as a question for the buyer to put to the lawyer. 13 mentioned a free consultation and 4 a paid one; Google AI Mode gave a consultation price in dollars in 2. The city-specific questions brought no fee percentage at all.

What to do with this

If you manage property, assume the buyer reads a fee range in the AI answer before reading your name, and make your own fee easy to find and easy to compare. If you are a lawyer or an agent, the answers mostly hand the buyer questions to ask about money. Answer those questions on your site in plain words: how you are paid, whether the first meeting is free, what a flat fee covers.

This study cannot say that publishing a price gets you recommended. It shows what the buyer is told about money when you are, and which engine says it.
