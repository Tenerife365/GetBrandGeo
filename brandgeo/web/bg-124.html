AI Visibility in Las Vegas: Engines Agree on Gaming Lawyers, Not Event Planners

We asked ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini six local buyer questions about Las Vegas. 12 firms were named by all three engines, 5 of them gaming law firms. Of 30 event and convention companies the engines named, only one was on all three lists.

Ask three AI engines the same question about Las Vegas and you get three lists. We wanted to know how much those lists overlap, because a business that appears on only one of them is invisible to buyers who use the other two.

On November 6, 2026 we put six local buyer questions about Las Vegas to ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini: a personal injury law firm, real estate agents, a property management company, an immigration lawyer, a gaming and casino law firm, and an event and convention planning company. Then we counted the firms that all three engines named.

How this was measured, and what it cannot tell you

18 answers: six questions, three engines (ChatGPT on gpt-5.5, Claude on claude-sonnet-4-6, Gemini on gemini-2.5-flash), all collected on November 6, 2026. We took every name an engine put in bold, then read each of the three answers to the same question to see whether it named that firm anywhere, in bold or in running text. Rankings such as "Band 1", numbering and advice labels were removed. Firms named after a person are reported as a count, not by name.

An exact match on bold text alone finds only 3 firms on all three lists. The hand read finds 12. This is one day of answers. It does not rank anyone and it does not say why an engine chose a firm.

Each engine named a short list, and five firms appeared in all three. All five carry the names of founding partners, so we report them as a count. One of them shows how fragile the match can be: Claude named it under another firm's name, with the old name in brackets after "formerly", and Gemini mentioned it only in a sentence about one of its lawyers. A reader would recognize it; a count of bold names would not. Nine more firms were named by one engine only.

This is one of the two questions written for this city, and the one where the engines agreed least. Between them they named 30 companies. Only A to Z Events appeared in all three answers. Eight more were named by two engines and 21 by one engine only. Gemini alone named 17, and 10 of those appeared in no other answer.

Property management, real estate and injury law

All three engines named Five Star Property Management and one more property manager. In real estate, one agent and one brokerage made all three lists; in personal injury, two firms did, both named after their founders. Each of those questions also produced several firms named by two engines but not the third.

Even in gaming law, where the engines agreed most, six of every ten names appeared in a single answer. In event planning that rose to seven in ten. A business on one list and absent from the other two is the normal case in both categories, and the practical difference is only how often the third list also carries you.

Immigration: no overlap at all

No immigration firm was named by all three engines, whether we counted exact bold matches or read the answers by hand. That does not mean the engines gave empty answers. It means that for this question, in this city, on this day, no firm was common to all three lists, so what a buyer saw depended on the engine they used.

Why counting bold names is not enough

Most AI visibility tools, ours included, start by reading the names an engine puts in bold. Here that finds 3 firms on all three lists; reading the answers finds 12. Engines shorten names, add a city, put a firm inside a ranking line, or mention it in a sentence without bold. For a business owner checking their own visibility, search the full answer for your name and its variants, not only the highlighted list.

What a Las Vegas business should do

Ask each engine your own buyer question, by name of your trade and "Las Vegas", and note which list you are on. Treat the three as separate channels: in event planning, 29 of 30 companies were missing from at least one engine.

If your firm changed its name or merged, check that every engine has caught up, and that your old and new names point to the same place on your site and your directory profiles.
