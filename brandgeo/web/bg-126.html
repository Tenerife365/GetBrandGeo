45% Ask AI for a Local Business. Almost All Double Check

BrightLocal's 2026 consumer survey found that asking AI for a local business went from 6% to 45% of US adults in a year, and that 97% of those users sometimes check the answer against real reviews. Next to our own data, it shows where the check lands: on the ratings and profiles the engines quote.

In one year, asking an AI tool for a local business went from something few people did to something close to half of US adults had tried. That is the headline of BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026, published in March. The part that gets less attention is what those people did next: almost all of them went and checked.

The survey is not new, but it frames what we have been measuring this month from the other side. Our studies count what AI engines say to a buyer. This survey asks the buyers what they do with it.

Sources, and what they cannot tell you

BrightLocal surveyed 1,002 US adults through SurveyMonkey; some figures are based on the 455 who had used an AI tool for a local business recommendation in the past 12 months. The report does not state when the fieldwork ran. We read it on 6 November 2026. A survey records what people say they do, which is not always what they do. Our own figures come from 591 AI answers to local buyer questions in 20 US cities, collected on July 24 and 25, 2026, as published in BG-108, BG-109 and BG-111.

The age split matters for anyone selling to a particular group. A firm whose buyers are mostly in their thirties and forties is dealing with a majority that has already asked an AI tool. One whose buyers are mostly retired is not, yet.

Trust, and checking

The survey shows a group that uses the answers without taking them on faith.

So trust and checking go together. Most users say they trust the recommendations, and nearly all of them still look at the reviews at least some of the time. Among people who had not used AI for this, 53% said they do not trust it.

The same pattern shows up with AI review summaries. 23% of users rely only on the summary, 59% go on to the review profiles for more, and 18% skip summaries altogether.

Where the check lands

Our data shows what the buyer is checking against. In BG-108 we counted every star rating and review count in our 591 answers. 186 answers quoted one. Of the 267 distinct ratings quoted, 86% were 4.5 or higher and only 5 were below 4.0. When an engine quotes a rating, it is almost always a high one.

When 42% of users always go to the review platform to check, the rating the engine quoted and the rating on the platform had better match. A firm described as "4.9 stars" in an answer and showing 4.3 on its profile invites exactly the doubt the buyer went to check for.

Engines also tell buyers to check, though very unevenly. In BG-111, ChatGPT gave vetting advice in 83 of its 120 answers: check a license, read reviews, interview several firms. Google AI Mode did so in 8 of 111. A buyer who starts in ChatGPT is told to verify; one who starts in AI Mode is mostly handed details and left to decide.

The two tools people named most

The survey's two most used tools, ChatGPT at 31% and Google's AI Mode at 23%, hand the buyer very different starting points, going by our own counts.

In our 591 local answersChatGPT (120)Google AI Mode (111)
Quoted a star rating or review count (BG-108)2857
Gave a street address (BG-109)277
Told the buyer how to vet the firms (BG-111)838

An AI Mode user more often gets the rating and the address in the answer itself, so the check is a comparison: does the profile say the same thing? A ChatGPT user more often gets a list of names and instructions, so the check is a search: where are this firm's reviews? Either way the buyer ends up on a review platform or a directory profile.

What this does not show

The survey does not link a specific AI answer to a specific check, and our data does not follow any buyer after the answer. Put together, they describe a likely path, not a measured one: a buyer asks, gets a short list with ratings, and goes to the reviews before calling anyone.

What to do with this

Treat the review platforms as part of the AI answer. Check that your rating and review count on Google and on the directories your category is sent to (BG-106 lists them) are current, and that what engines quote about you matches.

Reply to reviews, keep your profile details current, and do not chase a rating number on one platform while another tells a different story. The buyer who checks will see both.
