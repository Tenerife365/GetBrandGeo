AI Mode Can Book a Table. Is Your Business Bookable?

Google says AI Mode can find open slots for restaurant reservations, event tickets and beauty and wellness appointments across booking partners, then hand the user a short list. What that means for a restaurant, salon or clinic, and why Google's own pages disagree on who can use it.

Most of what AI engines do for a local business is talk about it: name it, describe it, quote its rating. Google's AI Mode has started to do something more direct for a few kinds of business. Ask it for a table for four on Saturday, two concert tickets, or a massage next week, and it can go looking for an actual open slot.

For a restaurant, a salon or a clinic, that changes the question from "does the AI mention us?" to "can the AI book us?"

Sources, and what they cannot tell you

We read Google's announcement of 17 November 2025 and its current Search help page for AI Mode on 6 November 2026. The two disagree on who can use the feature, and we did not find a 2026 announcement from Google that settles it. We have not tested the booking flow ourselves, and Google publishes no figures on how often it is used.

What Google says AI Mode does

You describe what you want in plain words, with your needs and preferences. AI Mode checks real-time availability across booking platforms and returns a short list of options with direct links. You finish the booking on the partner's site. Google does not complete it inside Search, according to its post.

What can be bookedPartners Google named
Restaurant reservationsOpenTable, Resy, Tock

The same post describes flight and hotel booking as coming later, not available at the time.

Who can use it: two answers from Google

Google pageWhat it says about access
Blog post, 17 November 2025Restaurant booking rolling out in the US with no Labs opt-in; event tickets and local appointments for all US Labs users
Search help page, currentAn "Agentic capabilities in AI Mode" experiment in Search Labs for US Google AI Pro and AI Ultra subscribers, 18 or older, signed in with a personal account

Either way, it is a US feature for now, and at least part of it sits behind a Labs opt-in or a paid subscription. The help page adds that Labs experiments are not available to Google Workspace accounts, and that the user must be signed in with Incognito off. That limits how many people see it today. It does not change what a business needs to have in place for when they do.

What a business needs for AI Mode to book it

Be on a partner it checks. Google describes AI Mode as checking availability across booking platforms, and names the partners. A salon that takes bookings only by phone, or through a system Google does not check, has no slot for AI Mode to find. If you already use one of the named partners for your category, you are in the pool. If you do not, that is the decision to look at.

Keep availability real. The feature is built on real-time availability. A booking calendar that is out of date, or that shows slots you do not honor, now feeds an assistant that hands them to customers. Block holidays and staff absences where the platform can see them.

Keep the details consistent. AI Mode already gives a lot of local detail in its answers. In our study of 591 answers to local buyer questions in 20 US cities, Google AI Mode gave a street address for a named firm in 77 of 111 answers (BG-109) and quoted a rating or review count in 57 (BG-108). Your name, address, hours and services on the booking platform, on Google and on your own site should match, so the business it lists and the one it books are clearly the same.

Make the partner profile worth landing on. The booking finishes on the partner's site, so that profile is the page a new customer actually sees before they commit: photos, services, prices, reviews. Treat it as part of your storefront, not an afterthought. Reply to reviews there as you would on Google, because a customer comparing two salons in an AI Mode list may well read them.

Who this does not reach yet

The categories are narrow: restaurants, events, and beauty and wellness. None of the partners Google named is for lawyers, real estate agents, property managers or most other professional services, which is where most of our own research has looked. For those firms AI Mode still recommends and describes; it does not book. That is a reason to watch this, not to ignore it. The step from describing a business to acting for the customer has been taken, in a few categories, by Google.

It is also different from the product side of agent shopping, which we covered in BG-083. A product page needs machine-readable prices and identifiers. A local service needs a bookable slot on a platform the agent checks.

What to do with this

If you run a restaurant, salon, spa or similar business in the US: check whether you are on one of the booking partners Google named for your category, and whether your availability there is accurate this week. Then check that your name, address and hours match across that platform, Google and your site.

If you are in another category, ask AI Mode the question your customers ask and read what it says about you today. The booking step may reach your category later; the description already has.
