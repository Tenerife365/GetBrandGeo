2. What these technologies are

2.1 Cookies

A cookie is a small text file placed on your device by a website. Cookies are described in two ways:

  • First-party cookies are set by the website you are visiting. Third-party cookies are set by another organisation, such as an analytics provider, whose code runs on the page.
  • Session cookies are erased when you close your browser. Persistent cookies remain on your device until they expire or you delete them.

2.2 Local storage and similar technologies

Browser local storage holds data on your device in the same way a cookie does, but it is not transmitted to a server with every request. The law treats local storage the same as cookies, so the same consent rules apply. We use local storage for your preferences, for your consent choice, and to keep you signed in to the dashboard.

We do not use device fingerprinting, tracking pixels for advertising, cross-site tracking, or data brokers.

3. The four categories, and the legal basis for each

  1. Strictly necessary / essential. Required to deliver a service you have explicitly requested, such as keeping you signed in or remembering your consent choice. Exempt from the consent requirement under Article 5(3) ePrivacy; our legitimate interest under Article 6(1)(f) GDPR where personal data is involved. These cannot be switched off.
  2. Functional / preference. Remember choices you make, such as light or dark theme and language. Requires consent where not strictly necessary. On our sites these are set only by your own explicit action in the interface.
  3. Performance and analytics. Help us understand which pages are useful. Requires prior opt-in consent, Article 6(1)(a) GDPR. Nothing in this category loads until you accept.
  4. Marketing and targeting. Build advertising profiles or track you across sites. Requires prior opt-in consent. We do not use any technology in this category. Google advertising signals are held at "denied" at all times, including for visitors who accept analytics.

7. Managing cookies in your browser

You can also block or delete cookies and site data directly in your browser. Blocking strictly necessary items may stop the dashboard from working.

  • Google Chrome: Settings → Privacy and security → Third-party cookies, and Settings → Privacy and security → Delete browsing data.
  • Mozilla Firefox: Settings → Privacy & Security → Cookies and Site Data, then Manage Data or Clear Data.
  • Apple Safari: Settings (or Preferences) → Privacy → Manage Website Data. On iOS: Settings → Apps → Safari → Advanced → Website Data.
  • Microsoft Edge: Settings → Cookies and site permissions → Manage and delete cookies and site data.

Most browsers also offer a private or incognito window, which discards all cookies and local storage when the window is closed. You can opt out of Google Analytics across all websites using the Google Analytics opt-out browser add-on.
