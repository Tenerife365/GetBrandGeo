From Tracking to Acting: Agents in AI Visibility Tools

At least three AI visibility vendors now sell an agent, not just a dashboard: Peec AI, Scrunch under Sitecore, and Profound. We read each one's own pages and set out what each says its agent does, what it can change without asking, and what to ask before you let one near your site.

For most of the short history of AI visibility tools, the product was a dashboard. It ran questions through ChatGPT, Gemini and the rest, recorded whether your brand came up, and showed you a chart. Over 2026 at least three vendors have started selling something else: an agent that takes the findings and does work with them.

We read what Peec AI, Scrunch and Profound say about their agents on their own pages. This is not a test of any of them. It is a side by side of their claims, so you know what to ask.

Sources, and what they cannot tell you

Everything below is a vendor claim, read on 6 November 2026 from Peec AI's launch post (21 October 2026) and its documentation, Sitecore's announcement of its Scrunch acquisition (3 June 2026), and Profound's funding announcement (15 September 2026). We used none of these products for this article. BrandGEO sells a competing product; read this with that in mind.

Peec AI: the most detail on paper

Peec announced its agent in a post dated 21 October 2026, and its documentation is the most specific of the three. The agent sits in the sidebar of the app. It comes with built-in skills, which Peec describes as ready-made analyses, and lets you write your own. It connects to more than 25 outside tools; the launch post names Linear, HubSpot, Customer.io, PostHog, Clay and lemlist, and the documentation adds Notion, Lovable and Webflow.

The recommendations it works from fall into three categories, in Peec's words: technical fixes, on-page improvements and off-page improvements.

The approval settings are the part worth reading closely. Read-only analysis needs no approval. A change, such as creating a prompt or a topic, needs an approval click. An Auto toggle approves actions for the rest of a chat, and a setting can make Auto the default. Each connected tool can be set to always allow, ask, or never, and read-only tools start on always allow. Scheduled routines can run read-only, ask before changes, or change without asking.

The agent runs on credits: 500 a month on Starter, 750 on Pro or Essentials, 1,000 on Advanced or Growth, 1,500 on Scale and 3,000 on the top tiers, shared across the company. Unused credits do not roll over.

Scrunch: an agent inside a content platform

Sitecore bought Scrunch in June 2026. Its announcement describes Scrunch's Agent Experience Platform and says Scrunch's recommendations will be automated within SitecoreAI's content management, content marketing and digital asset management products, with Scrunch connecting directly to Sitecore workflows. In practice that puts the agent next to the content itself, for companies already on Sitecore. The announcement says nothing about approval settings or pricing for the automation.

Profound: an agent called a marketer

Profound described its AI Marketer in its September 2026 funding announcement as an agent that scans a brand's data, analyzes AI answers and surfaces opportunities, then deploys sub-agents to act on them. The tasks listed are finding earned media opportunities, refreshing stale content, running competitive analysis and analyzing paid ad performance. The same announcement describes an Ads Studio for building and managing AI search campaigns across OpenAI, Google and Meta's ad managers. Approval and pricing for the agent are not covered there.

What to ask before you let one act

QuestionWhy
What can it change without a click from me?Defaults can be switched; know what "auto" covers
Which of my accounts does it log into?A connector to your site or CRM is write access
Where is the log of what it did?You need to undo a change you did not make
What happens when credits run out mid-task?A half-finished change can be worse than none
What evidence does each suggestion rest on?An agent acting on one odd AI answer can chase noise

The last question matters most for AI visibility. AI answers vary from run to run and engine to engine. In our own 591 local answers, the same question got very different kinds of answer from each of five engines. An agent that rewrites a page because one engine said one thing once is acting on thin ground.

Where BrandGEO stands

BrandGEO does not have an agent that changes your website, content or ads. It runs the questions your buyers ask, stores what each engine answered, and suggests what to fix. A person decides and makes the change. That is a choice about where we are today, not a claim that the other approach is wrong.

What to do with this

If you are trialling one of these agents, start it in read-only mode and keep approvals on. Read the evidence behind each suggestion before approving it, and check what the AI answers actually say about you before and after any change.

Whatever tool you use, the ground truth is the answer itself. Read it.
