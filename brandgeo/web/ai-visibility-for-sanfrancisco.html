AI Visibility in San Francisco: One Firm Is Unanimous Everywhere

We ran 6 real San Francisco categories (personal injury law, real estate, property management, immigration law, venture capital/startup law, and startup accounting) through the same AI Visibility pipeline BrandGEO runs for paying clients, across five AI engines. The headline: Kruze Consulting was named the #1 startup accounting firm by all 5 engines with zero exceptions, the first full unanimous result found outside a healthcare category anywhere in this research program.

Venture capital and startup law showed similarly concentrated strength, with Cooley and Fenwick & West both reaching 4 of 5 engines. Immigration law and individual real estate agents, by contrast, stayed as fragmented as anywhere else measured.

The first non-healthcare full unanimous result in this program

Kruze Consulting was named the #1 startup accounting and tax firm by every single engine tested, ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Google AI Mode, and Perplexity, with no competing #1 pick from any of them. Every prior full 5/5 unanimous-#1 result in this research program (Houston Methodist, Mass General Brigham) was a hospital system. This is the first time it's happened in a purely commercial B2B services category.

San Francisco's startup economy shows up three times over

Beyond Kruze Consulting's unanimous sweep, venture capital and startup law produced two firms at 4/5: Cooley (near-unanimous #1 across ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini) and Fenwick & West. That's three separate categories, all tied to San Francisco's real identity as the center of US startup and venture-capital work, all landing in the strongest tier of consensus measured anywhere in this program. No other city researched has shown this much concentrated economy-reflects-AI-consensus alignment in a single dataset.

A data-quality note, not a finding

All 5 engines returned usable data on every prompt this run, no collection errors. One directory site was misidentified as a law firm and removed as noise: "Avvo" (venture capital law prompt). This run also used Google AI Mode in place of the now-retired Meta AI engine used in earlier city pages.

What this means if your business is in San Francisco

  1. 1

    If you serve startups, the incumbent bar is the highest measured in this program

    Kruze Consulting, Cooley, and Fenwick & West all show near-total or total AI agreement. A competing firm absent from those answers faces the steepest documented gap found anywhere in this research.

  2. 2

    Property management and personal injury law are winnable, not locked down

    Both categories show clear leaders at 3/5, not full unanimity. There's real room to enter the consensus set without unseating an entrenched incumbent.

  3. 3

    Immigration law and individual real estate agents are the easiest doors in

    Neither category has a name crossing 2/5, and ChatGPT didn't even name a specific immigration attorney. That's the best-documented opportunity in San Francisco for a specific practitioner to become the AI-recommended name.

  4. 4

    Re-run this regularly, not once

    These answers reflect what the engines say today, this week. Engine behavior and extraction quality both shift between runs. A one-time check tells you less than an ongoing one.

● Common Questions

Which brand had the strongest AI consensus in San Francisco?

Kruze Consulting, for startup accounting and tax. All 5 engines tested named it the #1 firm with no exceptions, the first full unanimous 5-of-5 result found outside a healthcare category in this entire research program.

Does San Francisco's AI consensus reflect its real economy?

More than any other city measured. Three related categories, startup accounting (Kruze Consulting, 5/5) and two views into venture capital and startup law (Cooley and Fenwick & West, both 4/5), all produced top-tier consensus, directly reflecting San Francisco's identity as the center of US startup and venture capital legal work.

What was the most fragmented category in San Francisco?

Immigration law. No attorney or firm name repeated across any two of the five engines tested, and ChatGPT didn't name a specific practitioner at all, recommending a bar association referral service instead.
