AI Visibility in Minneapolis: Five Firms Converge at Once

We ran 6 real Minneapolis categories (personal injury law, real estate, property management, immigration law, medical device/healthcare law, and corporate law) through the same AI Visibility pipeline BrandGEO runs for paying clients, across five AI engines. One note up front: Google AI Mode's collection failed on 4 of the 6 prompts this run, a genuine technical error, disclosed directly rather than treated as "no opinion."

Despite that gap, the data that did come through was rich. Kleinman Property Management reached full 5-of-5 unanimous presence, and corporate law produced the densest multi-firm consensus measured anywhere in this research program, five separate firms all converging at once, directly reflecting Minneapolis's real concentration of Fortune 500 headquarters.

The densest multi-firm consensus measured in this program

Five separate corporate law firms, Taft, Dorsey & Whitney, Fredrikson & Byron, Ballard Spahr, and Maslon, all independently reached 3 of 5 engines or higher for "best corporate law firms in Minneapolis." No other category in any city researched has produced this many firms converging simultaneously. This directly reflects Minneapolis's real economic identity: Target, UnitedHealth Group, Best Buy, 3M, U.S. Bancorp, and General Mills are all headquartered in the metro area.

A real collection gap, disclosed directly

Google AI Mode's collection failed on 4 of the 6 Minneapolis prompts this run, a technical error, `status: error`, not evidence of zero AI visibility. Only the property management and corporate law prompts returned usable Google AI Mode data. Every consensus figure in the other four categories reflects the engines that actually responded, the same disclosure approach used for a similar Google AI Mode failure in Seattle's dataset.

A data-quality note, not a finding

Outside of Google AI Mode's collection error, the other 4 engines returned usable data on every prompt this run. Two directory/rating platforms were misidentified as law firms and removed as noise: "Justia" and "Avvo" (immigration prompt). This run used Google AI Mode in place of the now-retired Meta AI engine used in earlier city pages.

What this means if your business is in Minneapolis

  1. 1

    If you're in corporate law, the field is crowded but genuinely winnable

    Five firms all sit at the same consensus tier, no single runaway leader dominates the way Chicago's Kirkland & Ellis did. There's real room to enter that set.

  2. 2

    If you're in property management or personal injury law, the incumbent bar is real

    Kleinman Property Management and Schwebel, Goetz & Sieben both show full agreement among responding engines. A competitor absent from those answers faces a documented gap.

  3. 3

    Individual real estate agents and immigration law are the easiest doors in

    Neither category has a name crossing 2 of the responding engines. That's the best-documented opportunity in Minneapolis for a specific agent or attorney to become the AI-recommended name.

  4. 4

    Re-run this regularly, not once

    These answers reflect what the engines say today, this week, including the collection gaps. Engine behavior and reliability both shift between runs. A one-time check tells you less than an ongoing one.
