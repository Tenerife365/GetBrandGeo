ChatGPT Says Its Search Is a Fifth of Google's

OpenAI says ChatGPT now handles roughly one search-like query for every five on Google. The number comes with no method and no period. Here is what it does and does not tell a local business, next to what ChatGPT actually says when a buyer asks it for a local firm.

On 2 November 2026, at a company dinner in New York, the product lead for ChatGPT's consumer app said that ChatGPT's volume of search-like queries is now "roughly one-fifth the volume of queries of Google." The tech newsletter Big Technology reported it the next day, together with a second figure from OpenAI: weekly users rose from 1 billion to 1.2 billion in about 60 days.

If you run a local business, the natural question is whether a fifth of your search traffic has quietly moved somewhere you cannot see. The number cannot answer that, and it helps to be clear about why before deciding what to do.

How this was put together, and what it cannot tell you

The ChatGPT figures come from Big Technology's report of 3 November 2026, read on 6 November. They are OpenAI's own numbers, given in conversation and hedged with "roughly". The survey figures come from BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026, published 10 March 2026, 1,002 US adults. The ChatGPT behaviour figures are our own, from 120 ChatGPT answers to local buyer questions in 20 US cities collected on July 24 and 25, 2026, as published in BG-106 to BG-111.

What the one-fifth claim leaves out

Three things are missing, and each one matters.

What counts as search-like. In the part of the report that is free to read, the description is broad: what a user would traditionally have taken to a search engine, including lookups for people, places or things that need up-to-date information, and finance and health questions. That still leaves the counting rule open. A conversation can contain one question or ten follow-ups. The reporter asked exactly that, whether each prompt in a conversation counts as a query, and the answer sits behind the paywall.

The period and the baseline. No time window was given for ChatGPT's volume, and no source was named for Google's. The comparison sets one company's own count against a figure for another company's traffic that it did not say where it got.

Any outside check. No method was published and no third party has measured it. That does not make it wrong. It makes it a company statement, and it should be read as one.

The user figure is easier to place. A rise from 1 billion to 1.2 billion weekly users in about 60 days is a rise in people, not in searches. It says how many people open ChatGPT in a week, not what they ask it.

What we know about local buyers

A survey asks a different question: not how many queries, but how many people. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found:

FindingShare of US adults surveyed
Used an AI tool for a local business recommendation in the past 12 months45% (6% in 2025)
Used ChatGPT for one31%
Used Google's AI Mode for one23%
Used Google reviews, as BrightLocal reports it71% (83% in 2025)

So close to one US adult in three told BrightLocal they had asked ChatGPT for a local business in the past year. That is a better guide for a dentist or a law firm than a share of total queries, most of which have nothing to do with local services.

What ChatGPT says when a buyer asks

Our own data shows what happens after the question is asked. We put six local buyer questions to five engines in 20 US cities: a personal injury lawyer, real estate agents, a property manager, an immigration lawyer and two questions about each city's economy. Here is ChatGPT's side of it, out of 120 answers.

The pattern is consistent. ChatGPT names firms, points to directories, links out and tells the buyer to check before hiring. It rarely hands over contact details: in our data it gave a street address in 2 answers out of 120, and the four phone numbers it gave belonged to court, referral or legal aid lines, not to firms. A buyer who gets a ChatGPT answer still has to go somewhere else to make contact, and that somewhere is usually your website or a directory profile.

So what does the one-fifth number change?

Less than the headline suggests, and more than nothing. Even if the estimate is right, it describes all search-like use, not local service searches, and it says nothing about whether those conversations end in a call, a visit or a booking. What it does confirm is that ChatGPT is now a place people go before they choose, and the survey says local buyers are among them.

For a local firm, that shifts one practical question to the front: when ChatGPT answers the question your buyers ask, are you in the answer, and is what it says about you right? That can be checked directly, which is more than can be said for the one-fifth figure.

What to do with this

Ask ChatGPT the question your buyers ask, in your city, the way they would phrase it. Note whether you are named, which competitors are named instead, and which directories it points to. Then open those directory profiles and your own site and check that the facts it is likely to repeat are current.

Do it on more than one engine. In our data the same question got very different kinds of answer from each engine, and Google's AI Mode was used by 23% of the consumers BrightLocal surveyed.
