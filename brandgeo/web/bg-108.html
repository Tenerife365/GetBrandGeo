How Often AI Engines Quote Star Ratings

We read 591 AI answers to local buyer questions across 20 US cities and counted every star rating and review count. One answer in three quoted one. For property managers it was two in three, and the ratings quoted were almost all 4.5 or higher.

When an AI engine recommends a local firm, it usually says why: years in practice, a specialty, an award. Sometimes the reason is a number. A 4.9 rating from 600 reviews. A perfect 5.0 on a lawyer directory. We wanted to know how often that happens, on which engines, and for which kinds of firms.

Across 591 answers in 20 US cities, 186 quoted a star rating or a review count for a firm they named. For property managers the share was two in three. For questions about a city's own economy it was about one in eleven. And the ratings that did appear were almost all high.

How this was measured, and what it cannot tell you

We asked six local buyer questions in each of 20 US cities: a personal injury lawyer, real estate agents for a home buyer, a property management company, an immigration lawyer, and two questions specific to that city's economy. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Mode answered each one on July 24 and 25, 2026, and 591 answers came back usable.

We searched the answer text for a star rating (such as 4.8 stars, 4.9/5, 5 out of 5, or a 4.7 Google rating) and for a review count (such as 600 reviews or 1,200+ Google reviews), and read the matches by hand to check the patterns. One Google AI Mode answer was removed because its only match was a hotel class filter in a link title. Our stored copy of a Claude answer stops at about 2,500 characters and of a Google AI Mode answer at 10,000, so a rating quoted late in a long answer can be missed. We did not check whether any quoted rating was correct. We report what the engines wrote, not why they wrote it, and one collection run in July is not a statement about other days.

Google AI Mode often set the rating out as its own line under each firm, a score followed by a review count. Perplexity quoted a rating more often than a review count, and many of its ratings were a flat 5.0 next to an individual lawyer's listing. Claude, which answered from its own knowledge rather than a live search, quoted numbers least.

Property management stands out on every engine. Google AI Mode quoted a rating or review count in 17 of its 19 property management answers, Perplexity in 17 of 20 and ChatGPT in 14 of 20. Even Claude did so in 7 of 20. A landlord choosing a manager gets a score far more often than a buyer choosing a lawyer.

Immigration lawyers show the biggest gap between engines. Perplexity quoted a rating or review count in 15 of its 20 immigration answers, usually a 5.0 from a lawyer directory listing. ChatGPT did so in 1 of 20. The same question, asked on the same days, came back with a score on one engine and almost never on the other.

The city-specific questions, about the industries a city is known for, rarely came with ratings at all: 18 of 198 answers. Those answers named firms by what they do, not by what their customers said.

We pulled every distinct rating out of the 145 answers that quoted a numeric score: 267 in all, with a median of 4.8. Only five were below 4.0, and engines did quote them, such as a 3.5 out of 5 from more than 2,500 reviews.

That does not prove engines leave out lower-rated firms. A firm with a 4.1 may be named without its score. What it does show is that when an engine reaches for a number to back up a recommendation, that number is almost always 4.5 or above.

Where the numbers come from

Google was the platform engines tied a rating or review count to most often. Lawyer directories came next for legal questions: twelve Perplexity answers tied a rating to Avvo. Real estate answers leaned on Zillow, and property management answers on Google and Yelp. This overlaps with the platforms we counted in BG-106, but here the platform is not just named, it is quoted as a score.

What to do with this

Look up the rating and review count an engine would find for you on Google and, if you are a lawyer, on the main lawyer directories; if you are an agent, on Zillow. If you manage property, treat this as a priority: two in three answers in your category quoted a number.

This study cannot promise that more reviews get you recommended. It does show that a score is one of the facts engines repeat to buyers when they explain a choice, and that the scores they repeat are high. If yours is out of date or thin, that is the number a buyer may read first.
