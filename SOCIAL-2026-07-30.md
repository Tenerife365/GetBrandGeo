## LinkedIn Article (long-form)

**Title:** The Four Questions I'd Ask Any AI Visibility Vendor (Including Us)

Two weeks ago we retired an engine from our own product. Meta AI had been in
BrandGEO's line-up since the start. It came out on 16 July, and our own
advertised engine count went down the same day we removed it.

The reason was retrieval. The Llama models we could reach through that path
answered from training data only, with no web search. Ask that kind of model
which businesses do commercial catering in a city and it hands back a
confident, well-formatted list of what was true at some point before its
training cutoff. A business that launched this year is invisible to it. A
competitor that shut down two years ago might still sit at number three.
Nothing in the output says "this answer is from memory." It reads exactly
like an engine that went and looked.

That is the test we now apply to every engine before it gets a slot in
BrandGEO: query it with retrieval switched on, through a documented path, for
every run that produces a customer's report. Not "the model supports web
search." Web search was actually on, in the pipeline, for the report you got.

DeepSeek fails that test today. Every DeepSeek model we can reach through
OpenRouter is retrieval-free, which is the identical shape that got Meta AI
removed. Adding it anyway would have raised our number for no reason except
that it's a bigger number. Copilot is out for a plainer reason: Microsoft
ships no public API for it, so anyone claiming to monitor Copilot is either
using an undocumented path or measuring something else and calling it
Copilot.

On 29 July we added two engines that passed. Grok, on Growth PRO and above,
runs with its web plugin enabled. It also reads something the other six
don't: Grok is xAI's model and has access to X, so a brand with a strong
website and a thin social presence can look materially different to Grok
than to ChatGPT. And Google AI Overviews, measured separately from Google AI
Mode, which we've run since 16 July. That separation matters more than it
sounds. AI Mode is a tab a searcher has to switch to on purpose. AI Overviews
is the summary block sitting above the ordinary links, and nobody opts into
it. Reporting one of those as "Google" and calling it done hides the surface
most people actually see.

None of this is a standard only we could apply. Any vendor in this category
could adopt it. Most don't publish enough for you to check, so here are the
four questions worth putting to whoever currently sells you AI visibility.

One. For each engine you list, was web search enabled when you queried it?
Not whether the model supports search. Was it switched on, in the pipeline,
for the run that produced your report.

Two. When you say you monitor Google, which Google? AI Mode, AI Overviews,
or one of them reported as though it covers both. This is the question most
likely to produce a pause.

Three. Which engines on your list have never returned a result for your
account? An engine can sit on a pricing page for months without collecting
anything for you. Coverage claimed and coverage delivered are different
columns.

Four. What is your standard for removing an engine? Everyone has a story
about the engine they added. Ask for the one about the engine they took
away. If there isn't one, the list only grows, and a list that only grows is
closer to a marketing artifact than a measurement.

I'll say the uncomfortable part plainly. This argument stops favoring
BrandGEO the moment a competitor runs eight engines with retrieval switched
on for all eight. That's fine. It's the correct standard even when it costs
us the comparison, and we've now applied it against our own interest twice:
once by retiring an engine we had already shipped, and once by declining a
cheap one we could have added this month.

Engine count is the easiest number in this category to move and the least
informative one to compare on. Ask what was actually switched on.

If you want to see it applied to your own brand, the free audit on
getbrandgeo.com runs your queries through every engine your plan includes,
and shows you which surface each answer came from.

Full research, BG-021, "We Shipped Two AI Engines Today. The More Useful
Story Is the Two We Turned Down": https://getbrandgeo.com/bg-021-retrieval-not-engine-count.html
