   - **A documented rebalance never landed.** `planConfig.ts:400-420`'s table
     says allowances were restored to `5, 20, 50, 75, 250` on 2026-07-29 and
     that "no allowance was reduced". The constant twenty lines below still
     reads `5, 15, 35, 35, 120`. Verified directly. The comment describes a
     state that does not exist, and the cost model has been reasoning about
     numbers customers never received. Adopting the modelled ladder also closes
     the open "three conflicting Growth prompt counts" item: the code is the
     outlier, not the docs.
   - **A second inversion, not previously spotted:** `pro` is 120 prompts over
     20 sites, 6.0 per site, below Essentials' 7.5.
   - **Per-site monotonicity is arithmetically impossible at current prices.**
     Every budget is 15% of price and R1's allowances track price almost
     exactly, so per-site budget is flat at about EUR 22.4 across growth,
     growth_pro and managed. Three levers exist: cut allowances, raise the 15%
     ceiling, or drop per-site monotonicity as a requirement. All three are
     Constantin's call. The recommended ladder needs `pro` to drop 20 sites to
     10, because 600 prompts costs about EUR 408 against a EUR 225 budget.
2. **Pooling: SUM or MAX.** The architect pushes back on MAX and recommends SUM
   for the pools with MAX for the site allowance. Under MAX, a second Essentials
   site costs EUR 99/month and adds zero prompts and zero budget: it competes
   with the first site for the same pool, so the customer pays and their
   existing site gets worse. Under SUM the pool is still shared, just funded by
   every site, and margin holds at exactly the 15% the ladder was designed
   around. **This also gates the upsell copy** — at today's shipped numbers the
   Growth to Growth PRO boundary adds zero prompts, so there is nothing true to
   promise.
2. **Enterprise's website count.** free 1, essentials 2, growth 2, growth_pro 3,
   managed 10, pro 20, enterprise undecided.

## 6. Traps this session paid for. Do not re-learn these.

1. **A check that reads the working tree can pass while production is broken.**
   The logo fix existed on disk, uncommitted, for an unknown period. The check
   was green the whole time. Always confirm a fix is in `HEAD`.
2. **Enforcement lives in Postgres as often as in JavaScript.** I told Constantin
   `PLAN_PROMPTS` was enforced nowhere, having grepped only the TypeScript.
   `trg_enforce_prompt_cap` has been enforcing it in the database all along.
   **Grep the database too.**
3. **Source-regex test assertions are defeated by commenting a line out.** Two
   rounds of mutation testing used deletions only and both missed it. The worst
   case left `expire-plan-grants` reverting every client with a past grant date,
   with the harness fully green.
4. **The same fix in one of two places is the commonest bug here.** The AI
   Overviews exclusion existed in one page, so two screens reported different
   numbers for the same client at the same instant.
5. **A vendor's own assistant will confidently describe UI that does not exist.**
   Verify against an endpoint, not against what a product says about itself.
6. **BrandGEO and TalentWeLove crossed over three times in one day** — pasted
   requirements, the Stripe account, and a Netlify build log. Candidates, ATS,
   recruiting or Next.js means you are in the wrong repo.

## 7. D1 is designed. Nothing is in flight.

`docs/arch/multi-site-tenancy.md` is complete, 2,053 lines, revised in place
with all four pricing rulings. Read it before touching anything multi-site.

Three things in it that a builder must not undo:

1. **`public.user_clients` already exists in production**, with a
   `has_client_access()` membership function and 15 live RLS policies calling
   it, holding zero rows. D1 finishes an abandoned migration rather than
   starting one.
2. **Access and grouping are deliberately separate mechanisms.** `user_clients`
   decides who can reach a site; a new `clients.account_id` decides which sites
   pool their limits; and `account_id` must NEVER appear in an authorization
   predicate. Collapsing them would mean adding a site to an account silently
   grants every member access to it. Acceptance criterion 11 is the test: a
   member of site B gets 403 for site C even when C shares B's account.
3. **The architect reversed its own earlier position and said so**, rather than
   quietly. It had refused an organisation entity; a shared limit needs a shared
   owner, because a membership-derived account gives a different remaining
   budget depending on which user asks. The limit is not a property of the sites
   being limited.

Also worth knowing: the per-site budget check must fail CLOSED. If the account
lookup fails, collection is blocked. A budget check that cannot determine the
budget must not conclude there is none.
