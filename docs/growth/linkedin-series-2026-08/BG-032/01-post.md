## Post

We measured how much each AI engine's answer changes when you ask the same question in a different language. Not one engine against another. Each engine against itself.

The spread between the steadiest and least steady engine we measured was about six percentage points. The gap between languages inside every single engine was the other eighty-odd.

→ **12.1% to 18.7%** overlap range across the four engines measured, on identical ground
→ **5 of 16** comparisons where one engine's two language answers shared no company at all
→ **9 of 10** names one engine kept in its single most consistent category, out of 16 comparisons total

So if you were hoping there is a safe engine, one you can check in a single language and trust, there is not. The steadiest engine in our data still swapped out more than four of every five names when the language of the question changed.

There was one bright spot. In Rome real estate agencies, a category with strong national franchise networks and a heavily indexed listings ecosystem, one engine named ten companies in each language and nine of them matched. The highest agreement anywhere in the study, and it happened because that category is well documented in both languages, not because of which engine answered it.

Consistency turned out to be something you can build into your own presence. It is not something one engine happens to hand you for free.

Full engine by engine breakdown, numbers stated plainly rather than turned into a leaderboard.
