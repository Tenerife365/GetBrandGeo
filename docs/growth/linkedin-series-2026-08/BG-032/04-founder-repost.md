## Repost commentary

I will say the obvious thing before someone else does. Claude is one of the engines our own product measures for clients, and in this dataset Claude came out least consistent between languages, not most.

I thought about softening that. I decided not to, because the honest finding is not "Claude is bad." It is that none of the three engines we could rank were good at this, and the spread between the best and the worst was six points on a scale where the number that actually matters, the gap inside each engine between its own two languages, was closer to eighty.

Perplexity sat about six points ahead of Claude on stability. I do not think that means pick Perplexity. I think it means pick more than one engine and check both languages, because even Perplexity only got that steady on one specific category.

Rome real estate agencies. Perplexity named ten companies in Italian and ten in English and nine matched. It is the cleanest result in either this comparison or the language study underneath it, and I think it happened because that market is documented the same way in both languages, not because Perplexity is careful in a way the others are not.

If I had to bet, I would bet retrieval-based engines hold steadier because they can land on the same source pages regardless of the question's language. I would also tell you we cannot prove that from this data, and I am not going to pretend otherwise in either direction.
