## Article body

We asked AI engines the same four commercial questions about Paris twice, once in French and once in English. Across all four categories the two answers shared 15.3% of the companies they named, and 81.1% of the 127 companies named appeared in only one language.

One category produced the sharpest result in the whole four-city study, and it is worth reading the two lists side by side.

What we did

Paris was one of four European cities in a bilingual study we ran on 10 July 2026. Four commercial categories per city, each asked twice, once in the local language and once in English. Four engines answered: Claude, Gemini, Perplexity and Meta AI.

Two disclosures. ChatGPT returned an API error on all 32 prompts that day, so it is absent from this data rather than recorded as a zero. Meta AI was retired from our own product six days after this run, and its answers stay in the dataset, because removing a measurement to match a later product decision would falsify the record.

Wealth management: two different industries

The French question was Meilleurs conseillers en gestion de patrimoine à Paris. The English question was Best wealth management advisors in Paris.

Named in French only: Cheval Blanc Patrimoine, Agora Finance, Scala Patrimoine, Auguste Patrimoine, Prosper Conseil, Noun Partners, Haussmann Patrimoine, Rhétorès Finance, Astoria Finance, Cabinet Mazarin.

Named in English only: UBS Wealth Management, J.P. Morgan Private Bank, Lombard Odier, Mirabaud, Banque de Luxembourg, Quilvest, Aberdeen Standard Investments, Crédit Agricole Private Banking, FIDERE Family Office Paris, Laplace.

Named in both: Amundi, Pictet, Rothschild and Co, Indosuez Wealth Management, BNP Paribas Wealth Management, Société Générale Private Banking.

The left list is French independent advisory. The right list is international private banking. They are not competing lists of the same industry. They are two different industries answering the same question.

The middle list has an obvious property: every firm in it is a large institution with a long standing presence in both languages. Amundi, Pictet, Rothschild, Indosuez, BNP Paribas and Société Générale are the six firms big enough to exist in the English-language record and the French-language record at the same time.

The strongest single case: Cheval Blanc Patrimoine was named by three separate engines in French, and by none in English. Agora Finance and Rivaria Capital were each named by two engines in French and none in English. In the other direction, J.P. Morgan Private Bank and Société Générale Private Banking were each named by two engines in English and none in French.

The same shape in restaurants

Fine dining for a business dinner split the same way, if less dramatically. Kei was named by two engines in French and none in English. Arpège was named by two engines in English and none in French. Both are three star Paris restaurants. Neither is obscure. The language of the question decided which one you were told about.

What was removed before counting

The French answers produced several extraction artefacts that are not companies and were taken out by hand: "Avis Google" and "Avis Trustpilot", which are review source labels, "Frais", which means fees, and a personal finance media brand that answers a different question than which advisor. Neighbourhood and district names were removed from all four Paris categories.

What this means if you are a French business

If you are an independent French firm, the English-language answer is close to useless as a measure of your position, and it is the answer most international AI visibility tools will show you by default. In the wealth management category, an English-only measurement would have found essentially none of the French independents, and would have reported a competitive landscape made of Swiss and American banks.

If you are the French arm of an international group, the reverse risk applies. You may be visible in English, where your group's brand carries you, and absent in French, where domestic independents own the answer. Both are one measurement away from being known, in opposite directions, which is exactly why checking only one language and calling it done is the mistake this whole series keeps finding.

A separate piece in this series, BG-032, looks across all four cities at which engine changes its answer most between languages: Claude rewrote its list almost completely between the French and English versions of a question, while Perplexity held on to the most names across the language switch. The wealth management result above is one city's view of that pattern, not the whole of it.

Honest limits

Paris was the richest of the four city datasets, with 13 comparable engine and category pairs against 11 for the others, and Gemini returned usable names here where it did not elsewhere. That makes the Paris result the best supported in the study, and it is still one day, one run, four categories.

One name in the French-only column, Rivaria Capital, we could not independently confirm as an operating Paris advisory firm. We have left it in, flagged, because the finding is about what the engines printed, not about which firms exist. That distinction matters: an engine naming a firm that is hard to verify is itself worth knowing about, and quietly deleting it would hide it.

The way to know your own number is not to trust ours. Ask both versions of your own question, of at least three engines, on the same day, and compare the two lists yourself.
