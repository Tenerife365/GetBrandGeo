## Post

We asked AI engines who manages wealth in Paris. In French and in English.

In French, they named French independent advisors. Cheval Blanc Patrimoine. Scala Patrimoine. Auguste Patrimoine. Prosper Conseil. Haussmann Patrimoine. Small firms, French names, French clients.

In English, they named global private banks. UBS Wealth Management. J.P. Morgan Private Bank. Lombard Odier. Mirabaud. Crédit Agricole Private Banking.

These are not two rankings of the same industry. They are two different industries answering the same question.

Six firms showed up in both languages: Amundi, Pictet, Rothschild and Co, Indosuez Wealth Management, BNP Paribas Wealth Management, Société Générale Private Banking. Every one of them is a large institution with a decades-long presence in both languages. Size, not quality, is what got them into both columns.

→ **15.3%** overlap between the French answer and the English answer
→ **81.1%** of the 127 companies named appeared in only one language
→ **3** engines named Cheval Blanc Patrimoine in French, **0** did in English

This is the sharpest single result across the four European cities we measured.

Fine dining split the same way, if less dramatically. Kei was named by two engines in French and none in English. Arpège was named by two engines in English and none in French. Both are three star Paris restaurants. Neither is obscure. The language of the question decided which one you were told about.

If you are an independent French firm, checking your AI visibility in English is close to useless as a measure of your actual position, because that is not the answer your customers are getting. If you are the French arm of an international group, the risk runs the other way: visible in English, where your group's name carries you, and absent in French, where domestic independents own the answer.

Full study, with the complete wealth management lists side by side, and an honest note on the one name in it we could not independently verify.
