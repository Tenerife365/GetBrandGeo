## Repost commentary

There is a name in this week's article I could have quietly deleted, and I want to explain why I did not.

Two of our engines named a Paris wealth advisory firm, Rivaria Capital, when we asked in French who manages wealth in Paris. I tried to confirm it is an operating firm. I could not. It might exist under a name I did not find, it might be a garbled version of something real, or an engine might have generated it outright.

The easy move is to cut it and quietly report a cleaner number. I left it in, flagged, because the study is not measuring which advisory firms exist in Paris. It is measuring what an AI engine tells a person who asks it a question in French. If an engine will confidently name a firm I cannot verify, that is itself a finding, maybe a more important one than most of the names I could verify.

The rest of the Paris result does not need that caveat. Cheval Blanc Patrimoine, named by three engines in French and zero in English, is a real firm. So is every name in the six that showed up in both languages, all of them large enough to have a genuine presence in two languages at once. Fifteen percent overlap between the French answer and the English answer, on a question about who manages your money, is the sharpest gap we found across four cities. I would stand behind that number completely. I would stand behind Rivaria Capital only as evidence of what an engine will say, not as evidence that the firm is real.
