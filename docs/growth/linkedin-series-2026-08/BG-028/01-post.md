## Post

We asked four AI engines for the best employment lawyers in Berlin.

In German, they gave us people. Andreas Martin. Benjamin Stumpp. Philipp Kitzmann. Small practices, named individuals.

In English, same question, same day, same engines, they gave us institutions. CMS Deutschland. Beiten Burkhardt. GÖRG. Pusch Wahlig Workplace Law.

Two names survived the language change. Everything else in one list was simply absent from the other.

Berlin turned out to have the widest language gap of the four European cities we measured.

→ **8.9%** overlap between the German answer and the English answer
→ **88.5%** of the 139 companies named appeared in only one language
→ **4 of 11** engine and category pairs shared no company at all

You could argue this is a quirk of professional services, where German answers name a person and English answers name a firm.

So look at software, where nobody is named at all.

Asked in German which accounting software German startups use, two separate engines named Lexware Office. Asked the identical question in English, not one engine named it.

Asked in English which HR software Berlin startups use, two engines named HeavenHR. Asked in German, none did.

Same engines. Same day. The language decided whether the company existed.

Here is the part that decides who should care. A large firm with an English site, English press and international directory listings shows up in the English answers and often not the German ones. A small practice with a German-only web presence shows up in the German answers and not the English ones.

Whichever of those you are, you are invisible in exactly the half you are not looking at.

Full study, with the categories we removed before counting and an honest note on where German name extraction is hardest.
