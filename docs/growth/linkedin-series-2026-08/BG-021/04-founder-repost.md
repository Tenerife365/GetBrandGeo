## Repost commentary

I will say the part our own pricing page cannot say as cleanly. Our engine count went down this year, not up, and I would do it again.

We retired Meta AI in July because it stopped being able to see anything. The models we could reach through it answered from training data only, with no web search. It read exactly like an engine that had gone and looked. It had not. A business that launched last month simply never appeared in its answers, no matter how well that business was doing, and a competitor that closed two years ago could still be sitting there in third place. Keeping it would have meant a bigger number on a pricing page. Removing it meant a smaller, honest one.

I have noticed that in this category, engine counts only ever go up. I do not think that is always dishonest. Sometimes it is just easier to add a name than to go back and check whether one you already shipped is still doing the one thing that earned it a slot in the first place. But a list that only grows is not evidence of a better product. It is evidence that nobody has gone back and asked the question.

We turned down a cheap engine addition this same month, for the identical reason we removed one in July. I would rather stand behind a smaller number than a bigger one I cannot defend if someone asks me directly what was actually switched on when we queried it.
