## Post

We shipped two AI engines today. The more useful story is the two we turned down.

Every tool in this category advertises an engine count. Five, seven, twelve. The number is easy to raise and it is almost never the thing that determines whether the measurement behind it is any good.

Here is the question that actually separates them: when that engine answered, did it go and look, or did it answer from memory?

→ 7 engines monitored on Growth PRO and above, every one of them queried with live web search enabled
→ 2 added today: Grok, and Google AI Overviews, measured as a separate surface from Google AI Mode
→ 1 retired on 16 July 2026: Meta AI, because the models we could reach in that path answered from training data only, with no web search available
→ 2 we looked at and left out: DeepSeek, same reason as Meta AI, and Copilot, because Microsoft ships no public API for it

An engine earns a slot here when we can query it with retrieval switched on, through a documented path, for every run that produces a customer's report. "The model supports web search" and "web search was enabled in our pipeline" are different claims, and only the second one is worth anything to you.

The one most tools collapse into one line: Google AI Mode is a tab a searcher has to switch to on purpose. Google AI Overviews is the AI summary shown by default at the top of an ordinary results page, reaching a far larger share of searches. They disagree often enough that averaging them would hide the thing you actually need to know. We measure both, separately.

Removing Meta AI lowered our own advertised number. We did it anyway, and we would do it again for any engine that stopped retrieving.

Four questions worth asking whatever tool you currently use, in the first comment.
