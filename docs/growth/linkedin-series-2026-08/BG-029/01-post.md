## Post

Madrid looked like the exception to everything we found in Berlin.

Berlin's German and English AI answers overlapped by 8.9%. Madrid's overlapped by 23.2%, the highest of the four European cities we measured. On the face of it, Spanish-language and English-language visibility looked closer here than anywhere else.

It is not close. One category is doing almost all of the work.

Ask which hotel is recommended near Madrid-Barajas airport, in Spanish or in English, and both languages return the same seven names: Crowne Plaza, Hilton, Barajas Plaza, Clement Barajas, Maydrit Airport, Ibis Aeropuerto, Meliá Barajas. That is the highest-agreement category in our entire four-city study, and there is nothing Spanish about it. Every one of those names is an international chain whose website, and whose write-ups, already exist in both languages equally.

Take that one category out of the picture and Madrid starts to look like everywhere else.

→ **23.2%** overlap, the highest of the four cities, and mostly one category's doing
→ **74.7%** of the 87 companies named still appeared in only one language
→ **0** engines named Only YOU Boutique Hotel Madrid in Spanish, **2** did in English

Restaurants split the way the rest of Europe did. Asked in Spanish, engines named Amazónico, Ramses, El Invernadero, Maison Umami, Santceloni, La Castilla. Asked in English, they named Botín, Coque, DSTAgE, Saddle, Dani Brasserie, El Pichón, La Marisquería. Four names survived both lists.

Real estate agencies split by size, not by fame. Spanish kept it local: Gilmar, ÔKAM, Monago Consultores, Neinor Homes, Tecnocasa. English added the international networks: Knight Frank, Keller Williams, Remax, Lifetime Properties.

Here is the rule Madrid proves. The size of your language gap is a property of your category, not your country. Compete against global chains and both languages converge on the same list. Compete against other independents, which is most Spanish businesses in most categories, and the two lists diverge. The Spanish one is the one your customers are reading.

Full study, including the airport hotel case and an honest note on why Madrid's data is our thinnest of the four cities.
