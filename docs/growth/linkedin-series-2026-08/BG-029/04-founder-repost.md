## Repost commentary

Madrid is the one city in our four-city study that looks like it disagrees with everything else we found.

Berlin's German and English answers overlapped by 8.9%. Madrid's overlapped by 23.2%, the highest of the four. If I only showed you that one number, the honest reaction would be: maybe this language gap thing is smaller than BrandGEO is making it sound, at least somewhere.

I do not think that reaction is wrong to have. I think it is wrong once you look at what is actually inside that 23.2%.

Almost all of it is one category: hotels near Madrid-Barajas airport. Ask in Spanish or English and you get the same seven international chains, because their names do not change between languages and neither does anything written about them. Take that category out, and a boutique hotel with an English brand name, Only YOU, gets named twice in English and zero times in Spanish. Restaurants split the way they did in every other city. Real estate splits by whether the agency is local or international.

I would also rather say this myself than have someone else point it out: Madrid is the thinnest dataset of the four cities we ran. One of our four engines returned usable company names in only one of the eight Madrid prompts. That is a real limitation, not a footnote I am adding for comfort. I would defend the direction of this finding without hesitation. I would defend the exact 23.2% a lot less confidently than I would Berlin's 8.9%.
