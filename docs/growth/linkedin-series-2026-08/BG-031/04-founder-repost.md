## Repost commentary

The obvious objection to this one is that Rome is a tourist city, so of course the local recommendation and the guidebook recommendation are different lists. That is not a finding, that is supply and demand.

I think that objection is actually half right, and it is more useful to say so than to argue past it.

But it does not explain the real estate case. Nobody markets a real estate agency to tourists. There is no guidebook for that. And in that one category, Perplexity named ten companies in Italian and ten in English, and nine of them were the same names. The gap that opens up around restaurants and boutique hotels does not open up there.

Sitting with both of those, what I take from it is that the language gap is not a property of the category, it is a property of how well the business is documented in each language. Restaurants and small hotels in Rome mostly get written about in Italian by themselves and in English by travel media. Real estate agencies with national franchise networks get indexed the same way no matter which language you search in.

18.0% overlap between the two answers, across the whole study. Wide everywhere your business is a local story told in only one language. Narrow the moment it stops being one.
