## Post

We asked four AI engines for a restaurant recommendation for a business dinner in Rome.

In Italian, they named Trapizzino, Supplizio, Osteria Fratelli Mori, Glass Hostaria, Alla Bottega Trattoria de Santis.

In English, same question, same day, same engines, they named La Pergola, Salumeria Roscioli, Pierluigi, Dal Bolognese, Enoteca la Torre.

Five names made both lists. Everything else split along a line that reads almost editorial. Italian returned the places a Roman might actually book. English returned the places an international visitor has already read about somewhere else.

→ **18.0%** overlap between the Italian answer and the English answer
→ **81.2%** of the 133 companies named appeared in only one language
→ **3 Rome hotels** named by two engines in Italian and by none in English

The hotel data was sharper still. Hotel Raphael, Nerva Boutique Hotel and Singer Palace Hotel were each named twice in Italian and zero times in English. In the other direction, G-Rough was named twice in English and zero times in Italian.

You could read this as Rome being a tourist city, so of course the international guide and the local one differ. Fair, except the gap is not fixed. In real estate agencies, the one Rome category with a heavily indexed, nationally franchised market, Perplexity named ten companies in Italian and ten in English, and nine of them were the same names.

The gap narrows to almost nothing when a category is well documented in both languages. It stays wide when a business is documented mainly in one.

If your restaurant, hotel or practice has a strong web presence in only one language, that is not a rounding error. It is the half of the answer you are missing.
