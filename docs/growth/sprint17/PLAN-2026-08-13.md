## 1. The arithmetic, before anyone commits 17 days to a number

This section exists because committing the sprint to an unreachable number costs
more than the number is worth: it hides the wins that do land, and it picks the
wrong activities.

**Baseline, measured over the 38 days since launch:**

| Metric | All time | Per day |
|---|---|---|
| Real public audits | 8 | 0.21 |
| Self-serve signups | ~5 | 0.13 |
| Self-serve subscriptions | 0 | 0 |
| Paying clients | 1 (BpR, hand-sold, EUR 3,500 paid) | n/a |

**What 100 paid self-serve subscribers requires**, using generous conversion
assumptions (BrandGEO has no conversion data of its own, so these are industry
INFERRED and deliberately flattering):

- 100 paid at a 5 percent free-to-paid rate needs **2,000 free signups**, or 118 a day.
- 2,000 signups at a 20 percent audit-to-signup rate (roughly triple today's, and
  only reachable if the withheld painkiller is unlocked) needs **10,000 audits**.
- 10,000 audits at a 10 percent visitor-to-audit rate needs **100,000 visitors**,
  or about 5,900 a day, from a site that produced 8 audits in 38 days.

**What the available channels can actually deliver in 17 days**, given Product
Hunt is already spent (a product launches once; it scored 3 upvotes on Aug 4),
social starts from 0 followers and is blocked on the auto-poster, cold email
cannot send before its 5 to 7 day warmup completes, and the ~100 SEO pages are
invisible to Google with indexing measured in weeks:

- Realistic ceiling: **1,000 to 3,000 visitors**, 50 to 300 audits, 10 to 60 free
  signups, **0 to 3 paid self-serve subscriptions**.

The gap is not 20 percent. It is roughly 30x to 100x. INFERRED, from the measured
baseline and the measured channel states in `docs/audit/gtm-channel-audit-2026-08-13.md`.

**Meanwhile the one motion with a proven close is founder-led sales.** Every real
client was founder-sold and hand-provisioned; BpR just paid EUR 3,500. 100 Radar
subscriptions are worth EUR 2,900 a month. Two to four done-for-you clients at
EUR 299 to EUR 1,500 produce the same money or more, through the only mechanism
that has ever closed.

## 2. The recommended goal, three numbers instead of one

Ambition is kept. The target is restated so it can be hit, measured, and learned
from. `gtm-lead` runs against these; Constantin may overrule any of them.

| # | Goal | Target by 2026-08-29 | Why this one |
|---|---|---|---|
| **G1** | **Revenue** | **EUR 2,900 MRR-equivalent**, from any mix of founder-sold and self-serve | Identical money to 100 Radar subs, reachable through the motion that has actually closed |
| **G2** | **Volume** | **100 activated free accounts** (signed up AND ran one collection) | The leading indicator for self-serve, and the honest reading of "100 subscribers"; a stretch at roughly 6 a day against 0.13 today |
| **G3** | **The machine** | **3 or more channels firing daily by day 7 and still firing on day 17** | The audit's core finding is that no channel has ever run continuously. This is the only goal whose value survives past day 17 |

G3 is non-negotiable and is the one to protect if the sprint runs short of time.
G1 is the commercial commitment. G2 is the stretch.

## 5. Pricing surface recommendation (decision 5)

Asked for: what works best, given the audit. Recommendation, with the reasoning
so it can be argued with.

**Show three cards, not nine choices: Free, Radar EUR 29, Growth EUR 299.**
Move Essentials, Growth PRO, Managed and Enterprise behind a "Compare all plans"
link. Reasoning:

- Nine choices in one section is the measured state (5 cards, a mode switch to
  2 more, a billing toggle, and a second free offer pointing at the 48-hour
  form). A visitor who cannot self-select does not buy.
- **Free** stays because the instant audit is the strongest asset the company
  has and the free tier is its landing spot.
- **Radar EUR 29** is the volume tier and the only impulse-priced rung, which is
  what a subscriber-count goal needs.
- **Growth EUR 299** is the revenue tier and the anchor that makes EUR 29 read
  as small. It is also the tier the done-for-you conversation lands on.
- **Essentials EUR 99 is the one to hide.** It is the worst-positioned rung on
  the board: Peec Starter is EUR 85 with 50 prompts, 3 models of the buyer's
  choice, and daily refresh, against Essentials' 18 prompts, fixed engines, and
  weekly. Hiding it removes the comparison a prospect is most likely to lose.
- Nothing is deleted, no ruled price moves, and the hidden tiers stay buyable.
  This is reversible in one edit if the numbers argue against it.

**Fix Radar's competitiveness with words, not price.** Radar loses to Otterly
Lite ($29, 15 prompts, 4 engines including ChatGPT, daily) on every published
axis. Do not reprice: the ladder arithmetic is sound and there is no volume to
measure a change against. Instead:

1. **Name the engines on every card.** Free is Gemini, Radar is Gemini and
   Claude, ChatGPT starts at Essentials. A visitor who assumes ChatGPT and is
   not told is being allowed to believe something untrue, and will discover it
   at exactly the wrong moment. This also protects the ChatGPT-on-cost ruling
   rather than fighting it.
2. **Rewrite each card in outcomes, not units.** Every tier is currently
   described in prompts, engines and pages. The sprint ladder ruling's own
   decision 4 says "a package sells a tier, not prompts", and the cards never
   caught up. "Track the 7 questions your buyers actually ask" beats "7
   commercial prompts".
3. **Sell Radar on what it is uniquely good at**: it is the cheapest way to find
   out whether AI answers name you at all, weekly, with a landing page audit
   included. That is a different job from Otterly's breadth, and it is
   defensible without a superlative.

**One number worth rechecking before the ChatGPT ruling is final.** The decision
to keep ChatGPT out was made on a cost of EUR 0.108 per check, from
`sprint-ladder-ruling.md`. Production has since measured 152 real ChatGPT rows
at an average of **EUR 0.0615 per check** (`_cost.js:383-391`), about 43 percent
lower. That makes ChatGPT in the free screening audit roughly **EUR 0.25 per
audit**, not EUR 0.43: about EUR 1.50 a month at today's audit rate, EUR 37 a
month at 5 audits a day, EUR 150 a month at 20 a day. The ruling stands until
Constantin changes it; this is recorded so the decision is made against the
measured number rather than the estimated one, and it is the single change that
would most improve the free taste if the audit ever gets the traffic this sprint
is trying to send it.

## 6. Founder actions with exact commands

**BpR provisioning, now that INV-35 is paid.** Nothing does this automatically:
`stripe-webhook.js` handles `checkout.session.completed`,
`customer.subscription.updated` and `customer.subscription.deleted`, but not
`invoice.paid`. Run in the Supabase SQL editor:

```sql
UPDATE clients
SET plan             = 'growth_pro',
    plan_source      = 'package',
    plan_grant_until = '2027-06-02',
    plan_grant_note  = 'INV-35 founding package, EUR 3500, 10 months Growth PRO, paid. Activation on payment confirmation. Provisioned by hand 2026-08-13 per handoff-billing-2026-08-02.md step 1; stripe-webhook.js does not handle invoice.paid.'
WHERE id = 1;

INSERT INTO client_events (client_id, type, from_plan, to_plan, meta)
VALUES (1, 'plan_change', 'growth_pro', 'growth_pro',
        '{"reason":"INV-35 paid","invoice":"INV-35","amount_eur":3500,"term_months":10,"grant_until":"2027-06-02","provisioned":"manual","source":"handoff-billing-2026-08-02.md"}'::jsonb);
```

Current state confirmed before writing this: client 1 is already `growth_pro`
but `plan_source` and `plan_grant_until` are both null, so the grant is
unrecorded and the audit trail has a EUR 3,500 hole.

**Still open from the billing handoff, and now live commitments:** the invoice
promises 4 social channels while `PLAN_SOCIAL_CHANNEL_LIMIT.growth_pro = 3`, so
delivery needs a per-client override or a limit change; the Stripe catalogue
product descriptions still say "five engines" where Growth PRO has seven; and

## 7. The weekly auto-refresh ruling, and its sequencing trap

Constantin ruled on 2026-08-13 that automatic weekly refresh is better for
delivered value. Agreed, and it closes a live gap: 36 of 38 clients sit on
`manual`, so the weekly refresh every paid card advertises is not being
delivered to anyone who bought before 08-02.

**But the order is load-bearing and reversing it makes things worse.** Scheduled
runs are force-refreshes: `_enqueue.js:138-152` deletes the existing
`ai_results` rows for those prompts and engines, then recollects. This is
already proven in production, where the one weekly client's 08-02 rows were
wiped by the 08-09 scheduled run. Turning weekly cadence on across the book
before fixing that would delete every client's history every week, permanently
preventing the trend from accumulating, on a product whose own upgrade argument
is that the trend is the point.

Correct sequence:

1. `bg-backend`, briefed by `bg-architect`: make the scheduled path
   snapshot-and-append (or archive rows before delete), and fix the dashboard
   reads that aggregate all rows rather than newest-per-(prompt, engine), which
   is the reason the delete exists in the first place.
2. Then backfill: `UPDATE clients SET refresh_cadence = refreshCadenceFor(plan,
   category)` in effect, which yields weekly for every paid tier, monthly for
   free, and `manual` for the 27 research rows. The research exclusion is
   load-bearing: deriving cadence from plan alone would switch on about
   EUR 6,075 a month of budget ceiling for rows that are studies, not customers.
3. Then add the "your refresh ran, here is what changed" email, which is the
   only reason a free user would ever come back and does not exist today.

Step 2 spends real money across the existing book and is a HUMAN CHECKPOINT.
Step 1 without step 2 is still worth shipping on its own.

## 8. Open ruling this plan assumes

**Which motion owns the homepage** was left to this team. The sprint assumes
**self-serve owns the homepage for the 17 days**, because the goal is subscriber
count and the self-serve ladder is what the homepage already sells, with
`get-found-online.html` given a named route in the nav for the done-for-you
buyer instead of being linked zero times as it is today.

This is a working assumption, reversible after day 17, and it is in tension with
the 2026-07-18 ruling in `GTM-STRATEGY.md` section 4.2b that made the
done-for-you SMB buyer primary. That tension is real: G1 (revenue) will most
likely be carried by the done-for-you motion while G2 (volume) is carried by
self-serve. `gtm-conversion` should treat the pricing mode switch as the buyer
switch it actually is, and raise it to a decision the visitor makes early rather
than a toggle they find late.
