## Post

Across four European cities we found that 82.1% of the companies AI engines named appeared in only one of the two languages we asked in.

We also found one exception: Rome real estate agencies, through Perplexity, where 9 of 10 names matched across both languages.

What was different about that one category is the checklist. Nine items, ordered by how much work each one is against how much of the gap it plausibly closes. None of them require a rebuild.

The four that remove a blocker outright:

→ Your second language pages have to be real, fetchable URLs. Not a JavaScript switcher rewriting the same page, not a cookie, not a browser redirect. If a plain HTTP request to the German URL does not return German content, your German site does not exist for retrieval purposes, and nothing else on this list matters until that is fixed.

→ Reciprocal hreflang tags, each language pointing at the others and at itself, plus x-default. An afternoon of work.

→ Structured data in the language of the page it sits on, not the language you wrote the template in. We found three of our own FAQPage blocks silently invalid for weeks over one missing brace, which meant they were dropped entirely rather than misread.

→ One spelling of your own name, everywhere. Our study returned "Only You Boutique Hotel" and "Only YOU Boutique Hotel Madrid" as two separate strings from the same run. Any system counting mentions counts those twice, or matches neither.

The other five are more speculative, and we say so directly in the piece: getting described in the second language by something that is not your own site, answering the buyer's actual question on a page, not machine translating and walking away, keeping the same facts in both languages, and measuring both languages on a schedule so you know whether anything you did actually worked.

If you only do three, do the first, third and fourth on this list: the real indexable pages, the structured data in the right language, and the one consistent spelling of your own name.

Full nine, and which category converged and why, in the first comment.
