## Post

You can test whether ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity recommend your brand in your customers' own language in about ten minutes. No tool, no budget, no signup.

We ran this properly across four European cities and found that 82.1% of the companies these engines named appeared in only one of the two languages we asked in.

Here is the method, compressed to what you can actually do today.

→ Write the question a real buyer types. Not your brand name. "Best boutique hotels in central Madrid," not "what do you know about us." Naming yourself tests whether the engine has heard of you. The buyer question tests whether it recommends you to someone who has not.

→ Translate it as literally as the two languages allow. Change one variable at a time, not two.

→ Ask both versions, same day, at least three engines, each in a fresh conversation. In our study, one engine alone produced five comparisons where the two language answers shared zero companies. A single engine can be maximally unrepresentative in either direction.

→ Write down both lists of companies named, in order, not just your impression of them. Then ask three questions: Are you in both lists? Are the two lists even the same industry? Who shows up in both, and what are they doing that you are not?

Three mistakes ruin this test before it starts. Reading one run as a measurement. Counting a blank or errored answer as a zero (ChatGPT errored on all 32 prompts in our own collection run, so we excluded it rather than record it as a dramatic zero). And assuming your customers' local language is automatically the one that matters. Check who actually buys from you, not who you assume does.

Full method, plus where our own name extraction gets weakest, in the first comment.
