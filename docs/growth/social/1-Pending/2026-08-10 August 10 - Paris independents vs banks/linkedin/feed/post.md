We asked AI for wealth management advisors in Paris, once in French and once in English, same day.

The French question returned Cheval Blanc Patrimoine, Agora Finance, Scala Patrimoine, Auguste Patrimoine, Prosper Conseil, Noun Partners and Haussmann Patrimoine. French independents, several of them small.

The English question returned UBS Wealth Management, J.P. Morgan Private Bank, Lombard Odier, Mirabaud, Banque de Luxembourg and Quilvest. Global private banks.

Six names cleared both lists, and every one of them was a large institution with an established English-language presence: firms like Amundi, Pictet and Rothschild & Co.

Paris landed at 127 companies named across the two languages, 81.1% of them appearing in only one, 15.3% answer overlap between the French and English versions of the same question.

Those are not two rankings of the same market. They are two different categories that happen to share a language pair. A French independent competing only against the French list has no idea a second, entirely different shortlist exists in English, and the reverse is just as true for the global banks.

If you sell into a market that speaks more than one language, ask which language your revenue actually speaks before you trust either answer on its own.
