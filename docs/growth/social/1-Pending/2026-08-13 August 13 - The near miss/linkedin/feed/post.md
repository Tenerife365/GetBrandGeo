"Only You Boutique Hotel" and "Only YOU Boutique Hotel Madrid" came back as two separate strings from the same collection run, describing the same hotel.

Any automated system counting mentions counts those twice, or matches neither. That is not a wording preference. It is a near miss with a real cost: the hotel is present in the data and can still read as absent, depending on which spelling a pattern matcher happened to catch.

We found this while building the checklist behind our four city multilingual study, on our own data, and it is the same kind of mistake that produces an invented company name or a merged brand elsewhere in that dataset. It reads as correct at a glance, which is exactly what makes it expensive.

The fix is one canonical spelling of your own name, in every language, on every page, in your structured data, in your directory listings. Cheap, binary, and the kind of check that sits undone for months precisely because nothing visibly breaks.
