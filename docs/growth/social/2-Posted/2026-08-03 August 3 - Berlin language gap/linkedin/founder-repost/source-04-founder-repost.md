## Repost commentary

The objection I expected to this one came from our own team, and it was a good objection.

German names the lawyer. English names the practice. So when AI gives you Andreas Martin in German and CMS Deutschland in English, maybe we have not found a visibility gap at all. Maybe we have found a naming convention, and dressed it up as a finding.

That is a fair thing to say about professional services. It is not a fair thing to say about accounting software.

Ask in German which accounting software German startups use, and two separate engines name Lexware Office. Ask the identical question in English, on the same day, of the same engines, and not one of them names it.

Nobody is a person in that category. There is no naming convention to blame. The language decided whether a company existed.

Berlin was the widest gap of the four European cities we measured. 8.9% overlap between the two answers.

I have also put in writing where our own method is weakest, which is German specifically, because German answers name people and build compound words and our extractor handles that worse than it handles "Hilton". Some of the gap is that. The software cases are the ones I would defend.
