Madrid was a coverage problem. Paris, tested the same day, was something else.

Asked in French about wealth management advisors, the engines named independent local firms. Asked in English, they named large international private banks. Not the same list reordered by language. A different set of companies. One firm that showed up repeatedly in the French answers was missing from the English ones altogether.

So if you sell in more than one language, a clean result in one of them is weak evidence about the other. They behave like separate markets because the engines are reading separate sources for each.
