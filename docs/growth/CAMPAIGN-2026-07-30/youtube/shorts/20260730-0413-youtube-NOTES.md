**The point of this cut is to separate the DRIVER from the EXECUTION.** Run

## How this differs from run 1, and why each difference was chosen

Run 1 is `docs/growth/reel-campaign-ab/run-20260729-2200/youtube/`. It was read
in full before a word of this was written.

| | Run 1 (20260729-2200) | This cut (20260730-0413) |
|---|---|---|
| Opens on | the **event**: "An AI answered a question about your market today." | the **outcome**: "A shortlist got made this week. You were not in the room." |
| Direction of travel | forward in time from the event | backward from the artefact to the decision that produced it |
| What is lost | traffic and answers you cannot see | **candidacy**. The chance to be considered at all |
| Where the loss sits | in your analytics, as an absence | **upstream** of your site, ads and reviews, before any of them get a turn |
| Strongest beat | "Search at least told you your rank. AI answers tell you nothing by default." | "Two names came back from all five. Four of the five put them in the same order." |
| Evidence | none. No figure appears anywhere in run 1 | one measured result, sourced and dated |
| Scenes / length | 8 / 41.4 s | 10 / 43.8 s |

Three of those are constraints from the run brief rather than preferences:

**The opening had to move off the event**, because "an AI answered a question
about your market today" is run 1's whole first beat and reusing it would make
the two cuts a re-render rather than a replication.

**The search-versus-AI comparison is deliberately absent.** It is run 1's
strongest line and it is also the entire territory of driver #4, contrarian
("ranking first in Google does not mean you exist in AI"). Using it here would
contaminate two comparisons at once: this cut against run 1, and driver #1
against driver #4.

**"No click, no referrer, no row in your analytics" and "the loss leaves no
trace" are absent too.** That is run 1's middle third. The invisibility of the
loss is not this cut's argument. This cut's argument is that the loss happens in
a room you are not in and is finished before anything you control gets a turn,
which is a different fear from the same driver.

The remaining shape available to loss aversion that neither run uses is "the gap
between what your analytics show and what actually happened". That one is
reserved: it is the closest of the three to run 1's analytics beat and would make
a weaker separation than this one. It is the obvious candidate for a third pass.

### Why the cut is built this way

**Scene 1 states the conclusion first.** A loss-aversion hook works on the
feeling that something has already been decided. Run 1 built to that feeling over
three scenes; this one asserts it in four lines and spends the rest of the video
earning it. Shorts tolerates a beat more setup than the other three platforms, so
that budget is spent on scenes 6 and 7 rather than on the hook.

**Scenes 2 and 3 reconstruct the room, then name what was made in it.** "Not a
page of links" is the whole difference between this and a search-ranking problem,
and it is stated as a property of the artefact rather than as a comparison, so it
does not step on driver #4.

**Scene 4 is the load-bearing line.** Run 1's loss was a visit that never
happened. That framing invites the viewer to price it as one lost click. The loss
here is being considered at all, which is not recoverable by a better landing
page, and it is the version of the claim that survives a sceptical reading.

**Scene 5 places it in the funnel.** Everything a business spends on its own
presentation happens after the shortlist exists. Stated about the sequence, not
about people, so it needs no universal to land.

**Scenes 6, 7 and 8 are the evidence.** Run 1 shipped with no figure at all. If
loss aversion is going to be measured twice, the second pass should test whether
the driver still works when it is made checkable, so this one carries exactly one
measured result and says where and when it was taken.

**Scene 8 is what the number means, and it is deliberately small.** "That is not
a coin toss. That is a settled answer." It claims the answer was stable, not that
it is permanent, not that it is universal, and not that anyone is locked out.

### Method B, per-frame diff against a text-free control

`control.mp4` is the same plate with the same drift crop, the same duration, the
same frame count, the same numbered PNG frame sequence and the same encoder
settings, with no text composited at all. Diffing against a control rather than a
flat assumed canvas colour is what stops the `yuv420p` frame-edge chroma artefact
being reported as a failure on every edge.

**The diff threshold was measured.** A control cancels artefacts it shares; it
cannot cancel libx264 responding to content it does not have. So the noise floor
was measured in three regions empty by design, all far outside the layer union of
x 199..782 y 594..1172:

```
top strip   y 0..400      max |delivered - control| = 5
left strip  x 0..150      max |delivered - control| = 5
bottom      y 1500..1919  max |delivered - control| = 4
noise floor 5  ->  threshold 7
```

### The dark-on-dark blind spot, checked rather than assumed

The logo asset is a card, not a bare mark, and its field sits at or below the
canvas value, so no pixel method can resolve it. Probed on frame 1254 across the
whole declared rect, delivered against control:

```
declared logo rect x 392..587  y 596..791   (38,416 px)
  delivered Y: min   3  max 129  mean 24.5
  control   Y: min  12  max  15  mean 13.2
  |diff| max 116   pixels with diff>7: 5,063 of 38,416
  delivered pixels above abs threshold 56: 4,843 of 38,416

  diff>7  resolves only  x 448..530  y 640..744
  abs>56  resolves only  x 449..530  y 641..744
```

Both methods resolve only the roughly 83x105 violet mark and **miss 87% of the
card**, exactly as on runs 2, 4, 5 and 6. The card's minimum stored Y of 3 is
**below** the control's minimum of 12, so at its darkest it is not merely
invisible to a threshold, it is on the wrong side of the background. The union
therefore folds in the **declared** rectangle `x 392..587 y 596..791`, which is
fully contained by the type union on all four edges and changes nothing. It was
checked, not assumed.

**A one-pixel geometry bug was found and fixed by this same discipline.**
ffmpeg 8.1.2's `overlay` rounds an odd `y` DOWN to the next even row: measured
directly, `overlay=392:595` lands the logo at y 594. That would have made the
declared rect off by one against the pixels actually written, which is exactly
the kind of drift that makes a declared-rect pass untrustworthy for the one
element it exists to cover. Block tops are now snapped to even rows in
`mktext.js`, at a cost of at most one pixel of vertical centring, and the
declared rect and the measured alpha now agree exactly.

## Build

`ffmpeg 8.1.2-full_build-www.gyan.dev`, `node v24.16.0`, `numpy 2.4.6`. Remotion
is not installed and was not used. Nothing was installed. PNG intermediates
throughout, no JPEG, so nothing gets forced to `yuvj420p`.

Pipeline, five scripts:

1. **`mkbg.js`** writes a 1188x2112 binary PPM plate: base `#090A0F`, a `#7C3AED`
   glow top right, a `#6366F1` glow bottom left, a faint `#8B5CF6` centre lift and
   an edge vignette. Oversized so the video can drift-crop 1080x1920 out of it
   without hitting an edge. It prints its own peak luma, which is the first half
   of the threshold argument. `ffmpeg -i bg.ppm bg.png`.
2. **`scenes.js`** is the single source of truth for the cut: copy, per-line font,
   size, colour, and per-scene duration. Everything downstream reads it.
3. **`mktext.js`** runs the compliance assertion, then renders one transparent
   RGBA layer per scene with `drawbox` + `drawtext`, writing every string to a
   `.txt` file first and feeding it via `textfile=`. It emits `layout.json`
   (per-scene `blockTop`, `ruleTop`, declared logo rect) which the checkers read.
4. **`build.js`** composites to a numbered PNG frame sequence and encodes, and
   emits a text-free control through the identical pipeline.
5. **`measure.py`**, **`layercheck.py`**, **`probe.py`**, **`namescan.py`** are the
   four verification passes. All exit non-zero on failure.

### Traps this build had to handle

- **`color=black@0.0` does not survive format negotiation.** The lavfi source
  drops its `@alpha` and lands opaque, a subsequent `format=rgba` fills alpha with
  255, and every text layer ships as a full-frame black rectangle over the
  background with exit 0 and no warning. Used
  `color=c=black,format=rgba,colorchannelmixer=aa=0` with `-update 1
  -pix_fmt rgba` on the PNG write, and `layercheck.py` asserts the failure mode
  explicitly.
- **`drawbox` on transparent RGBA blends instead of writing alpha.** It renders at
  alpha 0 and vanishes silently. `replace=1` is set, and the drift column plus the
  ten per-scene rule probes are the two independent confirmations it worked.
- **`drawbox` cannot animate on 8.1.2.** No progress bar is drawn in this cut, so
  the trap does not arise; had one been wanted it would have been N static boxes
  gated by `enable`.
- **`overlay` snaps an odd y down to even.** Found by measurement, fixed by
  snapping block tops to even rows. See the safe-zone section.
- **No `ffconcat`.** Numbered PNG frame sequence at `-framerate 30`.
- **No `-ss` anywhere in verification.** Both files are decoded sequentially in
  one pass and indexed.
