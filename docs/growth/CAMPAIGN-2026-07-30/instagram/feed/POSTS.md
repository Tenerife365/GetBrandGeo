Property management reached 5 of 5 AI engines in Boston. Real estate agents reached 2 of 5.

Same city, same day, same five engines, same method. Houston ran the same six categories and split the same way: property management at 4 of 5, real estate agents at 2 of 5.

Our explanation for the split is a hypothesis and not a measured cause. Companies have documentation. Individuals have listings. ## Notes worth reading before posting

- **Post 1 and Post 4 both link to the Chicago city page.** That page carries a
  page level superlative claim about the research program that does not hold up
  against the Boston page collected the same day. Nothing in these captions or
  on any card quotes it. The invented name, the prompt, the ranks and the
  collection date are separate and trace to that page's own tables.
- **No caption and no card names a company or a person that turned up inside a
  result set.** Post 1 says "a firm name", Post 4 says "the same company", and
  both do so deliberately. Naming the engine being measured is fine and is done
  throughout.
- **Post 3 carries no engine count anywhere**, on the card or in the caption.
  The audit it draws on names an engine lineup that is not the current one, and
  the caption says so out loud rather than quietly omitting it.
- **No card states today's engine lineup.** Every finding here was measured on
  a five engine run, and putting the current seven on the same card would read
  as the denominator of the finding. If the current lineup has to appear in a
  comment or a reply, the shape to use is "we monitor seven engines; these runs
  predate two of them", never a single sentence that mixes the two.
- **"Link in bio"** closes every caption because Instagram captions do not carry
  live links. The first comment block holds the real URL. If you would rather
  not use a first comment, the bio link still works and the caption reads the
  same.
