# Voice reference recording script

For Constantin, to clone his own voice for BrandGEO short-form video.
Written 2026-07-29.

Read the whole thing in **one continuous take**. Do not splice sections together
later: different room tones stitched into one file is the single most common
reason a clone comes out sounding artificial.

Target length is about **three minutes**. That covers both kinds of system, the
ones that want 6 to 30 seconds and the ones that want one to five minutes.

---

## Before you press record

**Room, and this matters more than the microphone.**

- Smallest carpeted room you have, or a bedroom with the wardrobe open. Soft
  surfaces absorb the reflections that make a clone sound boxy.
- Kitchen, bathroom, or anywhere with a hard floor and bare walls: no.
- Close the window. Turn off anything with a fan, including the laptop if you can
  record on your phone instead.

**Microphone.**

- A phone held 15 to 20 cm from your mouth, slightly off to the side so your
  breath does not hit it directly, is genuinely fine. Voice Memos on iPhone,
  Recorder on Android.
- **Do not use AirPods, earbuds, or a laptop's built-in mic.** All three apply
  aggressive noise suppression that strips exactly the detail a clone needs.
- If you have a USB mic, use it, same distance.

**Settings.**

- WAV or lossless if the app offers it. If it only does m4a, that is acceptable.
- Mono. 48kHz or 44.1kHz.
- **No noise reduction, no EQ, no compression, no "voice enhancement".** Cloning
  wants the raw signal. Every clean-up feature makes the result worse.

**Delivery, and this is the part that actually sets the tone of every video.**

The clone copies how you sound in this file. So read it the way you want the
videos to sound, not the way you would read a document out loud.

- **Like you are telling a colleague something you just found out.** Direct,
  a bit clipped, mildly interested. Not presenting. Not selling.
- Slightly slower than your normal speaking pace, and **hold that pace the whole
  way through**. Most people start energetic and drift flat by the end, and the
  clone then averages into something that sounds bored.
- Do not smile through it and do not push energy. BrandGEO sells measurement.
  An enthusiastic voice on a brand about not exaggerating reads wrong.
- Leave a real half-second pause at every full stop. Pauses are data.
- If you stumble, pause for two seconds, then repeat the whole sentence from the
  start. Do not stop the recording. I will cut the bad take out.

## SECTION 1. Level and warm-up

Not used in the clone. It sets your recording level and gets the first-sentence
stiffness out. Read it, then pause for two full seconds before Section 2.

> Testing, one two three. This is Constantin, recording a voice reference for
> BrandGEO on the twenty-ninth of July. The room is quiet and I am about twenty
> centimetres from the microphone.

*(pause two seconds)*

## SECTION 2. Sound coverage

This carries the awkward sounds English clones usually get wrong: the *zh* in
measure and vision, the two *th* sounds, the *ng* ending, and the full vowel
range. It is written out of real BrandGEO material so nothing here is filler.

> We measure visibility, not vision. The whole thing rests on one idea worth
> thinking through: an answer engine either knows your brand or it does not, and
> the third possibility, that it half knows you, is the one that costs the most.
>
> Three years ago the question was ranking. Today it is retrieval. That shift is
> not cosmetic. A page that ranks first and gets retrieved never is worth less
> than a page that ranks eighth and gets quoted every time.
>
> Ask it a question. Watch what comes back. Count how often you appear, where you
> appear, and who appears instead of you. Then do it again next month and see
> whether the number moved.
>
> Good, bad, huge, tough, caught, boat, book, boot, bird, bite, about, employ.
> Judge the usual measure of pleasure against a rough decision.

*(pause two seconds)*

Numbers are where synthetic voices most obviously break, and almost every
BrandGEO video is numbers. Read these exactly as written, at the same measured
pace, no rising excitement on the big ones.

The figures below are the illustrative sample-report figures from the homepage,
not client results. Nothing here is a claim.

## SECTION 5. Real delivery

This is the actual thing. Everything above is technical coverage; **this section
is where the clone learns your rhythm**, so it is the one to get right. If you
only re-record one section, re-record this one.

Read it as a single continuous piece, the way you would want to hear it under a
thirty-second video. Do not perform it. Just say it.

> Most people check whether they show up in ChatGPT and stop there.
>
> That is one engine. There are seven that matter, and they do not agree with
> each other. The same question, asked the same way, on the same day, gets you a
> different answer from each one, and a brand that leads on two of them can be
> completely absent from the other five.
>
> You would not know. Nothing tells you. There is no ranking report for this and
> no console to log into. The only way to find out is to ask all of them and
> count what comes back.
>
> So that is what we built.
>
> Twelve buyer questions, fired at every engine, every month. Whether you were
> mentioned. Where you sat in the answer. Who got named instead of you. And
> whether that changed since the last run.
>
> Grok and Google AI Overviews went live this week. Both are collecting now.
>
> If you have never checked, the free audit takes about a minute and it does not
> ask for a card. Get BrandGEO dot com.

*(pause two seconds, then stop the recording)*

is a genuinely different feel and a comparison you can actually answer.
