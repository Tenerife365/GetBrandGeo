# TikTok captions, ready to paste

Nine cuts, one per run folder. Written 2026-07-30. Nothing here has been posted
or scheduled.

## How to use this

**Upload the `-silent.mp4`. Every time.** It has zero audio streams, not a silent
audio track. Pick a track from TikTok's own in-app library after the upload.

That is not a licensing convenience, it is the reason the file exists. On TikTok,
in-app audio is a ranking input, so a self-supplied track competes with
distribution rather than helping it. A track picked in-app is also cleared for
that platform by definition, where an uncleared self-supplied one gets muted or
region-blocked, which is worse than silence.

**The `-scored.mp4` is for paid, site embeds and decks**, anywhere the in-app
library does not exist. Its picture is stream-copied from the silent master, so
the two files are byte-identical in the picture. Do not organic-post it.

The `-cover.png` in each folder is the literal frame 0 of the master. TikTok will
usually pick its own cover; the PNG is there so you can match it if you override.

**Paste the caption as-is.** Each is under 100 characters including hashtags, sized
so the bottom inset stays valid. A long TikTok caption pushes the text over the
video and is a formatting error, not a style choice.

**The ON-SCREEN TEXT lines are optional.** They are suggestions for a TikTok text
sticker you would add in-app, on top of the video. The videos already carry their
own burned-in text and read fine without one. If you use one, put it in the upper
third, never the lower, and drop it before the last two seconds so it does not
collide with the logo card.

**One caveat on file names.** `run-20260729-2200/tiktok/NOTES.md` calls the second
file `tiktok-bed.mp4`. The file actually on disk in that folder is
`tiktok-scored.mp4`, same as the other eight. Every path below is the real one.

## Voice, and where it came from

Calibrated against four published files: `docs/linkedin-posts-2026-07-24.md`,
`docs/linkedin-post-bg-018-2026-07-22.md`,
`docs/linkedin-teaser-posts-published-paper-2026-07-16.md`, and the company-post
section of the first. Four things carried over, then compressed to TikTok length.

**1. The strongest line is an admission, not a claim.** "We built ours to refuse."
"The last two pieces of research we published were both about mistakes in our own
product." "including a real collection failure on our fifth engine that we disclosed
rather than quietly dropped." BrandGEO persuades by conceding, which is why caption 6
gives the free method away and caption 4 concedes the tool you own does something real.

**2. Numbers arrive naked, with the denominator and the date attached, never dressed
as a superlative.** "24% of all Perplexity citations in January 2026." "8
commercial-buyer prompts across up to 5 engines, collected 2026-07-10." "156-assertion
regression suite." Every post ends in a Source line. Where the count is unproven the
copy returns null rather than inventing one. So no caption here carries a figure at
all: the videos already hold the checkable ones, and a naked number in a caption has
nowhere to put its denominator.

**3. The payload is a reframe. The reader is told they are asking the wrong
question.** "not 'is AI visibility important,' but 'has my category already converged
on an answer.'" "sentence position and list rank are not the same unit of
measurement." Captions 3 and 4 are direct descendants of those two sentences.

**4. Short declaratives, plain verbs, no ornament, and the turn lands in five to nine
words.** "It shouldn't have." "Same city, same research method, same day."
"Marketing copy is written to persuade. Reddit is written to actually answer the
question." Questions never open a post; where one appears it sits at the very end as
the thing to go check. Every caption above is two sentences of that shape, which is
what makes them survivable at TikTok length: the LinkedIn posts get three paragraphs
to arrive at the turn, these get one clause.

## Verification

Two scanners, both run on the caption strings that will actually be pasted, both
negative-controlled. A scan that passes everything is indistinguishable from one that
never ran, so each was made to fire before it was believed.

**Scanner 1, compliance.** Dashes (em, en, figure, minus, horizontal bar, and the two
Unicode hyphens), banned vocabulary, superlatives, universals that quantify over
people, engines outside the Growth five, pricing and plan names, rhetorical-question
openers, the 100 character ceiling, and the 2-to-4 hashtag rule. Measured-subject
matching normalises both sides NFKD, strips combining marks, then case-folds, in that
order, because case-folding first lets an upper-cased accented form through.

Result: **9 of 9 captions clean, 9 of 9 overlay suggestions clean, exit 0.**

Negative control: **30 of 30 injections fired.** Four dash characters, three banned
words, five superlatives, four universals, four retired or off-plan engines, two
pricing forms, a rhetorical opener, an over-length caption, a one-hashtag and a
five-hashtag caption, and a measured subject injected in four spellings, including
`Engel & Völkers`, `Engel & Volkers` and `ENGEL & VÖLKERS`. All three name variants
were caught, which is the specific failure the campaign hit on a raw-byte matcher.
Captions were then restored and re-run: 9 of 9 clean.

**Scanner 2, reuse.** Each caption diffed against the drawn text of its own cut at an
n-gram floor of 2, with a closed-class function-word exemption, so a caption that
restates the video fails.

Result: **9 of 9 clean, exit 0.** Negative control: a verbatim on-screen line injected
into each of the nine captions, **9 of 9 fired.**

**This one caught two real defects rather than confirming a clean bill.** The first
draft for run-20260729-2318 read "A default is not won once. It repeats itself.",
which shares `a default` with the screen and restates `AND A DEFAULT GETS REPEATED.`
outright. The first draft for run-20260730-0013 read "The city was the wrong variable.
Check your category.", which shares `the city` with `SO IT IS NOT THE CITY.` and
restates the video's own conclusion. Both were rewritten to extend instead, and the
diff was re-run.

## Cuts whose on-screen claim could not be closed from NOTES alone

Three flags. None of them is a reason to hold a video, and none of them touches a
caption above, because no caption carries a figure. They are recorded because the
NOTES were the only source available here and in these three places the NOTES do not
settle the question.

**1. `run-20260730-0013` puts a superlative on screen.** Scene 15 reads `THE MOST /
FRAGMENTED / CATEGORY WE / MEASURED.` Its NOTES traces the wording to
`ai-visibility-for-denver.html`, which is a trace to a page, not a check against the
corpus. The campaign's own rule, added later the same day, is that a superlative is a
claim about every other page in the corpus and therefore cannot be verified by reading
the page asserting it. That corpus grep was never run for this cut. It may well be
true; it is not established by anything in the folder.

**2. `run-20260730-0113`'s NOTES rests on a claim its successor disproved.** Restraint
3 in that file describes Chicago's 5/5 as "the first and so far only fully unanimous
result in the whole program." `run-20260730-0216`'s NOTES then grepped the corpus and
found thirteen city pages carrying a 5/5, with Rome collected two weeks before
Chicago, so the claim was false on the day it was published. **The rendered video is
not affected**, because run 4 deliberately kept the superlative off screen and shows
only the labelled `5 / 5` for one category in one city, which stands. Only the
reasoning in the NOTES is stale.

**3. `run-20260730-0113` scenes 8 and 9 source counts from `bg-004.html`, which the
brief excludes for engine counts.** The cut shows `TOP AI RECOMMENDATION ON 3 ENGINES`
and `TIMES NAMED ON A FOURTH ENGINE 0` with no denominator anywhere, which is the
compliant reading and is argued at length in its NOTES. It is still the closest call
in the set, because that page's own methodology paragraph and its body name two
different engine lineups, one containing a retired engine and one containing an engine
on no purchasable plan. The NOTES flags the page as needing a fix at source. Until it
is fixed, any future asset reaching for that page will hit the same problem.

**Two smaller notes, both already disclosed in the relevant NOTES and neither a
blocker.** `run-20260729-2200`'s closing card uses `CANNOT SEE`, and `cannot` was added
to the banned-universals list after that cut shipped; a fair read of the loss-aversion
A-B against `run-20260730-0413` should know the two cuts are not held to an identical
standard on that one line. And `run-20260730-0613` draws its Paris figures from a run
where one engine's collection failed and a retired engine made up the fourth, which is
exactly why it shows `3 ANSWERS` and `NONE` as raw counts with no denominator and no
engine count anywhere near them.
