## Read this before reusing any line in this file

**All four cities come from ONE collection, 2026-07-10, on FOUR engines:
Claude, Gemini, Meta AI, Perplexity.**

That single sentence constrains every caption here, and the constraints are not
stylistic. They are what makes the copy true.

1. **Date the finding, do not imply it is current.** Every caption carries
   `10 July 2026` in its own language and says the data was collected, past
   tense. A reader who wants to check the claim needs the date to check it
   against. Dating it is the strength, not the hedge.
2. **Meta AI ran that day and was retired from BrandGEO's lineup on 2026-07-16.**
   Where a caption names it, the retirement is stated in the same breath. The
   retirement is a fact about our engine set, not about Meta AI.
3. **ChatGPT was not in this run.** An account-level API quota failure excluded
   it. Where a caption mentions it at all, it mentions it only as an excluded
   engine and only alongside the reason. Never write ChatGPT into the lineup
   that ran, and never let a reader infer it was one of the four.
4. **Never claim five engines, and never claim today's lineup for this data.**
   The engine count on screen and in copy is four, bound to 2026-07-10. Growth's
   current five-engine set has nothing to do with a July collection.
5. **Rome's "5 of 5" is not engine consensus and must never be written as one.**
   It counts five RESTAURANTS, from ONE engine, across TWO languages. Only four
   engines ran, so a five-engine agreement is not arithmetically available on
   this data at all. Both Rome captions state the denominators explicitly.
6. **No superlative about the corpus.** Madrid's page and Paris's page each
   assert a program-wide maximum from the same collection day. Madrid claims
   "the strongest consensus of any category, any city, in this program so far";
   Paris claims "the cleanest unanimous sweep in this research program so far".
   Two program-wide maxima from one day are mutually exclusive. Both are filed
   as defects. **Neither is reused here, in any language, in any form.** Rome's
   three superlatives and Berlin's are refused on the same grounds.
7. **No measured subject is named.** Not one hotel, restaurant, bank, agency,
   clinic, law firm or software product that appeared inside a result set. The
   engine may be named. What the engine named may not be.
8. **Every figure in every caption traces to a measurement on that city's own
   page**, under `brandgeo/web/ai-visibility-for-<city>.html`. The source line
   is recorded under each caption.

**Dashes.** The five banned codepoints (em U+2014, en U+2013, figure U+2012,
minus U+2212, horizontal bar U+2015) appear nowhere in this file, in any
language. Commas and full stops carry the work. ASCII hyphen U+002D is fine and
is used inside hyphenated words.

**Accented characters are expected and correct.** German, Spanish and Italian
captions carry their diacritics. Only the dash codepoints are banned.

### The matcher folds, it does not compare raw bytes

A raw-byte name matcher fails silently on diacritics, which is how a name
written without its accent walks through a corpus undetected. Both sides of
every comparison are folded, in this order:

```
apostrophe variants to ASCII    U+2019 U+2018 U+02BC U+00B4 ` U+201B U+FF07
NFKD
strip combining marks
casefold                        (this also maps the German eszett to ss)
French oe and ae ligatures expanded
collapse ae / oe / ue to a / o / u
keep [a-z0-9] and single spaces
```

**Case-folding is last on purpose.** Folding first leaves some precomposed
accented capitals intact. The `ae/oe/ue` collapse is the step NFKD cannot do,
because those are separate letters rather than decomposed forms; it is what
unifies the German transliteration of a name with its umlauted spelling.

Matching is then done two ways: as a token sequence, which avoids the substring
false positives a short name would otherwise produce, and as a concatenated
substring with an 8 character floor, which catches a name written with its
spaces removed.

### Three bugs the controls found, which is why they exist

1. **The corpus harvester was pulling `ChatGPT` out of a result column** and
   failing all six captions on it. An engine is the instrument, not a measured
   subject, and naming it is required by four of these captions. Engine names
   are now excluded from the corpus.
2. **The harvester was also pulling the prose fragment `both languages`** out of
   a Pattern column, which failed two clean English captions. Descriptive
   fragments are now filtered by WHOLE WORD, never by substring: the Rome build
   hit the opposite bug, where a substring filter rejected a genuine restaurant
   name because it contained the letters `set`.
3. **The five-engine check fired on copy that refutes the five-engine
   misreading.** Both Rome captions exist to say it is *not* five engines
   agreeing, and the Rome cut itself draws that negation on screen. A blunt
   "this phrase must not appear" rule fails the exact copy doing the right
   thing, so the check now looks at the three tokens before the phrase and only
   fails an ASSERTION.

   **That exemption was then controlled in its own right**, and it was broken:
   the Italian copula `sono` had been listed as a negator, so
   `Sono cinque motori d'accordo` would have been waved through as if negated.
   Found by stripping only the negator, leaving the phrase, and requiring a
   fire. Both languages now fire when the negator is removed and stay silent
   when it is present.
