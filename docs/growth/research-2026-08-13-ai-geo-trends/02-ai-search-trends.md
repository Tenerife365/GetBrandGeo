# AI search trends research — how AI search is actually changing (2026)

Research date: 2026-08-13. Scope: platform behavior, traffic/behavior data, citation-correlation research, expert commentary, and concrete documented examples. Competitor/product landscape is covered by a separate research track and is intentionally excluded here.

A general note on source quality before the findings: most content marketing published under "GEO statistics 2026" headlines is written by GEO/AEO tool vendors with no disclosed sample size, date range, or engine list — often citing each other in circles. Where a claim below traces to one of those, it is labeled **undisclosed methodology** and should be treated as a directional signal, not a fact to publish as-is. Where a claim traces to a named study with a stated sample size and date range (Ahrefs, Muck Rack, Seer Interactive, Adobe, Similarweb, Search Engine Journal/Victorious, the BBC/EBU, SparkToro, academic papers), it is labeled **disclosed methodology**. This distinction matters for BrandGEO's own content credibility and should be preserved in any article drawing on this file.

## 1. Recent platform changes (2026)

### Google: AI Overviews and AI Mode

- **May 6-7, 2026: five structural changes to AI Overviews and AI Mode**, described by Google as one of the biggest updates since AI Overviews launched. Inline citations now sit next to the specific sentence they support (not bundled at the end), hover previews show the site name on desktop, a new "Expert Advice" block pulls first-hand perspectives from forums, social media, and review sites, and a new end-of-answer section surfaces curated links to articles and case studies. Rolled out first to English-language US searches, with other markets following. [Deeper Insights, 2026](https://deeperinsights.com/news/google-updates-to-ai-mode-and-ai-overviews/), [Press Gazette, 2026](https://pressgazette.co.uk/platforms/what-google-ai-mode-push-means-for-publishers/)
- **May 19, 2026, Google I/O:** AI Mode passed **1 billion monthly users** roughly one year after its May 2025 US debut, with query volume more than doubling every quarter. Gemini 3.5 Flash became the default model powering AI Mode globally. Google also shipped an "Intelligent Search Box" (multimodal input — text, images, files, video, Chrome tabs), "Search Agents" that monitor the web and news 24/7 and push synthesized updates (summer 2026, Google AI Pro/Ultra subscribers), and agentic booking with direct-calling capability for categories like home repair and beauty, US rollout summer 2026. [Google, I/O 2026 announcement, 2026-05-19](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/search-io-2026/)
- **Agentic shopping / Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP):** co-developed by Google and Shopify, announced January 2026, an open standard letting AI agents complete checkout (product selection, payment, confirmation) on a merchant's behalf without a human navigating the site. US-first; expanding to Canada and Australia as of mid-2026, UK to follow. Walmart, Target, Visa, Mastercard, Amex, and Stripe have endorsed it. [Google Shopping blog, 2026](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/agentic-commerce-ai-tools-protocol-retailers-platforms/)
- **CTR impact:** click-through rate on the #1 organic position drops **58%** on queries that trigger an AI Overview, per Similarweb-based tracking — up from a measured -34.5% in April 2025, indicating the effect is growing as the feature matures, not stabilizing. [Keywords Everywhere AI Overviews tracker, 2026](https://keywordseverywhere.com/news/ai-overviews/), [seo-kreativ.de, 2026](https://www.seo-kreativ.de/en/blog/google-ai-overviews-updates-2026-en/)

### Gemini API / Grounding with Google Search

- **January 5, 2026:** Google changed grounding billing from a flat $35/1,000 prompts to usage-based **$14 per 1,000 search queries** — and a single prompt can trigger multiple billable searches if the model decides to issue more than one. [Google AI for Developers changelog, 2026](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/changelog)
- Grounding with Google Search can now be combined with Structured Outputs (JSON-precise extraction from live web results) and with Grounding with Google Maps, for Gemini 3 model family agents. A new "Grounding with Google Image Search" type is in preview for Gemini 3.1 Flash Image. [Google Developers Blog, 2026](https://developers.googleblog.com/new-gemini-api-updates-for-gemini-3/)

### ChatGPT / OpenAI

- Citation placement is **binary, not ranked** — unlike ten blue links, there is no "position 3" in a ChatGPT answer; a brand is either cited in a given response or it isn't. [Darkroom, 2026](https://www.darkroomagency.com/observatory/how-to-rank-on-chatgpt) — this framing recurs across multiple independent GEO-vendor write-ups and matches how the product visibly works, but note none of the underlying "40% weighting" style numbers attached to it (FAQ schema, inline citations) trace to an OpenAI disclosure; treat those specific multipliers as **undisclosed methodology**.
- **OpenAI does not publish how often ChatGPT triggers a live web search.** A same-week (August 2026) review of the public data explicitly states "we don't know for sure" and that all commonly cited figures (20-35% of prompts, "34.5% down from 46% in late 2024," etc.) are third-party estimates built on modeling assumptions about total daily prompt volume, not an OpenAI-published number. Treat every specific ChatGPT-search-trigger-rate percentage in the wild as an estimate, not a fact. [Otterly.ai, 2026-08-04](https://otterly.ai/blog/how-often-does-chatgpt-trigger-a-web-search/)

### Perplexity

- Perplexity's own claimed method: searches an index of **over 200 billion URLs** per query, runs a three-layer reranking pipeline, then embeds inline citations tying each claim to its source. Draws from multiple search APIs (Google, Bing) and retrieves/reads candidate pages directly; new content can reportedly be cited within hours of being indexed. Averages **21.87 citations per response** — the highest citation density of any major AI platform in the comparison used. [Leapd, 2026](https://www.leapd.ai/blog/ai-visibility/how-chatgpt-google-ai-overviews-and-perplexity-source-information-in-2026) — **undisclosed methodology** for the exact figures (vendor blog, no stated sample), but the retrieval architecture description is broadly consistent with how Perplexity has documented itself elsewhere.
- Perplexity leans hardest on freshness of any major engine: in the Seer Interactive study below, only 65% of Perplexity's citations were updated within the last year, the lowest of the three engines tested, but a separate estimate put 50% of Perplexity citations at under 13 weeks old — the two don't obviously reconcile, which is itself a sign that "freshness weighting" figures vary a lot by study design and vertical mix.

### Claude / Anthropic

- Claude's web search is a **Server Tool run by Anthropic in the cloud** — Anthropic does not connect to Google/Bing APIs itself; when Claude uses web search, results are reported to come from **Brave Search**. Every response using the tool includes citations by default. The `web_search_20260209` tool version (Claude 4.6+) adds "dynamic filtering," where Claude can write code to filter search results before they enter context, rather than dumping raw results in. [Claude Platform Docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/web-search-tool), [Apiyi.com, 2026](https://help.apiyi.com/en/claude-api-web-search-guide-en.html)
- Muck Rack's 25-million-link study (below) put Claude's citation rate at **55%** of responses, well below ChatGPT's 96% and Gemini's 82% — Claude cites far less often when it does answer.

### Copilot / Microsoft

- **Copilot Checkout** launched in the US on Copilot.com, January 8, 2026 — in-chat purchasing without redirecting to a merchant site, across Copilot, Bing, MSN, and Edge, with PayPal, Shopify, Stripe, and Etsy as launch partners. [GeekWire, 2026](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/microsoft-launches-copilot-checkout-joining-the-ai-shopping-race-against-amazon-google-and-openai/)
- **Brand Agents**, rolling out for Shopify merchants: an AI chat trained on a brand's own product catalog that answers product questions and engages shoppers "in natural, brand-aligned conversations" inside the merchant's own storefront context. [Search Engine Land, 2026](https://searchengineland.com/microsoft-launches-copilot-checkout-and-brand-agents-467175)
- A Copilot Studio "personalized shopping agent" template and a "catalog enrichment agent" template (extracts product attributes from images, auto-categorizes) both shipped for enterprise builders in 2026. [Microsoft Copilot Blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-copilot/blog/copilot-studio/new-and-improved-agent-governance-intelligent-workflows-and-connected-app-experiences/)

## 2. Traffic and behavior data

### Zero-click search

- **Ahrefs/Semrush-adjacent estimate, 2026:** zero-click share of Google searches has climbed to roughly **67.1%**, up from a 49% benchmark in 2019 — cited across multiple 2026 aggregator posts but the primary attribution chain back to Ahrefs/Semrush's own named report wasn't independently confirmable in this pass; treat as **directionally consistent but not independently pinned down**.
- **SparkToro (Rand Fishkin), disclosed methodology, published 2026-06-09 (updated 2026-06-18):** using Similarweb's desktop+mobile web panel for January-April 2026 (US only, excluding Google's mobile app, so likely an undercount), **68.01% of Google searches ended without a click** in the first four months of 2026, versus 60.45% in 2024 and 49% in 2019. AI Mode itself accounted for only **0.34%** of total search volume in that window — small in absolute share even as it drives outsized zero-click behavior. AI Overviews appeared on 20%+ of all searches and cut click-through by nearly 60%. Overall traffic share to websites fell about 22% (8 percentage points) between June 2025 and May 2026. [SparkToro, 2026](https://sparktoro.com/blog/in-2026-less-than-one-third-of-google-searches-still-send-a-click/)
- Query-intent split (source chain traces to a Semrush-attributed "Intent Study" repeated across aggregator posts, **not independently verified in this pass**): 74% of informational searches end without a click vs. 31% of transactional searches.
- Mobile zero-click rate is closing the gap with desktop: one 2026 aggregator citing a "Semrush Mobile Search Behavior Study" put mobile at 29.4% and desktop at 34.1% (up 12.1 points on mobile since 2022) — **could not independently verify this specific Semrush report in this pass**, flagging it as a claim to re-check before publishing a specific number.

### AI referral traffic (disclosed methodology — Adobe Analytics)

- **Adobe Digital Insights, based on 1 trillion+ visits to US retail sites**, reported (as of reporting in June 2026): AI-referred traffic to retail sites grew **138% year over year** in May 2026, and is up **1,324%** (14x+) since Adobe started tracking the category in October 2024. Visitors arriving via an AI referral spend **53% more time** on site and view **23% more pages** per visit than other visitors. Adobe separately reported AI-sourced traffic converting **54% better** than non-AI traffic, and shoppers **15% more engaged**. [Digital Commerce 360, 2026-06-17](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/06/17/adobe-ai-referred-traffic-to-retail-sites-doubles-in-a-year/), [Adobe Business Blog](https://business.adobe.com/blog/ai-traffic-surge-retail-sites-not-machine-readable)
- Adobe also reported AI-driven traffic to **travel sites up roughly 200%** in a separate vertical breakout — travel and retail both show AI referral becoming a real, not marginal, channel. [Adobe Business Blog, 2026](https://business.adobe.com/blog/adobe-report-ai-traffic-travel-sites-surges-200-percent)

### Similarweb: which engine sends the traffic

- ChatGPT's worldwide web-traffic referral share fell from roughly 76% (June 2025) to about 53% (May 2026), even as its total visit count held roughly flat — meaning the referral pie is being split across more engines, not that ChatGPT itself is shrinking. Homepage-only referrals (vs. deep links) from ChatGPT rose from ~26-29% of its referral traffic to ~62-63% over the same window, a pattern worth noting for content teams: an increasing share of ChatGPT-driven visits land on a homepage, not the specific cited page. [Similarweb blog, referenced 2026](https://www.similarweb.com/blog/marketing/geo/gen-ai-stats/)

### Publisher-side traffic collapse (disclosed, named outlets)

- Search referral traffic down **60% for small publishers, 47% for mid-sized, 22% for large publishers** per Chartbeat-sourced reporting picked up across multiple outlets in 2026. [ALM Corp summary of Chartbeat data, 2026](https://almcorp.com/blog/search-traffic-decline-small-publishers-chartbeat-data/)
- **The Planet D** (travel blog): lost half its traffic after Google's May 2024 AI Overviews launch, then lost another 90% on top of that, and ceased publication in 2026. **Stereogum** (music blog): lost 70% of ad revenue in 2026, with its founder attributing the largest share of the blame to AI Overviews. [AdExchanger, 2026](https://www.adexchanger.com/publishers/the-ai-search-reckoning-is-dismantling-open-web-traffic-and-publishers-may-never-recover/)
- **Reuters Institute (RISJ) report:** news publishers expect search referral traffic to fall by more than **40% over the next three years** (by 2029). [Search Engine Land, 2026](https://searchengineland.com/news-publishers-search-referrals-drop-report-467408), [Oxford RISJ/DPIR](https://www.politics.ox.ac.uk/news/news-publishers-expect-search-traffic-fall-more-40-next-three-years-new-risj-report-finds)
- Axios reported some publishers are now weighing whether to opt out of Google Search crawling entirely, given the traffic collapse without a comparable AI-referral offset for most content categories. [Axios, 2026-07-31](https://www.axios.com/2026/07/31/google-search-publishers-seo-geo-llms-ai)

### Consumers starting research in an AI chat vs. a search engine

- **General consumer, undisclosed-methodology aggregator claim:** "37% of consumers now start their searches with AI tools like ChatGPT instead of Google," and 85% of AI users still cross-check answers via traditional search before converting. Original attribution given as "Eight Oh Two's 2026 AI and Search Behavior study" — **could not independently verify the primary source or its sample size in this pass; flag before quoting a hard 37% figure.** [WhatConverts / Search Engine Land coverage, 2026](https://searchengineland.com/consumers-start-searches-ai-not-google-study-467159)
- **B2B software buyers specifically (G2's 2026 AI Search Insight Report — disclosed as a named vendor report, methodology not fully verified in this pass):** 51% of B2B software buyers now start research inside an AI chatbot instead of a search engine, up from 29% in April 2025; 71% say they rely on AI chatbots for software research, up from ~60% roughly seven months earlier. [Demand Gen Report coverage of G2, 2026](https://www.demandgenreport.com/industry-news/news-brief/half-of-b2b-software-buyers-now-start-their-research-with-ai-chatbots-g2/52737/), [G2 report landing page](https://learn.g2.com/g2-2026-ai-search-insight-report)
- **Generational split:** a Fractl/Search Engine Land survey of 2,000+ consumers found 66% of 18-24 year-olds use ChatGPT to find information. [thatsnoble.com summary, 2026](https://www.thatsnoble.com/resources/66-of-gen-z-uses-chatgpt-to-search-if-youre-not-in-the-answer-youre-not-on-the-shortlist)
- **EMARKETER forecast:** roughly **31.3%** of the US population will use generative AI search in 2026. [Referenced via Search Engine Journal AEO/GEO 2026 report coverage](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/aeo-and-geo-in-2026/563856/)

## 3. What actually correlates with being cited

### Disclosed-methodology studies (the ones worth building content around)

**Ahrefs — 75,000 brands, ChatGPT + Google AI Mode + Google AI Overviews (published 2025-12-12, author Louise Linehan, Ahrefs Brand Radar data, Spearman correlation):**

| Signal | ChatGPT | AI Mode | AI Overviews |
|---|---|---|---|
| YouTube mentions | 0.737 | 0.740 | 0.712 |
| Branded web mentions | 0.664 | 0.709 | 0.656 |
| Branded anchor text | 0.511 | 0.628 | 0.527 |
| Branded search volume | 0.352 | 0.466 | 0.392 |
| Domain Rating | 0.266 | 0.285 | 0.326 |
| Site pages / content volume | ~0.194 | — | — |
| Raw backlink count | weak across all three engines | | |

Ahrefs is explicit that this is correlation, not causation, and that the three engines show high cross-platform agreement (0.749-0.821) in *which* brands they mention, even though the underlying retrieval mechanics differ. Selection criteria: domains with Domain Rating > 40 and top keyword averaging ≥800 monthly searches. [Ahrefs, 2025-12-12](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-brand-visibility-correlations/)

**Search Engine Journal / Victorious — 175 brands, 5 verticals (legal, healthcare, SaaS, financial services, ecommerce), 8 platforms (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode, Meta AI), published 2026-07-29, author Michael Transon:**

- **96% of brands were described accurately** when an AI platform was asked directly about them, but **89% of brands never appeared** in AI-generated answers to category-research prompts ("best X for Y") — recognition and citation are two different things, and the gap is large.
- Of 49,391 citations logged, **99.99% pointed to third-party domains**, not the brand's own site. Only 4 of 150 tracked brands earned any self-citation in category-research answers.
- Brands with fewer than 2,000 indexed web pages mentioning them (third-party) appeared in AI category answers only 3% of the time.
- Correlation with AI mention rate: referring domains 0.49, third-party web mentions 0.45 — moderate, not strong, and notably lower than Ahrefs' branded-web-mentions figure above (different study, different cohort, worth noting as a discrepancy rather than reconciling it).
- Platform variance in *recognition* accuracy: Google AI Mode, Gemini, ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, and Copilot all exceeded 83% accuracy across verticals; Perplexity recognized fewer than 55% of SaaS/ecommerce brands correctly; Meta AI recognized just 46% of SaaS brands. [Search Engine Journal, 2026-07-29](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-brand-mention-study-victorious-spa/582765/)

**Muck Rack "Generative Pulse" — 25 million+ links, ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini, 17 industries, three editions since July 2025, most recent May 2026:**

- **Earned media accounts for 84% of all AI citations**, stable across three editions (range 82-89% since July 2025). Journalism alone is 25-27% of citations. Paid/advertorial content is essentially never cited (0.3%).
- Citation frequency by engine: **ChatGPT cites sources in 96% of responses**, Gemini 82%, Claude 55%. [GlobeNewswire release / Muck Rack blog, May 2026](https://muckrack.com/blog/what-is-ai-reading-may-2026)

**Princeton/Georgia Tech/Allen Institute/IIT Delhi "GEO" paper — the academic foundation of the category (arXiv 2023-11-16, published KDD 2024, 9,106+ downloads, 76+ citations as of March 2026):**

This is the closest thing GEO has to a peer-reviewed foundational study, but it predates 2026 and its benchmark (GEO-bench, a synthetic query set with black-box optimization) does not necessarily generalize to how today's production ChatGPT/Gemini/Perplexity actually retrieve — worth citing as "the paper that started the field," not as current-state evidence.

- Nine content techniques tested; five produced real, statistically distinguishable gains on their Position-Adjusted Word Count and Subjective Impression metrics: **Statistics Addition** (largest single lift, cited at up to +41%), **Quotation Addition** (~43% on one metric), **Cite Sources** (~28-30%, and specifically a 115% visibility jump for a page that started at position 5 — the technique helped lower-ranked pages more than top-ranked ones), **Fluency Optimization** (~29%), and **Authoritative Voice**.
- Four techniques did nothing or hurt: **Keyword Stuffing, Easy-to-Understand simplification, Content Padding, and Pure Persuasive Language** — worth flagging because "just make it easier to read" and "add more keywords" are exactly the two most common pieces of bad GEO advice still circulating. [arXiv:2311.09735](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.09735)

**Seer Interactive — content freshness, 7,683 pages / 47,097 citations, ChatGPT + Gemini + Perplexity, 4 verticals (pet retail, vacation rentals, energy, banking), March-June 2026, published 2026-07-24, author Sonny Vasquez:**

- 75% of cited pages were updated within the last year (88% within two years); content older than three years barely gets cited at all.
- Engine differences in freshness weighting: **Gemini 78%** of citations updated within a year, **ChatGPT 73%**, **Perplexity only 65%** — the opposite of Perplexity's reputation as the "most real-time" engine, at least on this metric and this sample.
- The more interesting finding: **72% of citations "looked fresh" by last-modified date, but only 42% of those pages were originally published within the past year** — meaning most of the freshness signal LLMs reward is coming from edits to older pages, not genuinely new content. Pages cited consistently across all four months averaged 68% freshness (median age 6 months); one-off spike citations were 86% fresh (median 2 months) — sustained citation correlates with moderately fresh content, not maximally fresh content. [Seer Interactive, 2026-07-24](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/study-content-recencys-impact-on-ai-visibility-in-2026)

### Where vendor marketing claims outrun disclosed methodology

- "FAQ schema and inline citations weighted approximately 40% higher in ChatGPT source selection" recurs across multiple GEO-vendor blogs (Darkroom, Kime.ai, Wellows, Ai Boost) with no named study, no sample size, and no stated date range behind the specific 40% figure. **Do not present this as fact** — present it as "widely repeated in the GEO industry, not independently verified."
- Claims like "76.4% of ChatGPT's most-cited pages come from domains with strong off-page presence" and "ChatGPT relies on RAG using Bing Search API for 92% of queries" trace to SEO-tool-vendor blogs with no disclosed methodology and should not be cited as fact in BrandGEO content without independent verification.

### The Ahrefs schema-markup study — a genuine myth-bust, disclosed methodology

Ahrefs tracked **1,885 pages that added JSON-LD schema** between August 2025 and March 2026, each matched against three control URLs from different domains with similar existing citation levels that did *not* add schema, and measured citation-count changes across Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode, and ChatGPT.

**Result: adding schema produced no statistically significant citation increase on any platform.** AI Overviews citations actually fell 4.6% (statistically significant in the wrong direction); AI Mode was up 2.4% and ChatGPT up 2.2%, neither statistically significant. Separately, in a 6-million-URL pass, pages cited by AI were nearly 3x more likely to already carry JSON-LD than uncited pages — but Ahrefs' own conclusion is that this is confounded: sites that bother with schema also tend to invest in technical SEO, authoritative content, and link building, so schema is a *marker* of a well-run site, not a *cause* of citation. [seroundtable.com summary of the Ahrefs study, 2026](https://www.seroundtable.com/study-schema-citations-study-41311.html)

This is a directly useful, credible myth-bust for BrandGEO content: it contradicts a claim ("add schema, get cited") repeated constantly across the undisclosed-methodology vendor posts listed above, using an actual matched-control design.

## 4. Industry expert commentary and predictions

- **Lily Ray** (VP of SEO Strategy and Research, Amsive; launched her own AI-search consultancy "Algorythmic" in 2026): ran a "gullibility experiment" showing Google AI Overviews, AI Mode, Gemini, and ChatGPT all incorporated a fabricated ranking claim into their answers within 24 hours of it appearing on the web — a live demonstration that these systems can absorb and repeat unverified claims fast, which is directly relevant to BrandGEO's pitch (both the risk of bad claims about a brand spreading, and the opportunity of legitimate claims spreading the same way). She has separately written that a GEO-first strategy pursued carelessly "might be destroying your SEO," arguing the two disciplines are not always additive. [tryprofound.com 2026 GEO expert list](https://www.tryprofound.com/articles/top-experts-in-generative-engine-optimization), [Lily Ray Substack](https://lilyraynyc.substack.com/p/your-geo-strategy-might-be-destroying)
- **Glenn Gabe:** known for being among the first to document, with Search Console data, exactly how a new Google AI feature shows up for real sites — positioned as the go-to source for "what actually happened to my traffic" forensic analysis rather than forward-looking prediction.
- **Mike King:** developed "Relevance Engineering," a systems-level framework treating AEO/GEO as the intersection of information retrieval, content strategy, UX, embeddings, digital PR, and measurement — explicitly pushing back on GEO being reduced to "a set of copy edits."
- **Search Engine Journal / Conductor "State of AEO & GEO in 2026" survey (named report, methodology partially disclosed as a CMO/marketing-leader survey):** 97% of marketing leaders reported a positive impact from AEO investment in 2025, and 94% plan to increase AEO/GEO investment in 2026; 56% already reported "high or significant" GEO investment in 2025. Contributors quoted include Aleyda Solís (Orainti), Eli Schwartz (author, "Product-Led SEO"), and Conductor's own research leads. [Search Engine Journal, 2026](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/aeo-and-geo-in-2026/563856/), [Conductor report landing page](https://www.conductor.com/academy/state-of-aeo-geo-report/)
- **Recurring 2026 theme across multiple practitioners:** "SEO is splitting in two" — one track optimizing for AI-mediated answers (entities, structure, being quotable) and one track still optimizing for the shrinking pool of traditional blue-link clicks, with the claim that keyword-level optimization matters far less than entity recognition and structural clarity now. This is a widely repeated framing rather than a single named source; treat as consensus sentiment, not a citable statistic.

## 5. Concrete, checkable examples

These are documented, named, independently reportable incidents — useful as grounded examples in blog posts rather than abstract "AI can be wrong" claims.

1. **Wolf River Electric v. Google (filed March 2026, Minnesota):** a solar installer sued Google after AI Overviews falsely stated the company was a defendant in a Minnesota Attorney General lawsuit over deceptive lending practices (a real lawsuit existed, against four *other* companies — Wolf River was not one of them). The complaint says Google's AI Overview cited real sources that did not actually contain the claim it attributed to them. A named customer canceled a $150,000 contract after seeing the false claim; the company alleges $24.7 million in 2024 damages alone and is seeking $110-210 million in total damages. Google moved the case to federal court; as of a January 2026 ruling, Google had missed a filing deadline in the case. [Star Tribune, 2026](https://www.startribune.com/google-ai-overview-lawsuit-defamation-great-river-electric/601371780), [Reason/Volokh Conspiracy, 2025-06-11 and 2026-01-12](https://reason.com/volokh/2026/01/12/google-missed-key-deadline-in-suit-alleging-googles-ai-libeled-business-court-holds/)
2. **Ashley MacIsaac v. Google (filed Ontario Superior Court, May 2026):** the Canadian musician alleges an AI Overview falsely identified him as a sex offender, which led to a scheduled concert being canceled. [Tech Times, 2026-06-16](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/318489/20260616/google-ai-overviews-defamation-ruling-ends-search-safe-harbor-every-ai-answer-engine.htm)
3. **BBC / European Broadcasting Union news-accuracy studies:** the BBC's own February 2025 study found 51% of AI answers to news questions had significant issues, 19% of answers citing BBC content introduced factual errors, and 13% of quotes attributed to BBC articles were altered or fabricated. A larger October 2025 follow-up across the BBC + EBU (more languages, more outlets) found the "significant issues" rate had improved slightly to 45%, with 31% of responses showing sourcing deficiencies (missing/incorrect attribution) and 20% showing major accuracy issues (outdated info, hallucination). This is a directly relevant, repeatable example of "being cited is not the same as being cited correctly." [infoDocket/Library Journal summary, 2025-10-22](https://www.infodocket.com/2025/10/22/bbc-largest-study-of-its-kind-shows-ai-assistants-misrepresent-news-content-45-of-the-time-regardless-of-language-or-territory/)
4. **The Planet D and Stereogum** (§2 above) — named, independently reported publisher casualties of the AI Overviews shift, useful as "this is not hypothetical" anchors.
5. **Reddit's Google/OpenAI data-licensing deals** ($60M/year to Google since Feb 2024, ~$70M/year to OpenAI since roughly mid-2024) are both up for renewal in 2026, and Reddit's own stock dropped on reporting (July 2026) that it may not renew the Google deal, given that Google's AI answers are simultaneously cutting into the referral traffic those licensing payments were supposed to offset. Wells Fargo estimated renegotiated combined value could reach ~$550M/year. This is a good concrete example of the tension between "your content trains/feeds the AI answer" and "the AI answer replaces the click you'd have gotten." [CNBC, 2026-07-22](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/22/reddit-stock-google-ai-content-deal.html)

## 6. Things that are still genuinely unknown or disputed

- **OpenAI does not disclose how often ChatGPT triggers a web search.** Every specific percentage in circulation (20%, 31%, 34.5%, 35%) is a third-party estimate built on assumed total prompt volume, not an OpenAI-published figure. Any BrandGEO content citing a ChatGPT search-trigger rate should say "estimated" and name the estimator, not present it as OpenAI's own number.
- **Does schema markup help?** Ahrefs' matched-control study (§3) found no significant causal lift from adding JSON-LD, despite a strong raw correlation between schema presence and citation. This directly contradicts dozens of vendor-blog claims that schema is a top citation driver. The honest position: schema correlates with citation because it correlates with overall site investment, not because engines specifically reward the markup.
- **Do backlinks still matter for AI citation, and how much?** Ahrefs found backlink count correlates weakly with AI visibility across all three engines it tested. Search Engine Journal/Victorious found referring domains at a moderate 0.49 correlation. Meanwhile, general SEO commentary in 2026 continues to assert backlinks are "still a core ranking signal" without reconciling that against the GEO-specific correlation data. These are not necessarily contradictory (backlinks may matter for getting indexed/crawled/ranked in the first place, which is upstream of being citable) but no study in this pass isolated that causal chain. Treat "backlinks for GEO" as unresolved.
- **Freshness signals disagree across studies.** Seer Interactive found Perplexity was the *least* freshness-weighted of the three engines it tested (65% of citations updated within a year, vs. 73-78% for ChatGPT/Gemini). Other aggregator claims describe Perplexity as the most real-time/freshness-sensitive engine. Both can't be the headline claim simultaneously; the Seer study is the one with disclosed methodology, so it should be preferred, but the discrepancy itself is worth naming rather than picking whichever number is more flattering to a "publish more often" pitch.
- **AI visibility scoring tools disagree with each other on the same brand.** One 2026 industry piece cites citation-rate variance up to "615x" across engines for the same brand/prompt set (Grok 27.01% vs. Claude effectively 0%), and separately notes that AI model updates, session/login state, and even minor prompt wording shifts change results — meaning any single "AI visibility score" is a snapshot of one methodology's choices, not a stable ground truth. Three independent 2026 benchmarks reportedly converge on a median score of "49/100" across 3,000+ brands, but the underlying scoring definitions are not identical between them, so that convergence may be coincidental rather than confirmatory. BrandGEO's own skepticism about undisclosed methodology (per its editorial voice) applies directly here — this is exactly the category BrandGEO's own scoring should be careful not to fall into.
- **"Documented before/after case studies of a real company's citation rate improving from a specific content change"** are essentially all vendor marketing in this pass — none disclosed a sample size, control group, or a citation-tracking methodology independent of the vendor's own paid tool. This is a real gap: there is currently no independently audited, causal case study showing "brand X did Y and citation rate moved by Z%, verified by a third party." Worth naming as a gap rather than filling it with a weak example.
- **Zero-click and "start research in AI not Google" percentages vary widely by source and several could not be traced to a verifiable primary study in this pass** (the 67.1%/2026 zero-click figure, the "37% of consumers start with AI" figure, the Semrush "Mobile Search Behavior Study" mobile/desktop split). SparkToro's 68.01% (disclosed Similarweb-panel methodology) is the most defensible zero-click figure found; the others should be re-verified against a primary source before being used as a headline stat.
