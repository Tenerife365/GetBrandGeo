## 2. Adjacent/legacy SEO players entering AI-visibility

Every major legacy SEO platform checked in this pass has added an AI-citation-tracking module in the last 12–18 months. The pattern is consistent: bolt an AI-visibility module onto the existing rank-tracking product, price it as an add-on or bundle it free during a land-grab phase, and lean on the existing keyword/query database as the foundation rather than building fresh prompt infrastructure from scratch.

- **Semrush** — "AI Visibility Toolkit," available inside Semrush One or as a standalone add-on. Three sub-features: Prompt Tracking (prompt-level visibility in ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Gemini), Brand Performance (share of voice and sentiment), and Visibility Overview (full AI-presence snapshot). Built on Semrush's own "prompt database of 289M+ AI queries," refreshed daily on a rolling basis, covering Google AI Overviews, AI Mode, Gemini, and ChatGPT. Source: [Semrush AI Visibility Features](https://www.semrush.com/kb/1626-ai-visibility-features), [Getting Started guide](https://www.semrush.com/kb/1496-getting-started-with-ai-visibility-toolkit).

- **Ahrefs** — "Brand Radar," the most transparently documented methodology found in this entire research pass (see §3 detail below). Free across all Ahrefs subscription tiers during its beta, including the entry-level $129/month Lite plan, with an explicit statement that it will "eventually become a paid add-on layered on top of Ahrefs plans that already price by seat and cap projects." Expanded in early 2026 to also track YouTube, TikTok, and Reddit visibility. Source: [Brand Radar methodology](https://ahrefs.com/blog/brand-radar-methodology/) (Feb 26, 2026); [YouTube/Reddit expansion, BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260106995102/en/Ahrefs-Adds-YouTube-and-Reddit-Tracking-to-Brand-Radar) (Jan 6, 2026).

- **BrightEdge** — "Generative Parser" (BGP) plus "AI Catalyst" and, launched March 2026, "AI Hyper Cube," described as the first purpose-built capability tracking how brands appear across AI-driven search *and agent* interactions (not just chat answers). Also added "AI Agent Insights" tracking how AI crawlers/agents interact with a brand's own site. Source: [BrightEdge press release](https://www.brightedge.com/news/press-releases/brightedge-generative-parse-identifies-and-detects-patterns-new-ai-search); [AI Catalyst blog post](https://www.brightedge.com/blog/introducing-brightedge-ai-catalyst-innovating-give-you-complete-search-and-ai-visibility).

- **Conductor** — repositioned itself from an enterprise SEO platform into what it now calls an "AI search visibility and answer engine optimization (AEO) operating layer." Launched the next generation of its AI Search Performance product April 1, 2026, plus "AgentStack," an enterprise suite of native LLM apps and turnkey agents. Published a "2026 AEO/GEO Benchmarks Report" claiming analysis of 3.3 billion sessions across more than 13,000 enterprise domains — this research pass could not retrieve the underlying data tables behind that headline claim (the source page timed out and returned a 403 on retry), so treat the 3.3B/13,000 figures as Conductor's self-reported top-line only, not independently verified detail. Source: [CMSWire coverage](https://www.cmswire.com/digital-experience/conductor-launches-agentstack-for-aeo/); [Conductor.com](https://www.conductor.com/); [BusinessWire headline](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251113364791/en/Conductor-Unveils-2026-AEO-GEO-Benchmarks-Report-How-AI-Shapes-Brand-Visibility-in-a-Zero-Click-World) (Nov 13, 2025).

- **seoClarity** — "Clarity ArcAI" tracks brand/page visibility inside AI Overviews and other AI answers, plus AI-bot access monitoring, sentiment, and hallucination detection. Also publishes an "AI Search Trend Report" drawing on data across its client base. Source: [seoClarity AI search visibility page](https://www.seoclarity.net/ai-seo/ai-search-engine-visibility); [AI Search Trend Report](https://www.seoclarity.net/research/ai-search-trend-report).

- **Search Atlas** — an "LLM Visibility" module tracking ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, SearchGPT plus sentiment and competitor benchmarks; full model coverage opens on its Pro tier. Also sells "OTTO," an AI agent that autonomously implements schema, meta tags, and internal-link changes via a site pixel with no developer required, pending manual approval per change. Source: [OTTO SEO product page](https://searchatlas.com/otto-seo/); [Search Atlas features page](https://searchatlas.com/features/).

- **Nightwatch** — started life as a traditional multi-engine SERP rank tracker (Google, Bing, Yahoo, DuckDuckGo) and added LLM tracking in the 2024–2025 window. Its stated differentiator versus most AI-tracking tools: it also tracks the real-time web searches AI models perform while generating an answer, not just the final text, giving visibility into the source pipeline (query → sources pulled → final answer) rather than only the endpoint. Entry pricing $32/month with a 14-day free trial. One review notes "specific user reviews about this feature are scarce," suggesting the AI module is newer and less battle-tested than the core rank tracker. Source: [Nightwatch blog, best AI search monitoring tools](https://nightwatch.io/blog/best-ai-search-monitoring-tools/); [Rankability review](https://www.rankability.com/blog/nightwatch-llm-tracking/) (2026).

- **SE Ranking** — split into two products: "AI Results Tracker," an add-on inside the core SE Ranking platform focused specifically on Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, and "SE Visible," a fully separate dedicated AI-visibility product covering the broader engine set. The distinction between the two is easy to miss when comparison-shopping. Source: [SE Ranking AI Mode Tracker](https://seranking.com/ai-mode-tracker.html); [SE Visible](https://visible.seranking.com/).

- **Advanced Web Ranking (AWR)** — AI Overview tracking is enabled by default across all plans on its existing "Google Universal" search engine option, with a purpose-built "Google Search + AIO" engine offered for more consistent scraping where source URLs would otherwise be missing or incomplete. Coverage spans 170+ countries down to city/zip level, inherited from its existing rank-tracking infrastructure. Also offers a free public AI Overviews tracking tool. Source: [AWR AI Overviews tracking guide](https://www.advancedwebranking.com/help/serp-features-tracking-in-awr-ai-overviews-sge); [free tool](https://www.advancedwebranking.com/free-seo-tools/google-ai-overview).

**Pattern worth naming in content:** none of the eight legacy tools above built a dedicated, from-scratch prompt-and-response infrastructure the way the GEO-native startups (Profound, Peec AI, Scrunch, etc.) did. They are extending an existing keyword database or SERP-scraping pipeline to also capture AI answers. Ahrefs is the one clear exception in terms of *disclosed methodology* (see §3), even though its underlying approach — expanding from real keyword/PAA data rather than hand-written prompts — is itself a legacy-tool adaptation, not a new measurement paradigm.

- A different report cited in the same search pass: the market grows from **$1.48 billion in 2026 to $17.02 billion by 2034**, a 45.5% CAGR — a base-year figure 36% higher than Dimension's for the same year.
- A third figure: the market was **$848 million in 2025**, projected to reach **~$19.8 billion by 2034** at a 50.5% CAGR from 2026–2034.
- US-specific: Dimension Market Research puts the **US GEO market at $365.4 million in 2026**, 42.9% CAGR. Source: [Dimension Market Research, US report](https://dimensionmarketresearch.com/report/us-generative-engine-optimization-market/).

These three global 2026 estimates ($1.09B, $1.48B, and an implied ~$900M–$1B from the $848M-2025-base trajectory) diverge by up to 36% for the *same year*, from market-research firms that don't disclose comparable methodology in the search results available. None of them appear to be Gartner or Forrester primary research — they read as independent market-research-report vendors (the kind that sell the full PDF behind a paywall), and this pass could not verify their underlying methodology. ## 4. What buyers actually ask and object to

**1. Consistency: the underlying AI answers themselves are not stable, which undercuts any single "visibility score."**
SparkToro ran a study of 600 volunteers submitting 2,961 individual queries across 12 prompts on three major AI platforms (chef's knives, headphones, cancer care hospitals, digital marketing consultants, among the categories tested). Headline finding: **there is less than a 1-in-100 chance that the same AI platform, asked the same question twice, returns the same list of brand recommendations**, and roughly a 1-in-1,000 chance the list comes back in the same *order*. Claude was found "slightly more likely" to repeat a list than ChatGPT or Google's AI, but still rarely repeated the exact order. The direct implication for any GEO tool: "ranking position" as a metric doesn't hold up the way a Google SERP rank does, because the thing being measured isn't stable between two consecutive runs. Source: [SparkToro research post](https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-ais-are-highly-inconsistent-when-recommending-brands-or-products-marketers-should-take-care-when-tracking-ai-visibility/); [Search Engine Journal coverage](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-recommendations-change-with-nearly-every-query-sparktoro/566242/).

**2. Accuracy/methodology skepticism is now a mainstream talking point, not a fringe complaint.**
A Brainlabs piece (April 20, 2026) argues plainly that AI-visibility measurement is inherently unreliable because the medium itself is inconsistent — not a flaw specific to any one vendor. It identifies four estimation approaches used across the industry (panel-based, clickstream inference, keyword-to-prompt modeling, API sampling), each with real weaknesses, and states the search-volume-to-AI-prompt-volume conversion factor most tools rely on is "largely assumed." Its core line: **"The concept of a 'ranking,' the foundational unit of traditional SEO reporting, simply doesn't translate to AI search."** Its recommendation to marketers is to prioritize "intelligence over accounting" — trend direction and competitive benchmarking rather than a precise score. Source: [Brainlabs](https://www.brainlabsdigital.com/ai-visibility-data-accuracy/) (Apr 20, 2026).

**3. "Is this just SEO with new branding?"** This framing shows up repeatedly across the search results for this pass (search-result summaries referenced marketers viewing GEO/AEO as "shallow rebrands of SEO dressed up with new jargon"), but this pass could not pin that phrasing to one specific, citable Reddit thread or LinkedIn post — it surfaced as a synthesized characterization across several secondary sources rather than a single quotable primary source. Worth noting as a real and recurring objection in the category, but flagged honestly here as not independently sourced to one URL. What *is* directly sourced: a founder explicitly countering the framing — SearchYVR's founder stated on launching a GEO service that the company is "not going to upsell clients on tactics we've already been doing for years" and that "this isn't about buzzwords, it's about adapting strategy to how people actually search today," which itself is evidence the objection is common enough that vendors pre-empt it in their own launch messaging. Source: [Barchart/SearchYVR](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/33252379/vancouvers-searchyvr-launches-geo-focused-ai-ready-seo-services-to-help-businesses-get-found-in-ai-generated-answers).

- Otterly.AI's own pricing page shows its 4 "core" engines (ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity, Copilot) included at every tier, but **Google AI Mode, Gemini, and Claude are all paid add-ons at every tier**, ranging from $9/month up to $439/month depending on plan level. A buyer reading only the $189/month "Standard" headline price would not see this without opening the full pricing table. Source: [otterly.ai/pricing](https://otterly.ai/pricing), fetched directly.
- A Peec AI G2 review complaint: the advertised $95/month entry price only covers three platforms in that reviewer's experience, with each additional LLM sold as a separate ~$35/month add-on — this conflicts with what Peec AI's own pricing page shows (6 models listed at every tier including Starter), which is itself worth flagging as a discrepancy between what a vendor's pricing page states and what at least one paying customer reports actually happening at checkout/usage. Source: [Peec AI G2 reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/peec-ai/reviews).
- General credit-based pricing complaints: engines are not billed equally under credit systems — one industry roundup states ChatGPT/Gemini/Perplexity/AI Mode checks run 0.25 credits each while **Claude can cost up to 8x the base per-check rate** on some platforms, that overage charges on usage-based plans commonly add 20–30% to the base price, and that some enterprise tools charge **$1,000–$5,000 in separate onboarding fees**. Source: [trysight.ai pricing guide](https://www.trysight.ai/blog/geo-optimization-software-purchase).

**5. What buyers converge on asking before signing, synthesized across several vendor-evaluation-checklist pages (Trakkr, AEO Vision, xseek, VisibilityStack):**
- Which models are *natively* supported vs. scraped/inferred, since legacy tools "claim" AI tracking but some only cover Google AI Overviews.
- Whether the tool identifies the exact citations/sources an answer drew on, not just whether the brand was mentioned — i.e. can it tell you if the model cited your own site, a review platform, a news outlet, or a forum.
- Whether the vendor discloses its collection method (API vs. scraping vs. hybrid) and whether it's ToS-compliant with each AI platform — API output is cleaner but can miss inline links/UI-only citation formatting that scraping captures, which is why some vendors run both.
- Whether it integrates with the buyer's existing CMS/analytics stack.
- A recurring, pointed line across these checklists: **"A demo from a vendor is not a trial — a demo shows you what the vendor wants you to see in the best possible light."** Buyers are advised to test with their own prompts side by side across shortlisted tools before committing. Source: [Trakkr GEO/AEO vendor checklist](https://trakkr.ai/guides/geo-aeo-vendor-evaluation-checklist); [AEO Vision, 10 questions before you buy](https://aeovision.ai/articles/geo-tool-evaluation-checklist-10-questions-before-you-buy/).

## 5. Public case studies and data points — methodology transparency compared

**Disclosed, credible methodology:**

- **Yext**, published Oct 9, 2025 (covered again in a Jan 2026 Yext blog post): analyzed **6.8 million AI citations** collected from **1.6 million queries per model** across ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity, between July 1 and August 31, 2025, structured across 4 intent quadrants and 4 industries (retail, financial services, healthcare, food service). Finding: **86% of AI citations come from sources brands already control** — websites (44%, 2.9M citations) and business listings (42%, 2.9M citations) dominate, with reviews/social contributing only 8% (545K citations). This directly cuts against a common assumption that earned media/forums like Reddit drive most AI citations. Sample size, date range, and industry breakdown are all disclosed, which is why this is treated as credible here rather than a vague claim. Source: [Yext research release, BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251009106549/en/Yext-Research-86-of-AI-Citations-Come-from-Brand-Managed-Sources-Clarifying-How-Marketers-Can-Compete-in-the-AI-Search-Era) (Oct 9, 2025); [Yext 2026 blog recap](https://www.yext.com/blog/2026/01/what-brands-need-to-know-about-ai-search-2026).

- **Ahrefs Brand Radar methodology** (see §2 above for feature detail): explicitly discloses that it draws from "People Also Ask" data plus a "semantic fanout" query-expansion system, built on a base of **28.7 billion keywords filtered from 110 billion discovered**, and reports modeled monthly query volume per platform (ChatGPT 14.5M, Perplexity 14.6M, Gemini 14.6M, Copilot 13.3M, Google AI Overviews 282M, AI Mode 24M, Grok 13M — totaling roughly 372M monthly). It also proactively discloses its own limitations: coverage is "strongest in English," it prioritizes high-demand queries over an "effectively infinite" long tail, and it deliberately **retains hallucinated or malformed links** in its data because they "reflect real model output" rather than cleaning them out to look tidier. This level of disclosed limitation is unusual in the category and is the closest thing to a transparency benchmark found in this pass. Source: [Ahrefs Brand Radar methodology](https://ahrefs.com/blog/brand-radar-methodology/) (Feb 26, 2026).

**Opaque, vendor/agency-published case studies (worth using as a contrast, not as reliable data):**

- Launchmind blog: claims a mid-market B2B SaaS client **"increased AI citations by 340% in 90 days"** via a GEO program (rebuilding 12 pages into "answer hubs," adding source-backed stats, strengthening internal linking, monitoring via ChatGPT/Bing and Google AI Overviews). No client name, no disclosed baseline measurement tool or method, no independent verification. Source: [Launchmind](https://launchmind.io/en/blog/geo-case-study-how-a-saas-company-increased-ai-citations-by-340-real-results-mk0vglmn/).
- A separate agency case study claims **AI visibility rose from 6.8% to 46.7% in 7 days** — again, no client name and no disclosed measurement methodology behind either the 6.8% baseline or the 46.7% result. Source: [Jaeckert-O'Daniel](https://www.jaeckert-odaniel.com/en/geo-case-study-46-7-ai-visibility-in-7-days/).

These two are presented here specifically as an example of the category's "vague, unfalsifiable growth claim" pattern that BrandGEO's own content style should contrast itself against — the numbers are large, round, and impossible to check because neither the client identity nor the measurement method is disclosed, which is exactly the opposite of what Yext and Ahrefs did above.
