## 2a. Personal profile

**Driver:** Contrarian, first-person open. **265 words.**

> Two weeks ago I removed an AI engine from our own product.
>
> Meta AI had been in BrandGEO since the start. It was on the pricing page. It
> was in the logo strip. And it was quietly making our measurement worse, because
> the models we could reach through that path answered from training data only.
> No web search.
>
> That sounds like a technicality. It is not. An engine with no retrieval tells
> you what was true before its training cutoff. A business that launched this
> year is invisible to it. A competitor that closed two years ago might still be
> ranked third. The output looks identical to an engine that actually went and
> looked, which is what makes it dangerous rather than merely useless.
>
> So we cut it. Our engine count went down, on a pricing page, in a category
> where everyone competes on that number.
>
> Today we added two more engines, and the same test decided both. Grok on its
> own answers from memory. With the web plugin enabled it does not. That
> configuration is the entire reason it is in the product. Google AI Overviews
> reads the live results page directly, so it never had a memory problem to solve.
>
> DeepSeek did not make it in, for the same reason Meta AI went out.
>
> If you are evaluating any tool in this space, the question is not how many
> engines they list. It is how many had search switched on when they ran your
> report. Most will not have published enough for you to check. Ask anyway.

**Comment to post under it, 20 minutes after:**
> The remove-an-engine story is the one worth asking any vendor for. Everyone has
> an add-an-engine story.

## 2b. Company page

**Driver:** Contrarian, product-forward. **255 words.**

> Two engines went live in BrandGEO today, on Growth PRO and above. That takes us
> to seven.
>
> Grok is the only one of the seven that reads X as well as the open web. Every
> other engine sees your site, your listings, publisher coverage and review sites.
> Grok also sees the conversation.
>
> Google AI Overviews is the one most tools are missing, and the distinction
> matters. AI Mode is a tab a searcher switches to on purpose. AI Overviews is the
> summary Google puts above the links on an ordinary results page, which nobody
> opts into and far more people see. They often disagree. Reporting one of them as
> "Google" hides the surface most of your customers are actually looking at.
>
> One thing did not change: the entry requirement. All seven run with live web
> search enabled. That is why the list is seven rather than twelve.
>
> We retired Meta AI on 16 July because it answered from training data only. We
> have kept DeepSeek out for the same reason. Copilot is absent because Microsoft
> ships no public API for it, and we would rather say we do not cover it than
> measure something adjacent and use the name.
>
> The full ladder: ChatGPT on Free. ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude on Essentials.
> Perplexity and Google AI Mode from Growth. Grok and Google AI Overviews from
> Growth PRO. Weekly refresh on every paid plan.
