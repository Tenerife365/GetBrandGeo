## This week's focus: BG-020, a cross-city consensus follow-up

**Topic:** BG-016 ("Cross-Engine Consensus: When AI Engines Agree, and When
They Don't," published 2026-07-14) ran its consensus methodology across the
original 7-city dataset and 20 buyer categories, finding 10 of 20 categories
reached a clear cross-engine leader. Since then the city dataset has grown to
27 cities (20 more US cities were added in a batch between 2026-07-24 and
2026-07-25, confirmed both live on the site and in Supabase, `clients.id` 28
through 47, one real collection day each). BG-020 re-runs BG-016's exact
question, "when do AI engines agree on a leader, and when don't they," at
roughly four times the sample size, and reports whether the original 10-of-20
split held up or moved.

**Why this and not something else:** it is next in the numbered series (BG-019
published 2026-07-24, three days ago, "rank-detection methodology"). It is a
direct, non-duplicate sequel to BG-016, not a new topic invented from nothing.
It needs zero new AI-engine collection since all 27 cities' data already
exists. And it stays inside what this blog actually is, original research on
AI Visibility measurement, rather than drifting into internal engineering
process (this run considered and rejected two engineering-audit angles, the
cPanel deploy pipeline audit and the Growth PRO billing-plan drift fix, both
real and well-documented in `docs/qa/`, but neither is about AI visibility
measurement, they are product/billing engineering, a genre mismatch with
everything BG-001 through BG-019 have covered).

**Suggested angles for Wednesday to choose between (not a full outline, and
the exact numbers below need verification before publishing, see the warning
under angle 2):**

1. **The safer angle.** Re-run BG-016's own methodology at 4x scale and report
   the real before/after: did the 10-of-20-category consensus rate go up,
   down, or hold, once 20 more cities' worth of data is included? This reuses
   an already-published, already-defended methodology rather than asserting a
   new one, so it carries less risk of overclaiming.
2. **A narrower, more citable angle, needs verification first.** Several of
   the new city pages' own live teaser copy independently claims a property
   management consensus pattern (examples pulled directly from the live site
   this run: Chicago "one property manager hit 5 of 5 engines, unanimous,"
   San Diego "full unanimous consensus on property management," San Antonio
   "property management's perfect streak hits 20 cities") and a separate
   immigration-law convergence pattern (Miami, Phoenix). **Before writing this
   angle, verify the actual tally against the raw `docs/*-research-findings.md`
   files, not the shorter web teaser lines.** A spot check this run did on a
   handful of the raw findings docs shows per-engine top picks for property
   management that differ substantially city to city (different companies
   ranked #1 by ChatGPT vs. Claude vs. Gemini in several cities checked), so
   the "unanimous" claim needs to be confirmed as being about within-city
   cross-engine agreement on that city's own #1 pick, not misread as the same
   company winning across cities. Don't publish an exact count without
   re-deriving it from the 21 relevant `*-research-findings.md` files directly.

Either angle is legitimate, verifiable content, not manufactured urgency.
Angle 1 is the lower-risk pick if there is any doubt about verification time
before Wednesday.
