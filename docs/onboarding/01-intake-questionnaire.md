# Intake questionnaire

Client: ______________________  Date: ____________  Run by: ____________

One hour. Sections A, C and D are the ones that decide whether the engagement
produces anything useful. If time runs out, finish those and send the rest as
a short follow up.

Every question carries a "why", because a client who understands why a question
is being asked gives a better answer than a client filling in a form.

**Blocking questions** are marked. Work cannot start without them:
A1, A2, A3, C1, D1.

## Section A. The brand as an engine sees it

**A1. What is the exact name you want to be found by? BLOCKING**
Why: every measurement keys on that string. The difference between the right
string and a near miss is the difference between a real score and a zero that
means nothing.

**A2. Every other name you are called. BLOCKING**
Abbreviations, the legal name, a former name, the name in another language,
the misspellings you see in your own inbox.
Why: an answer that says "IBSB" and an answer that says "International
British School of Bucharest" are the same result. Both have to count, or the
score is wrong in your favour and then wrong against you.

**A3. Your website, and any other domain a buyer might land on. BLOCKING**
Why: the domain is the second way a mention is detected, and a second domain
that ranks instead of the main one changes what we recommend.

**A4. In one sentence, what category would a stranger put you in?**
Why: we compare this to the category the engines actually put you in. When
those two disagree, that gap is usually the finding.

## Section B. Markets and languages

**B1. Which countries do you sell in? Rank them.**
Why: engines answer the same question differently by country. A market is also
the unit the plan is sized in, so this decides the quote.

**B2. Any city or region that matters more than the country as a whole?**

**B3. Which languages should the questions be asked in?**
Why: a brand can be present in English and absent in its own local language,
and that has been measured before.

## Section C. The questions a buyer actually asks

This is the most valuable section in the document. Take your time in it.

**C1. Write down the last five questions a real prospect asked you before they
bought. In their words, not yours. BLOCKING**
Why: generic category questions return a near constant result for a whole
segment, so they tell you about the segment and not about you. Your own buyers'
questions are what make the measurement about your company.

**C2. What does somebody type when they do not know you exist yet?**
Why: that is the question where being absent costs money.

**C3. Of all of them, which single question would be worth the most if an
engine answered it with your name?**
Why: that question goes first in the report, and the work is ordered by it.

**C4. Is there a question you are asked that you would rather an engine did not
answer for you?**

## Section D. Who you lose to

**D1. Name up to eight competitors, in the order you lose to them. BLOCKING**
Why: the sharpest line in any report is not "you are missing". It is "the
engine named somebody, and it was them".

**D2. Is anyone named in your category who should not be?**
Why: an engine naming a company that no longer trades, or that does not serve
the market, is a fact worth having and often a fast win.

**D3. Where do buyers compare you? Review sites, directories, forums, groups,
associations.**
Why: those are the pages engines read when they answer. Being absent there is
usually the reason for being absent in the answer.

---

## Section E. What is already being spent on being found

**E1. Who runs search and content today? In house, an agency, nobody?**

**E2. How often does something new get published, and who writes it?**

**E3. Are you buying paid search, and roughly how much a month?**

**E4. Do you have a public pricing page, a comparison page, an FAQ?**

Why this section exists: a measured zero shown to somebody who has never
thought about search is a curiosity. Shown to somebody already paying to be
found, it is a hole in something they are already buying. It also tells you
whether there is anyone on their side who can act on the report.

---

## Section F. Access, ownership and speed

**F1. Who can publish a change to the website this week, without a committee?**
Name the person.
Why: half of what a GEO report recommends is a page that has to be written and
published. If nobody can publish, the report is reading material.

**F2. What is the site built on?**

**F3. Can we have read access to analytics and Search Console?**

**F4. Is there anything about this engagement we may not say publicly?**

---

## Section G. Decision and money

**G1. Who signs, and above what amount does it need somebody else?**

**G2. Is there a budget cycle or a date this has to land before?**

**G3. Finish this sentence: this worked if, by [date], we ________.**
Why: this is the only sentence in the intake you will be measured against.
Write it in their words and read it back to them before moving on.
